## THE SHARED SPINE (all 3 do every one of these)

1. **Zero preamble.** No "hey guys," no "welcome back," no channel intro. The FIRST spoken
   word is the hook. Every time.
2. **Face delayed ~8-10 seconds.** All three open on b-roll or a motion graphic with VO of
   the payoff. Nate's face first appears at 0:10 / (layered, but the *promise* is spoken over
   graphics) / 0:08. The result lands before any "ceremony."
3. **Lead with a specific, named, quantified claim.** "3x more money in 30 days" / "complete
   beginner to AI native" / "Right now a new group of millionaires is being created... a
   company called Vulcan." Concrete + present-tense = urgent.
4. **The frame never goes static.** A visual change (cut, graphic build, chip, b-roll, push-in)
   lands every ~1.5-2.5s in the first 30 seconds. Bare talking head never runs more than ~5-7s.
5. **Motion graphics synced word-for-word to the VO.** Cards, chips, and HUD counters animate
   in exactly as he says the phrase they illustrate. This is the retention engine.
6. **An explicit "In this video" roadmap** builds around 0:26-0:33 in all three - a numbered
   agenda card (1... 2...) that tells the viewer the payoff is coming.
7. **One open loop planted early and NOT closed.** The "4 upgrades," the 30-topic list, the
   Vulcan story - each is teased in the first 30s and deliberately deferred. Vulcan is even
   re-opened at 1:58 ("remember, the company from the intro") with no payoff yet.
8. **Claims proven with real on-screen receipts + animated highlight.** Income chart, Reuters
   / SaaStr / blog screenshots with a yellow-highlighter animation drawing the eye to the exact
   number. Never "trust me" - always "look."
9. **Animate the argument, don't narrate it.** Funding options slide in as chips and get
   crossed out in red; "47x growth" becomes a rising line chart + a rolling money counter; the
   problem is shown as a live screen-record (sycophantic Claude, lying-about-the-feature Claude).
10. **Credibility by sameness first, resume second.** "I do NOT have a technical background,
    BUT I've built [X]." The "but" pivot converts a beginner's guard into trust. Name delayed.
11. **CTA folded into the story, then snap back.** The Skool plug rides the momentum ("before
    upgrade 1, grab these free") and cuts straight back - no dead-air "smash like" detour.

## THE PACING NUANCE (the part most people get wrong)

There are TWO schools here, and they prove the real rule:

- **Videos 1 & 3 = rapid cutting.** ~43-50 cuts in 120s, ~2.5s average shot length, ~6-8 visual
  changes per 10s in the first 30s.
- **Video 2 = long holds.** One continuous 34-second talking-head take with ZERO jump cuts,
  ~30s average segments. Yet it retains just as well.

**The rule is NOT "cut fast." The rule is "the frame must never stop changing."** Video 2
survives long takes because the graphics build element-by-element, the webcam stays as a PIP on
every screen-share, and the speech has zero filler. You can either cut every 2.5s OR keep the
frame visually mutating - but a static, unchanging talking-head shot is what kills retention.
The enemy is stillness, not long takes.

## THE VERBAL HOOK FORMULA (common structure, first ~35s)

1. **0-5s - Big specific claim.** Present-tense, named or quantified. Spoken over a graphic/b-roll.
2. **5-12s - Contradiction / curiosity gap.** "Looks like 100 engineers, but it isn't." "Problems
   you never notice." A "wait, what?" beat.
3. **12-20s - Promise + named deliverable.** "Four upgrades." "By the end you'll build anything."
   Countable and concrete, synced to a graphic that builds it.
4. **20-27s - Broaden relevance / kill objections.** "Doesn't matter if you build an app or run an
   agency." "Zero coding background." Nobody self-selects out.
5. **27-33s - Explicit roadmap + open loop.** "In this video: 1... 2..." card, one story left open.
6. **~0:26-0:45 - Credibility: sameness then resume.** Relatable first, proof second, name last.

## TYLER'S FIRST-2-MINUTES REBUILD

Your likely failure mode (confirm against your own retention graph): you open on your face + a
warm-up sentence, the frame sits static on the talking head for 15-30s, there are no synced
graphics, no receipts, no roadmap card, and no open loop. That is the 1:10 cliff.

The rebuild, beat by beat (map to any of your Claude/automation videos):

| Time | Do this | Production |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:08 | **Cold open on the result, over b-roll/graphic. Face hidden.** Speak the single most specific outcome ("I connected Claude to every app I own and it ran my whole morning before I woke up"). | Screen-record of the payoff + VO. HyperFrames motion-graphic. NO face yet. |
| 0:08-0:12 | **Face reveal on a contradiction / curiosity gap.** "And I never touched a line of auth code." | Cut to talking head, push-in. |
| 0:12-0:20 | **Name the deliverable, countable.** "Here are the 3 pieces that make it work." | HyperFrames cards build 1-2-3 synced to VO. |
| 0:20-0:27 | **Broaden + kill objection.** "Doesn't matter if you're a developer or have never opened a terminal." | Chips: "Developer" / "Never coded". |
| 0:27-0:33 | **Roadmap card + open loop.** "In this video: 1) the setup, 2) the one mistake everyone makes." Leave #2 unexplained. | "In this video" agenda graphic. |
| 0:33-2:00 | **Never let your face sit bare >5-7s.** Every claim gets a synced visual within ~1-2s. Prove every number with a real screenshot + highlight. Show the problem live, don't describe it. | Kinetic captions on key lines, receipts, screen-record demos, chips. |

### The non-negotiable production checklist (this is where HyperFrames earns its keep)
- [ ] Kill the intro. First sentence = the payoff.
- [ ] Delay your face to ~0:08, over b-roll/screen-record of the result.
- [ ] Kinetic captions building word-by-word on your 2-3 most important lines (color-code the key contrast, e.g. teal vs red).
- [ ] A new on-screen visual (card, chip, screenshot, cut) at least every 5 seconds - never a bare static shot.
- [ ] One "In this video: 1... 2..." roadmap card by 0:30.
- [ ] One open loop planted in the first 30s, closed near the END of the video.
- [ ] Every number shown as a real on-screen source with an animated highlight - never just spoken.
- [ ] Show the problem as a live screen-record, don't describe it.
- [ ] On any screen-share, keep your webcam as a PIP so your face never disappears.
- [ ] CTA folded into the narrative, then snap straight back to the content.

### The single highest-leverage change
If you do ONE thing: **delete the intro and open on the result over b-roll with your face hidden
for 8 seconds.** All three videos do it, and it directly attacks the first-10-second drop that is
almost certainly where your 1:10 is being set.
