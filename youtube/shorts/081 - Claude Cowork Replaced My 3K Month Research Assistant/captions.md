## LinkedIn
Claude Cowork replaced my research assistant. I was paying her $3,000 a month.

Here is what she used to do for me every week. Competitor research — 5 to 10 hours. Industry news summaries — 3 hours. Pulling data from analyst reports and formatting into executive briefs — 5 hours. Finding guest contacts and writing their backgrounds for podcast booking — 2 hours. Somewhere between 15 and 20 hours a week of focused knowledge work.

All of that is now a Cowork task. I describe what I need in plain English. Cowork runs the research across the web, cross-references sources, and writes a summary document directly to my Drive. It does it while I am in meetings, at dinner, asleep. The output lands when I need it.

Quality is the same. Speed is faster. Cost is zero beyond my Anthropic subscription.

My assistant was great. This is not a quality critique. It is an economics reality. The work of an entry-level research associate has been fully commoditized by AI agents with connectors, and the first movers on this shift capture the cost advantage.

If you are running a consulting practice, content business, or growth team and still paying humans for knowledge retrieval and synthesis, the math has changed.
