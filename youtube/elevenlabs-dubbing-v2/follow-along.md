## Step by step (Automatic Dubbing)

**1. Open Dubbing.** From the ElevenLabs dashboard, go to Dubbing.

**2. Add your video.** Upload your own file, or paste a link to a video you own. Confirm it is your footage.

**3. Pick your target language(s).** This is where the 90+ languages live. Start with one (Spanish is a strong first test).

**4. Check Cloning strength (Advanced).** Defaults to 7. Higher = the dub sounds more like your original voice. Lower = more natural in the target language, a little less like you. Leave it at the default for your first run, then experiment.

**5. Watch the credit cost.** Automatic Dubbing is priced per minute, so longer videos cost more. The cost shows before you generate. Failed jobs are not charged.

**6. Generate.** It transcribes your audio, detects speakers, translates, clones your voice, and lines the new audio up with your original timing.

**7. Play it back.** Compare the original to the dub. Listen for the tone, emphasis, and timing being preserved, that is the whole point.

## The step most people skip: review
Some languages come out cleaner than others. Before you publish:
- Listen through the entire dub, not just the first line.
- Watch for mangled names, off pacing, or a phrase that lands wrong.
- If a language is rough: nudge the cloning strength and re-run, fix it in Dubbing Studio (transcript edit / regenerate a clip), or do not ship that language yet.
- **Honest note:** re-running a language is a new generation = more credits. It is not free. Review is what keeps you from wasting credits and from publishing something off.

## Scale it: one video, many languages
Once one language looks good, add more off the same source video. One recording can become Spanish, Japanese, Hindi, Portuguese, and dozens more, all in your voice. Think about your whole back catalog: the content is already made, dubbing just unlocks the audiences that could not understand it before.
