## POST 1 - The story

Everyone told me to switch to GPT Image 2.

Every headline said the same thing. GPT Image 2 finally dethroned Nano Banana. Time to move on.

I generate images with AI every week, so this isn't a small switch for me. It's the front of everything I put out.

So before I changed anything, I tested it.

Not on art prompts. Not on architecture renders or grids of the alphabet, which is what most of the comparisons run.

I put three models through the work I actually repeat. Nano Banana Pro, GPT Image 2, and the cheap one nobody talks about, Seedream 4.5.

Six rounds. Thumbnails out of the box. A realistic scene. Text sitting on an image. Consistency across a whole set. Pricing. And one hard infographic to try and break them.

Here's what came out of it.

GPT Image 2 genuinely wins on text. Headlines, quote graphics, a dense infographic that has to get its facts right. If that's your job, use it. I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

For everything else I make, Nano Banana Pro is still the one. Better results with less prompting, faces that stay the same person, one look held across a whole set.

The surprise was Seedream 4.5. Not the best at anything. Good enough at most of it for a fraction of the price.

So the honest answer isn't a winner. It's that I open a different model depending on what I'm making that day.

And I only know which one because I tested them on my own work instead of trusting the headline.

If you had to pick one image model tomorrow, which would you reach for?

## POST 2 - The tactic (no link)

I stopped trusting AI model comparisons.

Not because they're dishonest. Because they're testing something that isn't my job.

Open any of them and you get the same prompts. A cyberpunk street. An architecture render. A grid of the alphabet in twelve fonts. Someone scores it, posts a ranking, and everyone switches tools.

I have never once needed a cyberpunk street.

So a while back I started doing something different, and it takes about an afternoon.

First I write down the five things I actually make with the tool. For me that's an image I'll publish, a face that has to look like me, text sitting on an image, a set of images that need to match each other, and something fast and cheap when I need a lot.

Then I run every model through all five with the exact same brief. Same words, no extra prompting for the one I already like.

Then I lay the outputs next to each other and pick per job, not overall.

That last part is the one people skip. You don't need a winner. You need to know which tool to open on a Tuesday.

When I did it, the ranking came out different from every headline I'd read. One model was clearly better at text. A different one was better at almost everything else I do. The cheap one nobody covers was good enough for the high-volume work.

None of that showed up in the comparisons, because none of them were testing my work.

And this isn't an image thing. It's the same for whatever model you're picking for writing, or code, or transcription.

The only benchmark that matters is the thing you do every week.

What's the one task you'd test your tools on?

## POST 3 - The honest take (soft link)

I tested three AI image models for a week and I can't tell you which one is best.

That sounds like a dodge, so let me explain what I actually found.

GPT Image 2 is better than the other two at text. Headlines, quote graphics, an infographic that has to get its facts right. That's a real gap and it's not close.

Nano Banana Pro is better at faces, at holding one look across a set, and at giving me something usable without much prompting.

Seedream 4.5 is worse than both and costs a fraction, which turns out to matter a lot when you're generating in volume.

Three models, three different answers, depending on the day.

Now the part the comparison posts leave out.

None of them nail it first try. I still write the prompt, look at what came back, and adjust. That loop didn't go away with any of them.

None of them know if the image is any good. They can't tell me the composition is muddy or that the idea was weak to begin with.

And the pricing moves. What's the cheap option this month may not be next month, so I'd rather you know how to test than take my ranking as permanent.

The way I think about it now is that these are three tools on a shelf, not three contenders for one slot.

Pick per job. Re-test when something big ships. That's the whole method.

Which one are you reaching for right now, and what for?
