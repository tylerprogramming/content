## POST 1 - The drop

Everyone kept telling me GPT Image 2 dethroned Nano Banana and it was time to switch. I generate images with AI every week, so before I changed anything I actually tested it.

Not on art prompts. Not on architecture renders or grids of the alphabet, which is what most of the comparisons run. I put three models through the work I repeat: Nano Banana Pro, GPT Image 2, and the cheap one nobody talks about, Seedream 4.5.

Six rounds. Images out of the box, a realistic scene, text sitting on an image, consistency across a whole set, pricing, and one hard infographic to try and break them.

Here's the honest verdict. GPT Image 2 genuinely wins on dense text and fact-checked infographics. If that's your job, use it, I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

For everything else I make, Nano Banana Pro is still the one. Better results with less prompting, faces that stay the same person, one look held across a set.

The surprise was Seedream 4.5. Not the best at anything, but good enough at most of it for a fraction of the price. If you're generating volume, that's the value pick.

So it's less about one winner and more about opening the right model for the job. I dropped the exact prompts and the setup in here.

## POST 2 - The tactic (try this today)

Here's something you can do this week that took me way too long to start doing.

Stop picking AI tools off other people's comparisons. Test them on your own work instead.

The method is kind of boring, which is why it works. Write down the five things you actually make with the tool. Not five interesting things, the five you repeat. Then run every model you're considering through all five with the exact same input. Same words, no extra prompting for the one you already like. Then lay the outputs next to each other.

The part people skip is the last step. Don't pick a winner. Pick per job.

When I did this with image models, one was clearly better at text and a different one was better at almost everything else I make. The cheap one nobody covers was good enough for the high-volume stuff. None of that showed up in the comparison videos, because none of them were testing my work.

It takes an afternoon. And you end up with an answer that's actually yours, instead of one you inherited from a headline.

This works for writing models, code models, transcription, anything you're choosing between.
