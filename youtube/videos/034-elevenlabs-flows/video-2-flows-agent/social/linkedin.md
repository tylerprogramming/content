## POST 1 - The story

I described an ad in one sentence and the pipeline built itself.

Not a demo on someone else's screen. My account, on camera. Paid partnership with ElevenLabs, and I'm one of their ambassadors, so let me tell you exactly what happened.

Making a video ad usually means five tools and a lot of moving between them. One thing for images, another for the clips, another for the voiceover, another for the music, then something to stitch it together.

ElevenCreative has a piece called Flows. It's a canvas where every node is a generation. An image node feeds a video node, the voiceover and the music come in beside it, and everything lands in a composition node that assembles the finished thing.

Normally you wire that canvas up yourself.

The Flows Agent builds it for you. You describe the ad, it asks what it needs to know, then it picks the models, creates the nodes, connects them, and runs the generations.

In the video I build a 30 second coffee ad from scratch with it. Five campaign images, animated clips off those images, a voiceover I chose, a music bed, all composed into one ad.

I never dragged a node.

The part I keep thinking about is what happens after the first version. I asked for cinematic text on screen, and it added it and re-ran the composition. The images and the clips I already liked stayed exactly where they were.

And once the pipeline exists, the product at the top is swappable. Same flow, new product, new ad. If you have a format that works and ten things to sell, that's the whole use case.

Honest note, because there always is one. This is not a single prompt. My Pepsi flow has a long conversation sitting behind it, and every run costs credits.

That's a real trade, and it's the one worth understanding before you start.

## POST 2 - The tactic (no link)

If an agent can spend your money, give it a ceiling instead of a babysitter.

I learned this the boring way, sitting there clicking approve on every single step of a run.

Here's the setup. I've been building video ads with the Flows Agent in ElevenCreative, which is a paid partnership with ElevenLabs. The agent creates the pipeline and then runs it, and every generation inside that pipeline costs credits.

So there's a permission setting, and it has three positions.

Auto run, where it does everything without asking. Confirm, where it stops and checks with you before each step. And the middle one, where it runs anything under a credit threshold you set, and only asks when a step goes over it.

I run the middle one. My threshold is 300 credits, and a typical image node comes in around 178, so most of the pipeline moves without me.

The expensive steps still stop and wait.

Which is exactly the split I wanted. I don't need to watch every image get made. I do want to know before something big runs.

What surprised me is how much that changed how I use the thing. On confirm-everything I was the bottleneck, so I batched fewer variations and iterated less. On auto-run I wasn't paying attention to what things cost.

The threshold gave me both. Speed on the cheap stuff, a hand on the wheel for the rest.

And I think this generalizes past video. Any agent that can spend, whether that's tokens or credits or API calls, is easier to trust when the limit is a number instead of your attention.

Set the number once. Stop approving line by line.

If you're running agents that cost money, how are you capping them right now?

## POST 3 - The honest take (soft link)

I keep seeing "one prompt made this entire ad" and I want to give you the honest version.

Paid partnership with ElevenLabs, I'm one of their ambassadors, and I still think this is worth saying plainly.

I built a video ad with the Flows Agent in ElevenCreative. It genuinely did pick the models, create the nodes, wire them together, and run the generations off a description.

But my best flow, the Pepsi one, has a long conversation behind it. Scroll the chat panel and you'll see a lot of back and forth.

That's not a knock on the tool. That's how working with any agent goes.

You get an 80 percent version, then you talk it the rest of the way. Nudge the prompt on the image node, swap the video model, ask for the text treatment, re-run the composition.

The thing that makes it worth it is that iterating stays cheap in effort. Only the part you changed gets redone, and nothing you already liked gets rebuilt.

The thing to go in aware of is that iterating is not free in credits. Every run bills. On the starter plan you get 30,000 credits a month and a full ad build ran me about 32,000, so if you're doing this seriously the bigger plan is the real answer, not a nice-to-have.

That's the part the one prompt posts never mention, and it's the part that decides whether this fits your work or not.

Where it clearly earns its place is repeat output. Build the pipeline once, swap the product, run it again. Ten ads from one flow instead of ten builds.

So the framing I'd offer is this. It's not a magic button, it's a pipeline builder that you steer by talking, and that's a genuinely better deal than wiring nodes by hand.
