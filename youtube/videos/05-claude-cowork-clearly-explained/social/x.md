## Standalone insight tweets (spread across the week)

**1 (the reframe)**
Most people use Claude like a smarter ChatGPT. A box you type into, it types back.

Cowork is the same brain with a pair of hands. It reaches into your computer and actually does the work.

That's the whole leap.

**2 (the safety habit)**
The thing that makes Cowork safe is dead simple.

Before it does anything, you hand it one folder. That's the only place it can touch. It can't wander the rest of your computer.

You're giving it the keys to one room, not the house.

**3 (proof / demo 1)**
I pointed Cowork at my Downloads folder, the disaster everyone has.

One plain-English request: rename everything so the names make sense, sort it into clean folders.

4 minutes later it was done. By hand that's an afternoon nobody ever does.

**4 (the secret power / web)**
People sit up at this part.

Add the Claude in Chrome extension and Cowork can use the open web for you. I had it open my YouTube Studio and write a performance report from my real analytics.

A job that ate hours of my month. Gone.

**5 (the capstone)**
Here's where it stops being a chatbot.

I took that "write my channel report" task and scheduled it. Every Monday, on its own.

Now the report is in my folder before I've had coffee. I didn't ask. It just shows up. You don't use it anymore. You hire it.

**6 (connectors)**
Cowork isn't trapped on your computer either.

One click connects it to Gmail, Google Drive, Notion. So instead of browsing the web for something, it reaches straight into your own accounts and files the result where it belongs.

**7 (honest take)**
I won't pretend Cowork is flawless.

No memory across sessions yet. Desktop only. Live tasks need the app open, though scheduled ones run on Anthropic's servers so your laptop can be closed.

But it's a research preview. The worst it will ever be, and already this good.

**8 (where to start)**
Frozen by too much choice? Pick one tonight:

1. Organize a messy folder
2. Research a topic
3. Create a document or spreadsheet
4. Analyze data you already have

Get one win. Your brain fills in the rest.
