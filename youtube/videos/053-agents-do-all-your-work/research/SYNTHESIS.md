Three briefs (nate-herk-agents.md, nick-saraev-automations.md, ai-agents-landscape.md) all point
the same way. This is the fused positioning + the concrete changes to make.

## What all three agree on

1. **The lane is Claude / Claude Code agents, and it prints.** Nick's Claude course 2.29M, Tech With
   Tim 1.50M, Nate's Claude tutorial 922K, plus a wall of clones at 800K-1.6M. This is exactly Tyler's
   lane. Move: **anchor on "Claude" by name** in title, thumbnail, and throughout - don't hide it under
   generic "AI agents."
2. **~1 in 3 "AI agent" videos is an automation in a costume.** Nate's two biggest (1.11M, 902K) are
   n8n router-workflows, not agents. Nick literally says on camera "they're not AI agents." The audience
   rewards REAL, model-native agents doing a concrete task far more at the very top.
3. **The honesty gap is the whole opening.** Everyone over-promises ("do ALL your work") then demos toys
   on throwaway accounts (Nate's "Michael Scott" + paper trading). Nobody admits where it breaks.

## The moat (what nobody else combines)

Tyler is the honest, engineer-grade version: **real Claude agents doing my REAL job on my REAL accounts,
and here's exactly where it failed.** Three pillars no competitor stacks together:
- **Real accounts, real stakes** - his actual inbox, calendar, business (not Michael Scott, not paper trading).
- **He names the agent-vs-automation bait-and-switch on camera** - instant credibility + a curiosity hook.
- **Honest limits + real SWE background** - Nate/Nick lean no-code; Tyler is an actual engineer, so "it's
  just a text file" from him is authority, not a gimmick.

The closest existing analog is Nate's "I Turned Claude Into a 24/7 Trader" (592K) - Claude Code + Routines,
a stateless agent on a schedule with memory files. It validates our whole model. We do the real, relatable,
daily-work version of it instead of a flashy trading toy.

## The 5 concrete changes to make 053 amazing

1. **Anchor on Claude by name.** Retitle toward "How To Use AI Agents To Do ALL Your Work (Claude Code)"
   or lead with Claude in the first line. Say "Claude" early and often; it is the #1 demand term.
2. **Add the agent-vs-automation beat, on camera (NEW).** Early - right after the live run or the one-idea -
   call it out: "Most 'AI agent' videos are just an n8n workflow with one AI step. That's an automation.
   A real agent decides its own steps. Here's the difference, and everything I show you is the real thing."
   This is a differentiator AND a curiosity spike ("the videos you've been watching were lying a little").
3. **Make a real FAILURE a featured beat, not just the limits section (NEW).** The landscape says the
   failure moment can BE the hook. Plant it in the open loop ("I'll show you the one that got it wrong"),
   then show a real miss mid-video and how you catch it. Toy-demo channels never do this; it is pure trust.
4. **Sharpen the sticky mental model.** Not just "text file + tools." Make it: **"an agent is a text file
   plus your apps, put on a schedule, and its memory is just more files."** That one sentence is the thing
   people repeat. (Nate's "text file + a schedule" does big numbers; we add the apps + memory.)
5. **Contrarian confidence in the open.** Lean into "real accounts, real work" vs everyone else's toy demos:
   "This isn't a demo on a fake account. This is my actual inbox and my actual business." That single line
   separates it from the entire field.

## What to AVOID (from all three)
- No money amounts in the title, no "$400K" hooks (that's Nick's lane; off-voice for Tyler).
- Don't call a workflow an agent (the whole point - be the one who's honest).
- No agency/FOMO CTA, no guru name-drops.
- Not a 2-6 hour marathon; the tight 30-40 min build wins on retention.

## Net: what stays vs what's added
- STAYS (already right per research): demo-before-build (live run at 0:30), real receipts, honest limits,

- ADD: Claude-by-name anchor, the agent-vs-automation call-out beat, a featured real-failure moment, the
