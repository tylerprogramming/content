**Main tweet:**
I'm not a video editor. I didn't build a tool. But my videos now get their dead air cut and captions added automatically. I just talk to Claude Code and it edits for me. Here's the whole workflow:

**Reply 1:**
The two parts of editing I hated most: cutting dead air (every um, every pause) and adding word-by-word captions. Both are totally mechanical. There's no creativity in finding a silent gap. A computer should just do it.

**Reply 2:**
So now I open HyperFrames, HeyGen's open-source video tool, drop a clip in, and ask Claude Code to cut the silences. A 3 minute clip drops to under 2. The only thing removed is the quiet.

**Reply 3:**
Captions are the same move. I copy a style prompt, paste it into Claude Code, and it transcribes with Whisper and wires the captions on. Want a different look? I just ask for another one.

**Reply 4:**
One thing I learned: to find silence, don't use the transcript. Whisper stretches words across the pauses so the gaps vanish. Measure the actual audio loudness instead. That's the difference between clean cuts and garbage.

**Reply 5:**
It's not one-click magic and it doesn't replace a real editor. It won't pace your story or pick your best take. It just does the boring parts. But once the styles are dialed in, it's cheaper than the caption apps I was paying for.

**Reply 6 (CTA):**
Everything is open-source and free: HyperFrames, Whisper, ffmpeg, tied together with Claude Code. Full walkthrough: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AH1ZpX7SD9Y
