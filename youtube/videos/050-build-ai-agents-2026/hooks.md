# 050 - Cold Opens (word-for-word)

Structure for all three (from the Nate first-2-min teardown): cold-open PAYOFF over b-roll, FACE hidden until ~0:08, then a specific/quantified claim, the deliverable, an objection pre-empt ("you don't need to be an engineer"), DELAYED credibility (IBM/Chase/Pfizer), a roadmap tease, one open loop, hard cut into step 1.

Voice: no em dashes, no hype words, short sentences, "so/and then," humble. Tyler is a software engineer - the resume is DELAYED trust, never "I'm not a developer."

---

## RECOMMENDED - Hook A: "It ran before I woke up"

**[0:00-0:08 - b-roll of the terminal running / a clean daily-brief card building. NO face. VO:]**
This morning, before I even sat down, an AI agent read my whole inbox, told me the five emails that actually mattered, and had the replies already drafted.

**[0:08 - hard cut to face, push-in:]**
And I built it in about the time it takes to watch this video.

**[0:11-0:19 - deliverable, cards build 1..2..3:]**
So we are going to build one together. A real agent. It connects to your actual inbox and your calendar, and it does a real job for you every morning.

**[0:20-0:27 - objection pre-empt, chips: "Never coded" / "No framework":]**
And you do not need to be an engineer to do this. There is no framework, no glue code. You mostly just tell it what you want in plain English.

**[0:27-0:33 - delayed credibility:]**
I have been a software engineer for about eight years. IBM, then Chase, now Pfizer. And I am telling you, the way we build these in 2026 is nothing like it was even a year ago.

**[0:33-0:41 - roadmap card + open loop:]**
Five steps. By step three it is reading real email. And that agent from the start, the one that ran before I woke up, I will show you exactly how it did that at the end.

**[hard cut to Step 1]**

> Why recommended: the payoff is concrete and quantified ("five emails, replies drafted"), the open loop ("how it ran before I woke up") is planted and deferred to the end, and the credibility lands late and does the "trust me, this changed" job without ever calling him a non-developer.

## Hook B: "Chatbot vs agent" (the reframe)

**[0:00-0:08 - split b-roll: a plain chat window on one side, the agent doing work in an app on the other. NO face. VO:]**
Most people think they have used an AI agent. They have used a chatbot. There is a big difference, and once you see it you cannot unsee it.

**[0:08 - hard cut to face:]**
A chatbot talks. An agent does the work, inside your real apps.

**[0:11-0:19 - deliverable:]**
So today we build a real one. Claude, connected to your inbox and your calendar, running a job for you. Not a demo. Something you would actually keep.

**[0:20-0:27 - objection pre-empt, chips:]**
And no, you do not need to be an engineer. No framework to learn. You describe the job, it does the job.

**[0:27-0:34 - delayed credibility:]**
I write software for a living. Eight years, IBM, Chase, now Pfizer. The old way to build these was genuinely hard. This is not that anymore.

**[0:34-0:41 - roadmap + open loop:]**
Five steps. Step three is where it stops being a chatbot and gets its hands on a real app. And there is one thing everybody skips in step two that quietly breaks the whole thing. I will show you.

**[hard cut to Step 1]**

> Why it works: the reframe is a strong curiosity gap for people who assume they already know this. Open loop is the "step two mistake." Slightly less quantified than Hook A, which is why A wins.

## Hook C: "I gave Claude a job"

**[0:00-0:08 - b-roll: agent output scrolling, a calendar and inbox getting handled. NO face. VO:]**
I stopped asking AI questions. I started giving it jobs. And the first job I gave it was the one I hate most: my morning inbox.

**[0:08 - hard cut to face:]**
It handled it. Read everything, flagged what mattered, drafted the replies.

**[0:11-0:19 - deliverable:]**
So we are going to build that exact agent, together, right now. It connects to your real inbox and calendar and it runs a real routine for you.

**[0:20-0:27 - objection pre-empt:]**
You do not need to be an engineer for this. No framework, no heavy code. If you can describe the task, you can build the agent.

**[0:27-0:34 - delayed credibility:]**
I have been a software engineer for eight years. IBM, Chase, and now Pfizer. A year ago this took real work. In 2026 it takes about five steps.

**[0:34-0:41 - roadmap + open loop:]**
Here they are. And by the end you will see how to hand it off completely, so it just runs on its own every morning while you do something else.

**[hard cut to Step 1]**

> Why it works: "stopped asking questions, started giving jobs" is a clean, quotable reframe and very on-voice. Open loop is the hand-off/automation payoff. A little softer on the quantified claim than Hook A.
