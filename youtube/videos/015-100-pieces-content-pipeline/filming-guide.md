# Filming Guide
## How I Create 100+ Pieces of Content Per Week with AI

---

## Pre-Recording Setup

### Desktop/Screen Recording Prep
- [ ] Clean desktop, hide personal bookmarks
- [ ] Terminal open with font size bumped up (at least 16pt so viewers can read)
- [ ] Have a fresh directory ready for the live demo (e.g., `~/content/youtube/demo-video/`)
- [ ] Blotato open in a browser tab, logged in, with the current week's calendar visible
- [ ] Kie.ai open in another tab for carousel generation demo
- [ ] Have a sample video package already completed as a backup (in case live generation takes too long or errors out)

### Content to Have Ready
- [ ] A completed content calendar screenshot or Blotato view showing 100+ scheduled posts (for the hook)
- [ ] Side-by-side examples of the same topic on different platforms (LinkedIn text vs. Instagram carousel vs. X post)
- [ ] Pipeline diagram (simple, can be made in Excalidraw or Canva): Research → Planning → Filming → Content Gen → Visuals → Scheduling
- [ ] Weekly calendar graphic showing the rhythm (Weekend = Research, Batch Day = Filming, Weekdays = Autopilot)
- [ ] Stats overlay graphics: "70% of creators spend 10+ hours/week" and "Only 26% prefer AI content"
- [ ] "Centaur Model" diagram: Human Direction + Machine Speed

### Terminal Commands to Type on Screen
These are the exact commands for the live demo sections:

```
# Research (Section 4)
/yt-search content repurposing AI

# Transcribe reference (Section 4)
/transcribe https://youtube.com/watch?v=[pick a real video ID]

# Video package (Section 5)
/yt [paste brief context about the topic]

# Social content (Section 6)
/content [slug]

# Shorts (Section 6)
/shorts

# SEO (mentioned briefly)
/seo [slug]
```

## Filming Playbook (Section by Section)

### Hook (0:00 - 1:30)
**Setup:** Screen recording of Blotato calendar, then cut to camera.
**What to film:**
1. Screen record: Slowly scroll through the content calendar showing all the scheduled posts. Pause briefly on each platform section.
2. Camera: Tyler at desk, direct to camera. Confident, not hype-y.
3. Screen record: Side-by-side of LinkedIn vs. Instagram vs. X post showing the same topic in different formats.

**Tip:** Film the camera portions after the screen recordings so you can reference exactly what's on screen.

### The Problem (1:30 - 3:00)
**Setup:** Camera only (with stat overlays added in post).
**What to film:**
1. Tyler talking to camera about the old workflow. Let it feel personal, like you're telling a friend about how things used to be.
2. No demos in this section, just you and the stats.

**Tip:** Film this in one take if possible. Authenticity matters here.

### System Overview (3:00 - 5:00)
**Setup:** Camera + pipeline diagram overlay (added in post, or draw live on iPad).
**What to film:**
1. Tyler talking through the six stages. The diagram builds on screen as each stage is mentioned.
2. Emphasize the split: "Stages 1-3 are thinking. Stages 4-6 are reformatting."

**Tip:** Film camera first, add the diagram in post. It's easier to time the animation to the voice than vice versa.

### Live Demo: Research (5:00 - 8:00)
**Setup:** Screen recording with picture-in-picture (Tyler in corner).
**Commands to type on screen:**
1. `/yt-search content repurposing AI` - Wait for results (about 30 seconds)
2. Show the research output file briefly
3. `/transcribe [URL]` - This takes about 60 seconds

**Handling dead air while AI processes:**
- When /yt-search is running: "It's pulling the top results on YouTube right now, sorting by views, looking at what titles are performing."
- When /transcribe is running: "This is downloading the audio and running it through Whisper. Takes about a minute. In a real session I'd be reviewing the research while this runs."
- If it takes longer than expected, cut to the finished result. Say "I'll skip ahead to the output."

**Tip:** Run these commands once before filming to make sure they work and to estimate timing. Have the finished outputs ready to cut to if anything glitches.

### Live Demo: Video Package (8:00 - 12:00)
**Setup:** Screen recording with PIP.
**Commands to type on screen:**
1. `/yt` with topic context pasted in
2. This generates multiple files, takes 2-3 minutes

**What to show on screen:**
- The command being typed
- Files appearing in the directory (quick `ls` of the output folder)
- Open titles.md, scroll through briefly
- Open hooks.md, show the three options
- Open script.md, scroll to show length and structure
- Open filming-guide.md, highlight the practical details

**Handling dead air while AI processes:**
- "This is the big one. It's analyzing the transcripts, doing web research, and building the full package. This usually takes two to three minutes."
- Talk about what it's generating while it works: "It'll come back with five title options, three hook scripts, a full video script with screen markers, and a filming guide like the one I'm using right now to film this video."

**Tip:** This is the longest processing wait. Consider a time-lapse cut with a "2 minutes later" text overlay, then show the results.

### Live Demo: Content Generation (12:00 - 16:00)
**Setup:** Screen recording with PIP. Fast cuts between different outputs.
**Commands to type on screen:**
1. `/content [slug]` - Generates LinkedIn, Instagram, X posts
2. `/shorts` - Generates short-form scripts

**What to show on screen:**
- LinkedIn post output (highlight: no hashtags, no markdown, conversational)
- Instagram carousel content (highlight: six slides, hook on slide one)
- Kie.ai generating the carousel visuals (quick screencap)
- X thread output (highlight: under 230 chars, no hashtags)
- Shorts scripts (highlight: under 60 seconds, hook-first)
- Quick counter graphic: "That's 47 pieces of content from two commands"

**Pacing:** This section should be FAST. Quick cuts. Show output, explain in one sentence, move to the next platform. Don't dwell.

**Tip:** Film the Kie.ai carousel generation separately and splice it in. It takes a few seconds and looks great as a visual.

### Live Demo: Scheduling (16:00 - 18:00)
**Setup:** Screen recording of Blotato.
**What to show:**
1. Blotato dashboard with connected accounts
2. Scheduling a few posts as examples
3. The calendar view with everything filled in
4. Quick walkthrough of the posting schedule (Mon/Thu = long-form, etc.)

**Tip:** Have the week already partially scheduled so you're not building from zero on camera. Show the process of adding a few, then cut to the full calendar.

### Weekly Rhythm (18:00 - 20:00)
**Setup:** Camera with calendar graphic overlay (added in post).
**What to film:**
1. Tyler explaining the weekly flow. Relaxed, conversational.
2. The calendar graphic builds as each day is explained.

**Tip:** This is a good section to film sitting back from the desk, more casual posture. It's the "here's how this fits into real life" moment.

### AI Slop Section (20:00 - 22:00)
**Setup:** Camera only. More serious tone, but not preachy.
**What to film:**
1. Direct to camera. Acknowledge the concern honestly.
2. Explain the centaur model simply.
3. Land the key phrase: "I handle the thinking. AI handles the reformatting."
4. Mention the 26% stat.

**Tip:** Film this in one or two takes. It should feel real, not rehearsed. This is the most important section for building trust with skeptical viewers.

### CTA + Close (22:00 - 23:00)
**Setup:** Camera, same as hook setup.
**What to film:**
1. Quick mention of Creator Engine + Skool
2. Subscribe CTA
3. Natural sign-off

**Tip:** Keep it under 60 seconds. The video earned the subscribe already.
