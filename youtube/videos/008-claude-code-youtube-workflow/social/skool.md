## POST 1 - The drop

I finally turned my whole YouTube research and planning into four commands.

It used to take me two or three hours before I even started writing. Open YouTube, click through a dozen videos, take notes, then outline everything from scratch in a Google Doc. Every single video, the same routine over again.

So I built four Claude Code skills to do it for me.

The first searches YouTube and hands me the newest videos on a topic sorted by views, so I can see what's working and what nobody has touched. The second pulls a full transcript of a top video, then I talk to Claude about its structure and where the gaps are. The third takes my topic and angle and writes the whole package, titles, hooks, a full script, and a filming guide. The fourth generates the thumbnail with Kie.ai.

Here's the fun part. This video was planned by that same system. The script came out of it, and so did the thumbnail.

I'm not selling a tool. These skills are just markdown files with instructions, so any of you can build the same thing for a workflow you repeat.

I dropped the setup and the exact skills in here. What part of your content process would you hand off first?

## POST 2 - The tactic (try this today)

A Claude Code skill is just a markdown file. I want to make that really clear, because I think the word "skill" makes it sound harder than it actually is.

There's no framework and there's no code. It's a text file with instructions in it.

Here's how to make your first one. Pick something you do the same way every week. Write down the steps in plain English, the way you'd explain it to someone new on your team. Do this first, then this, here's what good output looks like, here's what to avoid. Save it as a markdown file.

That's the skill. Now you ask for it by name instead of re-explaining yourself every time.

The thing nobody tells you is how low the bar is. If you can explain your process to a person, you can write one.

All of mine are free and public if you want to copy the format: github.com/tylerprogramming/claude-skills

What's something you explain the same way every single time? That's your first skill.

## POST 3 - The discussion (no link)

I want to ask you all something, because I think the answer is different for everyone.

I run my whole YouTube research and planning through four Claude Code skills now. Search, transcripts, the script package, the thumbnail. It took me a while to work out which parts were actually worth automating.

The thing I learned is that the parts worth handing off are the ones that are repeatable AND boring. Not the ones that are hard. Hard is usually where the value is, and you want to keep that.

For me it was research. Two or three hours of clicking through videos before I could start writing. Completely mechanical, zero creativity, and it was the reason I skipped it half the time.

So here's my question.

What's the repeatable, boring part of your week? The thing you do the same way every single time and dread a little?
