I run my entire YouTube channel from a terminal.

Not part of it. The whole research and planning side.

For a long time, starting a video meant the same slow routine. Open YouTube, search the topic, click through a dozen videos, take notes on what's working. Then a Google Doc, and outline everything from scratch.

Every new video, the same thing over again. Two or three hours before I even started writing. Half the time I'd skip it and just wing it.

So I built a system. Four Claude Code skills that handle the whole pipeline.

The first one searches YouTube for me. One command pulls the newest videos on a topic and sorts them by views. I see what's working, and what nobody has covered yet.

The second pulls the full transcript of a top video. Then I actually talk to Claude about how it's structured and where the gaps are.

The third takes my topic and my angle and writes the whole package. Titles, hooks, a full script, a filming guide. It even does its own web research first.

The fourth generates the thumbnail.

Here's the part I like. This video was planned by that same system. The script I read on camera came out of it, and so did the thumbnail people clicked to get there.

I want to be honest about what this is. These skills aren't a tool I'm selling. They're just markdown files with instructions.

Any of you can build the same thing for a workflow you repeat every week.

And the best part is it compounds. Every skill I add makes the next video faster.

If you built one skill for your own work, what would you automate first?
