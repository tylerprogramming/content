## Why this rides a proven lane
The "I Automated My Entire ___ With Claude Code" title pattern is one of the highest-performing formats on AI YouTube right now. It works because it stacks three things viewers already want to click on:
- A concrete, finished outcome ("entire ___ automated") - not a tutorial promise, a done thing.
- A specific, hyped tool (Claude Code) that people are actively searching.
- An implied "you could do this too" payoff.

Tyler already has proof this exact lane converts for HIS channel and HIS audience - the original YouTube-workflow version is his #1 video by views and subs gained in the cohort. This is not a guess. This is running the play that already worked, one level up, with more surface area to show and a broader promise.

## The differentiator (why this beats the copycats)
Ninety percent of "I automated X with Claude Code" videos are staged. Someone builds a toy demo for the camera, it works once, and you never see it again. Tyler's angle is the opposite and it is the entire moat:

- It runs on a REAL, live channel. 23.5K subs, real videos shipping every week, real analytics behind it.
- The skills are REAL and already built - /yt-search, /transcribe, /yt-package, /yt-seo, /shorts, /content. Not created for the video. These are the tools that actually produce the channel you're watching.
- He demos them LIVE. He talks to Claude Code on camera and you watch it work. Nothing is faked, nothing is edited to look faster than it is.
- The output is verifiable. Roughly 32 pieces of content a week from 1-2 content days, and the viewer is literally consuming one of those pieces right now.

That last point is the killer. The video IS the proof. You are watching an artifact of the exact system being described.

## Honest-framing guardrails (non-negotiable)
This channel's whole brand is that Tyler doesn't overclaim. That trust is the asset. Rules for this script:

- NO fake money claims. Never "I made $X," never "this makes me $X/month," never dollar amounts on screen. The proof is the content volume and the real channel, full stop.
- Real numbers only. 23.5K subs, ~32 pieces/week, 1-2 content days, the specific skills. If a number isn't real, it doesn't go in.
- Show the seams. Explicitly cover what the automation does NOT do - it doesn't film for him, it doesn't replace judgment, he still reviews and approves everything, and bad input still makes bad output. Naming the limitations is what makes the strong claims believable.
- No "set it and forget it" fantasy. This is leverage, not magic. Frame it as "one afternoon of my time turns into a week of content," which is true, instead of "fully hands-off," which isn't.
- The tools are the hero, Tyler is the operator. Keep it grounded in "here's a system you can build," not "look how special I am."

## Who this serves (both audiences, one video)
Per the channel positioning (AI agents and automations for builders AND solopreneurs), this video is unusually good at serving both at once:

- Aspiring builders / technical viewers: they came for "how did he wire Claude Code skills to do this." The system section (layer 2) and the live demos give them the buildable blueprint. They leave wanting to build their own skills.
- Solopreneurs / creators / non-technical operators: they came for "can I run a whole content operation by myself." The showcase (layer 1) and the opportunity section (layer 3) give them the vision - one person, one afternoon, a full brand's worth of content, or a service you could sell to clients.

The 3-layer spine is what lets one video hold both:
1. Showcase - "I automated my entire content pipeline" (hook + result, everyone stays)
2. System - "here's exactly how, and you can copy it" (builders lean in)
3. Opportunity - "you could run a content business, or do this for clients" (solopreneurs lean in)

## CTA strategy
- Hero funnel: https://free.tylerai.dev/youtube/ - free pack + newsletter. This is where the "I want to copy this system" energy goes. One clear ask.
- Secondary, soft: the Skool community, mentioned once, low-pressure, never the raw URL as the primary.

## Format and pacing
- Target 12-15 min. Tight. This is a demo-heavy video, so momentum matters - cut every dead second, keep the live demos moving, don't let Claude Code "thinking" time drag on screen (speed-ramp or cut).
- Retention structure: open on the result (a week of content), rewind to "here's how," pay off with live demos, land on the opportunity. Standard proven arc.
- Reuse the winner's DNA. The original video's comment section and retention graph are the best guide we have - lean into whatever moments landed there (the "watch it actually work" beats).

## Risks and how we handle them
- Risk: feels like an ad for his own skills. Fix: teach the transferable concept (custom skills = repeatable workflows you define once), not just "buy my stuff." Give real value in the how.
- Risk: too much scope, viewer gets lost. Fix: anchor everything to the single kickoff -> full week visual. One input, many outputs. Keep returning to that spine.
- Risk: "I could never build this." Fix: the opportunity section explicitly lowers the bar - start with one skill, one workflow, and the free pack hands them a starting point.
