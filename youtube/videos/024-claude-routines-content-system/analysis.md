## The KEY Insight: Ride Anthropic's Own Framing

Anthropic's video is the authoritative framing for this entire topic, and it is worth aligning to because it is both proven (192K) and "official," which gives the framing borrowed credibility. Two things from their description are the model to ride:

1. **The "proactive teammate" hook.** Their exact line: Routines turn Claude Code into "a proactive teammate that reads your repo and opens a PR before you've opened your laptop." That "before you've opened your laptop / it ran while you slept" framing is the single most shareable idea in the topic. It makes the value visceral. Tyler should open on this energy, just swapped from "opens a PR" to "had your competitor report, your inbox digest, and five video ideas waiting before you opened your laptop."

2. **The "trigger, context, steering" mental model.** Anthropic teaches that every Routine comes down to three decisions: the trigger (what fires it), the context (what it can see and read), and the steering (how you have told it to behave). This is a clean, memorable, teachable framework, and almost nobody else in the lane uses it. Tyler should adopt it explicitly. It upgrades the current "trigger types + connectors + SOP prompt" explanation into a named model the viewer can hold in their head, which is exactly what makes a tutorial quotable and link-worthy.

**The differentiation move:** Anthropic frames this for developers (read your repo, open a PR). Tyler applies the identical mental model to a content business (read your inbox, scrape your competitors, draft your week). Same proven framing, audience nobody else is serving with it.

## Runtime: Should the 29 Minutes Tighten?

Honest read: shorter overperforms in this lane. Nate Herk cleared 100K at 7 minutes and 89K at 16 minutes. The two clear wins under 17 minutes are the loudest signal in the scan. The 29-minute runtime is not disqualifying. Anthropic did 22 minutes at 192K, Nate did 31 minutes at 55K, and Jono did 68 minutes at 78K. Long works when every segment escalates and nothing drifts.

**Recommendation:** keep the runtime, but pressure-test it. Every minute must earn the next. Specifically:
- The hook and Point 1 (what Routines are) should land inside the first 3 minutes, hard. Adopt the trigger/context/steering model here so the explanation is fast and memorable, not meandering.
- The two example Routines are the proof and should stay, but trim any setup narration that repeats.
- The live build, the /loop reveal, and the comparison table are the differentiated meat. Protect those.
- If anything sags in the edit, cut from the business-case monologue (Point 2), not from the demos. The demos are what Nate's 7-minute winner does not have.

If Tyler wants a second asset out of this, the 7-minute Nate model is a ready-made Short: just the /loop reveal or just one Routine, single-focus, "10x" framed.

### Gap 3: The /loop command is uncovered
Local recurring tasks via /loop is the "nobody's talking about it" beat. It is the perfect retention payoff because it answers a question the cloud-Routines half raises ("what about tasks that need my local files?").

### Gap 4: Honest, verifiable demos
Most of the lane is hype and concept. Tyler verifies on camera that the Routine actually ran in the cloud, shows the real Slack output, and keeps the existing caveat about confirming cloud execution rather than claiming it. Honesty is the trust differentiator.

Nate Herk drives to a free Skool community and it works. Tyler runs the identical model. This is not a differentiator on its own (it matches the lane), but it means Tyler is not fighting a paid-vault disadvantage. The differentiation has to come from the content-business angle, not the CTA.
