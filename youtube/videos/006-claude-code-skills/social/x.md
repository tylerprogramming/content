**Main tweet:**
I retype the same prompts into Claude Code less and less. I save the workflow once, then run it with one command. They're called skills. I use three every day and built each in about a minute. Here's how they work:

**Reply 1:**
A skill is just a saved way of working with Claude. Instead of explaining what you want every time, you write it down once and Claude remembers. Three I use daily: /transcribe, /journal, and one that scripts my videos.

**Reply 2:**
The part that surprised me: there's no code. Open the folder and a skill is one file called SKILL.md. Plain English instructions, a name, and the tools it's allowed to use. That's the whole thing.

**Reply 3:**
Two places you can put them. In a project's .claude folder it works only there. In your home ~/.claude/skills folder it works everywhere, every project, every chat. That's what makes them feel like superpowers.

**Reply 4:**
And you update them just by asking. If my journal skill isn't asking the right questions, I say update it to also ask about my mood. It does. The tools grow with you.

**Reply 5:**
The pattern is simple. Do something with Claude. Like the result. Say make a skill out of this. Done. Next time it's one command.
