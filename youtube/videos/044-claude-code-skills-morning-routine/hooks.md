# Hooks - 044 Claude Code Skills Morning Routine

Three options for the first 30 seconds. Word-for-word. Pick one, memorize the first two lines, let the rest breathe.

The reference video's hook was 12 seconds of pure promise and then 11 seconds of credentials. It worked, but it left the door open on proof. Every hook below puts something real on screen inside the first 15 seconds, because that's the gap.

---

## Option A - The confession
**Technique: pattern interrupt through self-deprecation**

> I've built fifty-one Claude Code skills.
>
> [beat]
>
> I use three of them before nine in the morning.
>
> These three. Email, my community, and my channel numbers.
>
> Twenty minutes of checking things, every single morning, gone.
>
> And at the end of this video I'm going to build a fourth one from scratch, on camera, that runs all three of them with one command. No code. It's a text file.
>
> Let me show you the actual mornings, with my actual numbers, not a demo account.

**Why it works.** The 51 sets an expectation and the 3 breaks it in the second line. Self-deprecation buys trust faster than credentials do, and it inoculates you against the "AI bro with 50 tools" reaction that the informed segment of this audience has pre-loaded. "Not a demo account" is a direct shot at what the 241K video never delivered.

**Note:** verify the count on film day. If it's not 51, say the real number.

---

## Option B - The cold open
**Technique: show, don't tell - start mid-action**

> [Start on the terminal, already typing. No face yet.]
>
> This is my actual morning.
>
> [type `/gmail unread from the last 24 hours` and hit enter]
>
> That's my real inbox. Not a demo.
>
> [output starts landing]
>
> While that runs - I used to do this by hand. Open Gmail, open Skool, open YouTube Studio, three tabs, twenty minutes, every morning, before I'd done a single thing that mattered.
>
> Now it's three commands. And by the end of this video you'll have built one yourself, without writing any code.
>
> [now cut to face]
>
> Let's go.

**Why it works.** The single biggest weakness of every competing video in this category is that nothing is ever shown running. Opening *inside* the thing running is the hardest possible flex on that. Delaying your face by 20 seconds is unusual enough to hold a scroller. The dead air while the command runs is filled by the "I used to do this by hand" story, which is exactly where that story belongs.

**Risk:** needs a clean take and a real inbox you're willing to show. Have the redaction plan ready before you hit record.

---

## Option C - The arithmetic
**Technique: curiosity gap through a specific number**

> Twenty minutes.
>
> That's how long I used to spend every morning just *checking* things. Email. My community. My channel numbers. Three tabs, no decisions, no work done.
>
> Twenty minutes times five days is an hour and forty minutes a week. That's a video I didn't film.
>
> So I replaced the whole thing with three Claude Code skills. And a skill is not code - it's a text file that tells the AI how to do one job. I'll show you exactly what's inside one.
>
> Here's the morning, start to finish. Real numbers, real inbox.

**Why it works.** The reference video promised "hours" and never quantified a single one. Doing the arithmetic out loud is the cheapest credibility in the video. "That's a video I didn't film" translates abstract time into a loss the audience feels. The one-line definition of a skill lands early, which the fully non-technical audience needs.

**Note:** time your actual manual routine once before filming so the 20 is honest. If it's 12, say 12.

---

## Recommended

**Option B**, with Option A's "fifty-one, I use three" line moved to roughly 0:45 as the transition out of the cold open.

B attacks the exact weakness that leaves the 241K video beatable, and the two together give you a proof-first open and a trust-building confession without spending either one too early. C's arithmetic is worth keeping too - drop it into the close instead of the open.
