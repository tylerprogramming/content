## Production do's (from the brief - non-negotiable)
- Film HORIZONTAL (16:9). This is a long-form YouTube video, not a short.
- NO microphone visible in the shot. Use a lav hidden under the shirt, or an off-camera boom or desk mic out of frame. If the mic is in frame, reframe before you roll.
- Smart-casual clothing. No pajamas, no logo tees that clash with the ElevenLabs orange in the thumbnail.
- Good light. Natural window light or a ring/key light in FRONT of you. Do not sit backlit (no bright window behind you).
- Clean or relevant background. Tidy desk, plant, or a simple wall. Nothing distracting or messy behind you.
- AUDIO CHECK before filming. Record 15 seconds, play it back, confirm levels and no room echo/hum BEFORE you shoot the whole thing.

## THE HARD COMPLIANCE RULE - only dub what you own
- Every clip you put into the Dubbing tool on camera MUST be a video Tyler made and owns. His own footage, his own voice.
- Do NOT dub a movie, a TV clip, a song, another creator's YouTube video, or any third-party content. The brief bans it and it puts payment at risk.
- Pick the demo clip ahead of time: a short segment (30-90 seconds) from one of Tyler's existing published videos where he is talking clearly to camera. That clip is both the cold-open reveal AND the live-demo input, so they match.

## Exact on-screen demo steps (record the screen for these)
1. Open the Dubbing tool from the dashboard.
2. Upload YOUR owned clip (or paste a link to your own video). Say out loud that it is your own footage.
3. Point at the credit cost preview area so viewers know it is coming.
4. Pick the target language (start with Spanish). Show the language list so the "90+" is visible.
5. Open Advanced settings -> show Cloning strength (default 7) -> explain higher vs lower -> leave at default for this run.
6. Point at the credit cost before Generate. Say it is priced per minute and failed jobs are not charged.
7. Generate. Cut forward to the finished dub.
8. Play the Spanish dub. Then play the original English and the Spanish dub side by side so the preserved tone/timing is obvious.
9. REVIEW BEAT: show where you would edit the transcript / regenerate a clip in Dubbing Studio (or describe it if maintenance-mode). Emphasize listening through before publishing.
10. SCALE: add Japanese and Hindi off the same source clip (use the pre-dubbed files to keep the record tight). Show one video becoming several languages.
11. End with the original and the dubbed versions lined up.
