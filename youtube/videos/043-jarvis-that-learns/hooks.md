# Hooks

First 30 seconds. Open fast, no preamble, no "in this video."

---

## Hook A: the file that changes (RECOMMENDED)
**Technique:** show the payoff artifact immediately, then withhold the how.

> So this is a text file my assistant wrote about my own channel.
>
> [SHOW: brain.md, scroll to a dated rule]
>
> It says showcase videos beat tutorials for me, confirmed on July 30th. I did
> not write that line. An agent did, after it looked at how the last video
> actually performed, and then it went and updated the rule it had written the
> week before.
>
> That is the part everyone skips. There are a bunch of Claude Code assistants
> out there now, and I have looked at most of them. They all produce stuff.
> None of them ever check whether the stuff worked.
>
> So I built one that does. It is free, it is on GitHub, and I am going to show
> you the whole thing.

Why it works: the artifact is small, real, and slightly strange. A file that
rewrote itself is a better cold open than a dashboard tour, and it front-loads
the differentiator in about fifteen seconds.

---

## Hook B: the honest comparison
**Technique:** name the competitor, concede the point, then turn it.

> Chase AI put out a really good video on turning Claude into a personal
> assistant. Email triage, research briefs, content repurposing. I watched the
> whole thing and I took notes.
>
> But there is one thing missing from it, and from every other version of this
> I have seen. Nothing in the system ever learns. It produces, forever, and
> nobody ever tells it whether any of it worked.
>
> [SHOW: the HUD, agents lighting up]
>
> So here is mine. Same idea, one extra loop, and that loop is the whole point.

Why it works: naming a real creator generously is trust-building, and it makes
the differentiator concrete instead of abstract. Slight risk of sending viewers
to his video, so keep it to fifteen seconds and move.

---

## Hook C: the reboot problem
**Technique:** pattern interrupt, open on a flaw instead of a win.

> Most of these AI assistant setups have a problem nobody mentions. They only
> run while your laptop is open.
>
> Mine ran at 6:30 this morning while I was asleep. It found this.
>
> [SHOW: radar panel, 13.1x breakout]
>
> A video doing thirteen times what that channel normally does, one day old.
> Then it wrote itself a note about what to do about it.
>
> So let me show you how it works, and the one thing it does that the others
> do not.

Why it works: opens on a specific unglamorous flaw, which reads as honest
rather than salesy. The 13.1x number is real and on screen. Weakest of the
three at stating the actual differentiator up front.
