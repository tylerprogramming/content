## Post 1: The Platform Formatting Problem

Most AI-generated social content fails for one simple reason: it's not written for the platform it's being posted on.

LinkedIn posts don't use hashtags or bold markdown. X posts don't have hashtags either - they reduce reach. Instagram gets exactly the same 5 hashtags every time. YouTube Community posts ask a question to drive comments.

When you copy-paste the same content across every platform, it looks off. Readers notice, even if they can't explain why.

The fix isn't writing everything from scratch. It's teaching your AI the rules once - and having it apply them automatically every time.

I use a file called CLAUDE.md. It lives in my home directory. Every time I start Claude Code, it reads the file and knows exactly how each platform should be formatted, what my posting schedule looks like, and where to save files.

One config. Consistent output. Every week.

The AI tools most creators are using treat every platform the same. The ones getting real traction from content have figured out that distribution is a system - not a spray-and-pray.

## Post 2: How Research Changes What You Make

Most content creators decide what to make based on what they feel like making that week.

The ones consistently getting views decide based on what the data says is already working.

Here's a simple way to close that gap. Before you plan any video, search your topic on YouTube and pull the top-performing results from the last 30 days. Look at what's getting views. Look at what angles are missing.

I did this recently searching "claude code tutorial" and found 11 videos all aimed at developers. Not one aimed at content creators. That one observation became a video that's already outperforming my channel average.

The insight wasn't creative genius. It was just looking at the data before making the decision.

This kind of research used to take an hour manually. Now I run one command in my terminal and get a formatted report in about 90 seconds - view counts, titles, channels, sorted by performance and saved to a searchable file.

Research first. Make what you know is going to perform.

The gap between "feels right" and "data says yes" is where most content careers stall out.

## Post 3: The Real Math Behind Content at Scale

Here's what 32 pieces of content per week actually requires when you do it manually.

Two long-form video scripts. Two sets of titles and hooks. Two YouTube descriptions. Seven LinkedIn posts. Five X posts. Four Instagram carousels with 6 slides each. Five Short scripts. Five TikTok captions. Five Instagram Reel captions. Two YouTube Community posts. Scheduling across every platform.

Most solo creators burn out trying to keep up with a fraction of that. The ones who scale either hire a team or build a system.

The system I use runs through Claude Code. I run a research command, a script command, a repurpose command, and a scheduling command. The actual content creation part of my week - the writing, formatting, platform-specific adapting - takes about 20 minutes.

The 2 filming days are the rest of it. That part still takes me.

This isn't about replacing your creativity or your face on camera. It's about not spending your creative hours on tasks that a well-configured AI can handle in seconds.

The creators who figure out that distinction early are going to have an enormous advantage over the ones who are still manually writing every caption two years from now.
