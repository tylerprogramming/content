## POST 1 - The story

I used to be the person moving files around for Claude.

Not writing. Not thinking. Just moving things.

Copy a paragraph out of a doc, paste it into the chat, read the answer, paste that answer back into another file. Over and over, all week.

The thinking part took two minutes. The moving part took an hour.

Then I started using Cowork, and the moving part went away.

Here is what it actually is. Cowork lives in the Claude desktop app, not the website. You point it at one folder on your computer.

Now it can open what is in that folder, read it, and build new files back into it.

So instead of pasting, I just ask.

The first thing I tried was a messy client folder. Contracts, invoices, intake forms, all dumped in one place. I asked it to sort that out and it did, on my actual machine.

Then I dropped in six phone photos of receipts. It handed back an expense spreadsheet with vendor, date, and amount, and it flagged a subscription I had been paying for twice.

The one that changed my week was scheduled tasks. I asked once for a Monday morning report. Now it runs on its own and the file is sitting there before I have had coffee.

I want to be honest about one thing. It is still a research preview. It is not perfect and it has real limits today.

But this is the worst it will ever be, and it is already doing real parts of my work.

So I made the whole course on it. About an hour, module by module, and there is no coding anywhere in it.

## POST 2 - The tactic (no link)

The best thing I did with AI this year was write one plain text file about myself.

That sounds underwhelming. Stick with me for a second.

For a long time every session started the same way. I would explain who I am, what I do, who I am writing for, how I like things formatted. Then I would finally ask my actual question.

Next session, I typed all of it again.

I was paying a context tax on every single task, and I was paying it by hand.

So I wrote it down once instead.

It is a file sitting in a folder on my computer. Plain English. Who I am, what I am working on, how I write, what good output looks like, what I do not want to see.

The AI reads that file before every task in that folder.

That is the whole trick.

The difference showed up immediately. A report came back formatted the way I like it and I never said how. An email draft came back sounding like me instead of like a press release.

Here is the part I think transfers to anyone. Most people treat AI like a stranger they meet fresh every morning, so they keep re-introducing themselves.

Writing the context down once turns it into something that already knows you.

And it is never finished. Every time it gets something wrong, I add a line to the file. Ten minutes of work, spread out over weeks.

You also do not need a special tool for this. Any AI that can read a file on your machine can do it.

## POST 3 - The honest take (soft link)

Everyone is calling this stuff an AI employee. I use it every day, so let me be straight about what it actually is.

Cowork is Claude with access to a folder on your computer. That is the honest description.

It can open your files, read them, build new ones, and run a task on a schedule without you.

It is also still a research preview. It gets things wrong. Sometimes it does the task sitting right next to the one you asked for.

So here is what I actually hand it.

Anything mechanical and checkable. Sorting a messy folder. Pulling numbers out of receipt photos into a spreadsheet. Building the same weekly report from the same files.

Those are jobs where I can look at the output for ten seconds and know whether it is right.

And here is what I keep.

Anything where being 85 percent right is worse than not doing it at all. Anything I cannot verify at a glance. Anything that is really a judgment call wearing a task costume.

That line matters more than which tool you pick.

Never expect these things to be a hundred percent accurate. Even at 80 or 90 percent, they need you standing there.

The mistake I keep seeing is people expecting magic, getting something mediocre, and writing the whole category off.

The realistic version is better anyway. Find the boring repeatable part, hand that off, keep the part that needs you.

For me that was file wrangling and the Monday report. For you it is probably something completely different.
