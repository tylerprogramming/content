## POST 2 - The tactic (try this today)

Here is the ten minute version of the course you can do today, no setup required.

Open a text file and write down the stuff you keep re-explaining to AI every time you open it.

Who you are. What you actually do all day. Who you are writing for. How you like things formatted. What good output looks like to you. What you never want to see.

Plain English, the way you would explain it to someone new on your team. No structure required. Mine started as maybe fifteen lines.

Save it in the folder you work out of. In Cowork you point it at that folder and it reads the file before every task.

That is it. That is the whole tactic.

The reason it works is that most of us treat AI like a stranger we meet fresh every morning. You keep re-introducing yourself, and then you wonder why the output sounds generic.

Once it has the file, the drafts come back sounding like you without being asked. Reports come out formatted the way you like them and you never said how.

And it is never done. Every time it gets something wrong, add a line. That is the feedback loop, and it is why this gets better every week instead of staying the same.

You do not need Cowork specifically for this either. Any AI that can read a file on your machine works.

Try it today and tell me what you put in yours. What is the thing you are tired of re-explaining?

## POST 3 - The discussion (no link)

I want to ask you all something, because I think everyone's answer is different.

I have been running real parts of my week through Cowork for a while now. Sorting folders, receipts into spreadsheets, the Monday report, drafts in my voice. And the thing I learned is not which prompt to use. It is where the line sits.

The tasks worth handing off are the ones that are repeatable, boring, and checkable. I can glance at the output for ten seconds and know if it is right.

The tasks I keep are the ones where being 85 percent right is worse than not doing it. Anything I cannot verify quickly. Anything that is really a judgment call dressed up as a task.

Once I got that line clear, the whole thing got useful. Before that I was either handing off too much and cleaning up messes, or too little and doing everything myself.

So here is my question.
