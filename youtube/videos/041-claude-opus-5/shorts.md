## Reel 1 — "A cheaper version of the best AI model" (value take)
**Hook:** "Anthropic just put out a model that's almost as good as their most expensive one, for half the price."
- Claude Opus 5 is out. $5 in / $25 out per million tokens. Their top model, Fable, is double that.
- The pitch: it gets close to Fable's quality in a lot of things, at half the cost.
- My take after real use: for everyday building I can't tell them apart, and it's the same price as the model I was already on, so it was a free upgrade.
- Honest limit: Fable still has a slightly higher ceiling on the hardest jobs, so I keep it for the heavy stuff and use Opus 5 for everything else.
- Bottom line: for most of what you do, you probably don't need to pay double anymore.

## Reel 2 — "Same prompt, two AI models, two websites" (head-to-head)
**Hook:** "I gave the new Claude and the most expensive Claude the exact same prompt and had them each build a website. Watch."
- Same brief, same everything, only the model is different. Split screen.
- Both came back with a real, working landing page.
- Honest result: they're basically the same.
- The kicker: one cost about half as much.
- So if you can't tell the output apart on everyday work, why pay double?

## Reel 3 — "The AI setting nobody talks about" (effort-toggle tip)
**Hook:** "There's one setting in Claude that controls what it costs you, and most people never touch it."
- It's the effort / reasoning level: low, medium, high, extra-high. Controls how hard the model thinks.
- Counterintuitive part: higher isn't always better. On a lot of tasks, medium gives the same result for a fraction of the cost and time.
- How I use it: start lower, bump it up only when the task is genuinely hard.
- The 2026 point: the thing to tune isn't the model, it's the effort. That one dial is how you control your AI bill.
