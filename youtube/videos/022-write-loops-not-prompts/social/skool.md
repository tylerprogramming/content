## POST 1 - The drop

If you missed this one, it is still the video I point people at most.

I stopped prompting Claude Code one thing at a time. The guy who runs Claude Code at Anthropic said he does not prompt anymore, he writes loops that prompt for him. I am not a developer and that one line changed how I run my week.

Here is the simplest way I can put it. A prompt is a vending machine. One coin in, one thing out, then it is over. A loop is an employee. You tell it what good looks like once, and it keeps working until the job is actually done.

My favorite version has a funny name, the Ralph Wiggum loop. It runs the same instruction over and over until the work is finished. Claude Code turned that idea into a plugin, /loop.

In the video I walk through the commands that matter. /loop runs a task on a schedule. /goal runs until a finish line you wrote is true. /batch fans one job across a bunch of agents. If you only learn one, learn /loop.

Then I show you my real one. It fires at 7am before I am awake, does the boring research, drafts the work, and then stops and shows me. It does not send anything anywhere. I approve the good ones with coffee.

You do not need to code for any of it. What is the first thing you would hand to a loop?

## POST 2 - The tactic (try this today)

Here is the thing I wish someone had told me before I tried to automate anything.

Do not start with a loop. Start with one good result.

Most people pick a task, wrap it in automation, and then wonder why the output is bad. The problem is they never once did that task well with Claude sitting right there in front of them. You cannot describe good if you have not seen good.

So try this order instead. Pick the most boring repeatable thing in your week. Not the hard thing, the hard thing is usually where your value is and you want to keep it. Then make Claude do that one thing manually, one time, and nudge it until you actually like the output.

Now write down what you did. Plain English, the way you would explain it to someone new on your team. Do this first, then this, here is what good looks like, here is what to avoid. That file is a skill.

Then wrap that exact thing in /loop. Every Monday, do this. That is your first loop, and you just stopped being the thing inside it.

The part that compounds is the corrections. Every time it does something you do not love, you fix it once and tell Claude to remember. Day 30 is genuinely better than day 1 because you never re-teach the same note twice.

## POST 3 - The discussion (no link)

I want to ask you all something, because I think the answer is different for everyone and I am curious where the overlap is.

I have loops running while I am asleep now. Research, drafts, the boring repeatable middle of my week. It took me a while to work out which parts were actually worth handing off.

What I learned is that the tasks worth automating are the ones that are repeatable AND boring. Not the ones that are hard. Hard is usually where the value is, and you want to keep that part.

The other thing I learned is that a loop needs a place to stop. Mine does the work and then just waits and shows me. It does not send anything, it does not publish anything, it does not decide anything. That one rule is the only reason I trust it enough to leave it alone.

So here is my question, and I mean it literally.

What is the repeatable, boring part of your week? The thing you do the same exact way every time and dread a little before you start?

Drop it below with how long it takes you. If a few of you name the same thing, I will build a loop for it and share the whole setup in here so you can copy it.
