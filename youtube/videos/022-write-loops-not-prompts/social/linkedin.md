I stopped prompting Claude Code.

Not because I got tired of it. Because the guy who built Claude Code said something that stuck with me.

He said he doesn't prompt anymore. He writes loops that prompt for him. His job is to write the loop.

I'm a content creator, not a developer, and that one line changed how I run my whole business.

Here's the difference, as plainly as I can put it.

A prompt is a vending machine. You put one coin in, you get one thing out, and then it's over. Tomorrow you walk back up and do it again.

A loop is an employee. You tell it what good looks like once, and it keeps working. It checks, it acts, it repeats, until the job is actually done.

The version I like most has a funny name. The Ralph Wiggum loop. It just runs the same instruction over and over until the work is finished. Claude Code turned that idea into a plugin, /loop.

There are four commands that do most of the work.

/loop runs a task on a schedule. /goal runs until a finish line you wrote is true. /batch fans one job across a bunch of agents. And ultracode is for scale. If you only learn one, learn /loop.

Here's my real one. Every morning at 7, before I'm even awake, a loop runs my content research, drafts the week's short scripts, and then stops and shows me. I read the drafts with my coffee and thumbs-up the good ones.

One thing I want to be honest about. A loop is only as safe as its guardrails. A good one knows the intent, has something that can say no, has skills worth calling, and can always stop.

You don't need to be technical to start. Pick the most boring thing you do every week. Get one good result by hand. Then wrap that exact thing in a loop.

The best part is it compounds. Every time it does something you don't love, you fix it once and tell Claude to remember. Day 30 is better than day 1.

What's the one repetitive task you'd hand to a loop first?
