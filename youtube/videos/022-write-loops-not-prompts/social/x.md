**Reply 1:**
A prompt is a vending machine. One coin in, one thing out, then it's over. A loop is an employee. You tell it what good looks like once, and it keeps working until the job is actually done.

**Reply 2:**
The version I love has a funny name. The Ralph Wiggum loop. It just runs the same instruction over and over until the work is finished. Claude Code turned that idea into a plugin, /loop.

**Reply 3:**
Four commands do most of it. /loop runs a task on a schedule. /goal runs until a finish line is true. /batch fans one job across agents. ultracode scales it up. If you learn one, learn /loop.

**Reply 4:**
My real one runs every morning at 7, before I'm awake. It runs my research, drafts the week's short scripts, then stops and shows me. I thumbs-up the good ones with coffee. That's my content for the day.

**Reply 5:**
A safe loop has four parts. It knows the intent. It has something that can say no. It has skills worth calling. And it can always stop. Give it those and you can let it run without babysitting it.

**Reply 6 (CTA):**
You don't need to code. Pick one boring weekly task, get one good result by hand, then wrap it in /loop. Full walkthrough here: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RQ57cUcGDGg
