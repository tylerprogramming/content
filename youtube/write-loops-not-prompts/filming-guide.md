# Filming Guide: Stop Prompting Claude Code. Write Loops Instead.

This is the "do this, click that" version. You can follow this without reading the script.

---

## Pre-Recording Setup

**Environment cleanup**
- [ ] Clean terminal: bump font size so text is readable at 1080p (16-18pt min).
- [ ] Use your dark Claude Code theme. Close unrelated tabs/apps. Hide any private info (emails, client names, API keys).
- [ ] Pre-open Claude Code in your content working directory (`~/content` or wherever your skills live).
- [ ] Have your skills list ready to show but NOT scrolled to anything sensitive.
- [ ] Disable notifications (Do Not Disturb). No Slack/Telegram popups mid-recording.

**Assets to prepare before you hit record**
- [ ] The Boris Cherny quote as a clean text graphic (see below - editor adds it, but know the wording).
- [ ] 2-3 pieces of REAL finished content to show as B-roll (a posted short, a carousel, an email). This is your proof. Critical for Hook 2 if you use it.
- [ ] A screen recording of a `/loop` that has ALREADY run, so you can cut to "here's the output it produced." Record this BEFORE filming the talking parts so you have the payoff footage ready.
- [ ] Optional: the four-command recap card and the four-guardrails card (editor builds, but list them so VO matches).

**Decide your hook before you film**
- Default: Hook 1 (borrowed authority, talking to camera).
- If your finished-content B-roll is strong, also shoot Hook 2 (cold open) and pick in the edit.

**Quote wording to get exactly right (say it cleanly):**
> "I don't prompt Claude anymore. I have loops running that prompt Claude and figuring out what to do. My job is to write loops." - Boris Cherny, Head of Claude Code

## Step 1 - Film the hook (talking head)

**What you do:** Camera only, no screen yet. Deliver Hook 1 from `hooks.md`. Memorize the beats, don't read.

**What you say:** (full text in hooks.md - the key beats)
> "The guy who built Claude Code just said something that flips everything... 'I don't prompt Claude anymore, I write loops.'... 700,000 views in a day... I'm going to show you a real loop that runs my content business while I sleep. No code required."

**Note:** High energy. The first 5 seconds decide retention. Do 3 takes, pick the punchiest.

---

## Step 2 - Film the mental shift (talking head + B-roll cues)

**What you do:** Talk to camera. Editor will drop in the vending-machine and loop graphics over you.

**What you say:** Vending machine vs employee. "You stop being the thing inside the loop. You write the loop once, and the loop does the prompting."

**How to fill dead air:** There's no waiting here, it's all you. Just keep the energy up and let the editor cut to graphics.

**Visual moments to capture:** Gesture when you say "one output" (hold up one finger) vs "a whole system" (open hand). Gives the editor something to cut to.

## Step 3 - Film the proof segment (talking head)

**What you do:** Talk to camera. Editor overlays the New Stack headline + the "8x more output" stat.

**What you say:** "This came from Boris Cherny, he runs Claude Code at Anthropic... the same machine that makes a developer ship 8x more code makes you ship 8x more content."

**Note:** This is the bridge from "dev thing" to "creator thing." Say it with conviction, it's the whole reason your audience should care.

---

## Step 4 - Film the 4 commands (SCREEN RECORDING)

Switch to screen recording. You're demonstrating, not fully executing yet - just show each command being typed so viewers see the syntax.

**4a - /loop**
- Type in Claude Code:
```
/loop
```
- Then show a realistic full example (don't run it yet, just type it so it's visible):
```
/loop every morning at 7am, run my content research skill for this week's topics
```
> Say: "This is the one you start with. Interval plus a task. The task can be one of your own skills."

**4b - /goal**
- Type:
```
/goal keep going until this is true, then stop
```
> Say: "Instead of every morning, you give it a finish line. A second faster model grades the work after every pass."

**4c - /batch**
- Type:
```
/batch write me 5 short scripts from this research
```
> Say: "One instruction, split across a bunch of agents at the same time. This is for volume."

**4d - ultracode**
- Type a normal-looking prompt with the trigger word:
```
ultracode audit all my skill files and find any that overlap
```
> Say: "You just put the word ultracode in your prompt and Claude Code writes its own program to coordinate up to a thousand sub-agents. You won't need this on day one."

**What happens next:** Don't actually let these all execute on camera (ultracode especially can run long). This step is about SHOWING syntax. The real execution is Step 6.

**How to fill dead air:** Narrate the recap as you go: "loop is schedule, goal is until-done, batch is volume, ultracode is scale."

---

## Step 5 - Film the guardrails (talking head)

**What you do:** Talk to camera. Editor builds the 4-item checklist on screen.

**What you say:** "Intent. A way to say no. Skills. A stop. If your loop has those four, you're safe to let it run."

**Note:** This earns trust. It signals you're responsible, not reckless. Don't skip it.

---

## Step 6 - THE HERO DEMO (screen recording) ⭐

This is the most important segment. Best to have recorded the "after" footage in advance (see setup) so you can show real output, not a spinner.

**6a - Set the scene**
- Show your terminal in your content directory.
- Briefly scroll your skills list. Don't dwell, just show the VOLUME. "I have a skill for every repetitive job in my week."

**6b - Write the content loop live**
- Type this for real, reading it out loud as you go:
```
/loop every morning at 7am, run my content research for this week's topics, then draft the short-form scripts from what you find, and show me the drafts before doing anything else
```
> Say: "I didn't write a prompt. I wrote a loop. One time. Now every morning at 7, before I'm awake, this fires."

**6c - Cut to the payoff (pre-recorded)**
- Cut to the loop having already run: show the drafted scripts sitting there waiting.
> Say: "And here's the guardrail. It does NOT post. It does NOT schedule. It stops and shows me. I wake up, read them with my coffee, thumbs up the good ones. Done before breakfast."

**Note:** Sit in this beat. This is the emotional payoff of the whole video. Let it breathe.

**6d - The /goal example**
- Type for real:
```
/goal keep improving this video description until it has a hook in the first line, all chapters filled in, and the right keywords, then stop
```
- Let it iterate if it's quick, OR cut to a pre-recorded run landing on a final version.
> Say: "This one runs until it's right. It writes a version, a second model grades it, not good enough, tries again. Loops on its own until it hits the bar. Then stops."

**How to fill dead air (if a loop runs slow on camera):**
- "While that's working, notice I'm not doing anything. That's the point. I used to be typing the next prompt right now."
- "It's reading my research skill, then it'll hand that to the script skill."
- Or just cut it in the edit. Don't leave a silent spinner.

**If something errors on camera:** Keep it. Narrate it: "See, it hit a snag, and watch, the loop catches it and adjusts. That's the difference, a prompt would've just stopped." Real beats polished. Then cut to a clean take if needed.

## Step 7 - Build your first loop (talking head)

**What you do:** Camera. Editor builds the 3-step card.

**What you say:** "Pick the most boring repetitive thing. Make Claude do it once manually. Then wrap it in a slash loop. That's your first loop." Then the compounding note: "fix it once, it remembers, day 30 is better than day 1."

## Step 8 - Recap + CTA (talking head)

**What you do:** Direct to camera, warm and direct.

**What you say:** Recap the 4 beats. Then: "I went from spending my mornings prompting, to waking up with the work already drafted. Biggest change in how I use AI all year. If you want the actual skills I loop together, watch this one next."

**Visual:** Point to where the end-screen card will go (the "Claude Code for Content Creators" or "100 pieces of content" video).

## On-Camera Tips

- **Energy:** This is a "new meta" video. Urgency sells it. Talk slightly faster than feels natural.
- **The one line that matters most:** "You stop being the thing inside the loop." Land it. Pause after it.
- **Demo > slides.** The hero demo is why this video beats the talking-head competitors. Spend your editing time making that segment crisp.
- **Keep the terminal readable.** If text is tiny, zoom in post. Viewers on phones need to read the commands.
- **Don't fake it.** Use a real loop you actually run. Authenticity is your edge over the dev creators covering this.
- **Pre-record the payoff.** The single biggest filming mistake here would be live-running a slow loop and leaving dead air. Capture the "after" first, cut to it.
