## SECTION 2: THE PROBLEM (1:30 - 3:00)

[SHOW: Stats on screen, simple text overlay]

Here's the reality. Seventy percent of creators spend ten or more hours a week just making content. And most of them are only posting a few times on one or two platforms.

That's not a content strategy. That's a part-time job.

[SHOW: Tyler talking to camera]

I was there. A year ago, I was spending my entire weekend making two YouTube videos and maybe a LinkedIn post. That was it. No shorts. No carousels. No Skool posts.

And the worst part? I knew I should be on more platforms. I just didn't have the hours.

So I started asking a different question. Instead of "how do I make more content," I asked "how do I make the same content work harder?"

[SHOW: Simple diagram appearing on screen - one circle labeled "1 Video Idea" with arrows pointing to multiple platform icons]

What if one video idea could become content for every platform I'm on? Not by cross-posting the same thing everywhere. But by actually reformatting it for each platform.

That's what this system does.

[NOTE: Keep this section tight. The problem should feel personal, not preachy. Tyler lived this.]

---

## SECTION 3: SYSTEM OVERVIEW (3:00 - 5:00)

[SHOW: Pipeline diagram on screen, building piece by piece as Tyler explains each step]

Let me give you the bird's eye view before we dive into each piece.

The pipeline has six stages.

Stage one, research. I find what's already working on YouTube so I'm not guessing at topics.

[SHOW: Arrow from Research to Stage 2]

Stage two, planning. I take that research and build a complete video package. Titles, hooks, a full script, a filming guide.

Stage three, filming. This is the only part that's actually me on camera. Everything else is automated.

Stage four, content generation. One command and I get LinkedIn posts, Instagram carousels, X threads, Skool posts, short-form scripts. All platform-specific.

Stage five, thumbnails and visuals. AI-generated thumbnails and carousel images.

Stage six, scheduling. Everything gets queued up in one tool and it posts throughout the week.

[SHOW: Full pipeline diagram now visible]

Now here's the key. I only do original thinking in stages one, two, and three. That's the research, the planning, and the filming.

Stages four, five, and six? That's reformatting. That's taking my ideas and putting them into the right shape for each platform.

And reformatting is exactly what AI is good at.

[NOTE: This is the philosophical core of the video. The distinction between thinking and reformatting is what separates this from "AI slop." Hit it clearly but don't belabor it.]

## SECTION 4: LIVE DEMO - RESEARCH (5:00 - 8:00)

[SHOW: Tyler's screen, terminal open]

Alright, let's do this live. I'm going to build a real content week from scratch, right now.

It all starts with research. I type one command.

[SHOW: Terminal - type `/yt-search content repurposing AI`]

This searches YouTube for the top-performing videos on whatever topic I give it. It pulls view counts, engagement, titles, what's working.

[SHOW: Results appearing in terminal, then the saved research file]

In about thirty seconds, I have a complete competitive analysis. What videos are getting views, what titles are performing, what angles haven't been covered yet.

[NOTE: While results load, talk through what it's doing: "It's pulling the top results, sorting by views, and looking at what's actually getting traction."]

Now I pick the two or three best reference videos and transcribe them.

[SHOW: Terminal - type `/transcribe https://youtube.com/watch?v=example`]

This downloads the audio, transcribes it with Whisper, and saves the full text. Takes about a minute.

Why do I do this? Because the best way to make a great video is to study great videos. I want to know their structure, their hooks, their pacing. Not to copy, but to learn what the audience responds to.

[SHOW: Transcript file opening briefly, then close]

So now I have research and reference material. Stage one, done. That took maybe five minutes total.

[NOTE: Keep the terminal visible but don't linger on it. The output matters more than the command.]

## SECTION 5: LIVE DEMO - VIDEO PACKAGE (8:00 - 12:00)

[SHOW: Tyler at desk]

Stage two is where the magic happens. I take everything from the research phase and feed it into one command that builds the entire video package.

[SHOW: Terminal - type `/yt` and paste context about the topic]

This does a few things. It analyzes the reference transcripts. It does its own web research to find stats and angles. Then it generates titles, hooks, a full script, an SEO-optimized description, and a filming guide.

[SHOW: Files appearing in the directory - ls output showing analysis.md, titles.md, hooks.md, script.md, description.md, filming-guide.md]

Let me show you what comes out.

[SHOW: Open titles.md]

Five title options, each under seventy characters, with an explanation of why it works and which one is recommended.

[SHOW: Open hooks.md]

Three different hook options. Word for word, what to say in the first thirty seconds. Different techniques for each one.

[SHOW: Open script.md, scroll through]

A complete script with timestamp estimates, what to say, what to show on screen, and production notes. This isn't some generic outline. It's a real script based on the research we just did.

[SHOW: Open filming-guide.md]

And this is my favorite part. A filming guide that tells me exactly what to have ready, what commands to run on screen, and how to handle the dead air while AI processes.

So from one command, I have everything I need to film a video. No staring at a blank page. No wondering what to say next.

[SHOW: Tyler talking to camera]

Now, is the script perfect? No. I always adjust it. I change lines that don't sound like me. I add personal stories. I cut sections that feel like filler.

But it's a thousand times easier to edit something than to create from nothing. This turns a four-hour writing session into a thirty-minute editing session.

[NOTE: This is critical. Tyler must emphasize that he edits and personalizes. This is not "press button, get video." Show a quick example of a line in the script vs. how Tyler would actually say it.]

I use Kie.ai to turn those into actual designed slides. Light theme for Instagram. Dark theme for LinkedIn carousel PDFs.

Same information, completely different visual format. That's what platform-specific means.

X threads.

YouTube Shorts and TikTok scripts.

The last piece is getting all of this scheduled so I don't have to think about it for the rest of the week.

[SHOW: Scheduling posts in Blotato, quick walkthrough]

LinkedIn gets a post every single day. Two of those are tied to the video releases. The other five are standalone posts on the same topics.

Instagram carousels go out four times a week. Two from the video packages, two from the shorts package.

[SHOW: Calendar view with everything scheduled]

Total time from research to fully scheduled? About three hours. Maybe three and a half if I'm really dialing in the scripts.

## SECTION 9: ADDRESSING AI SLOP (20:00 - 22:00)

[SHOW: Tyler at desk, more serious tone]

Now let me talk about the elephant in the room. AI content.

I know what some of you are thinking. "Tyler, this is just AI slop at scale."

Here's why it's not.

[SHOW: Text on screen - "The Centaur Model: Human Direction + Machine Speed"]

There's this concept called the centaur model. Half human, half machine. The human does the thinking. The machine does the executing.

In my pipeline, the thinking is: what topic matters, what angle is unique, what's my actual take on this, what stories do I tell on camera.

The machine handles: reformatting that take for LinkedIn's format, reformatting it for Instagram's carousel format, reformatting it for X's character limit.

[SHOW: Diagram - "Tyler's Brain" pointing to original ideas, "AI Pipeline" pointing to platform formatting]

The AI never decides what I say. It decides how to format what I've already said for each platform.

That's the difference.

[SHOW: Tyler at desk]

And the data backs this up. Only twenty-six percent of people say they prefer AI-generated content. That number used to be sixty percent. People can tell when AI is doing the thinking.

But they can't tell when AI is doing the reformatting. Because the ideas, the personality, the actual substance? That's still me.

I handle the thinking. AI handles the reformatting. That's it.

[NOTE: This is the most important section for credibility. Tyler should be genuine here. Not defensive, just clear. This is the section that separates this video from every other "AI content" video.]
