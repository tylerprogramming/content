## Short 1 - "2 hours of YouTube research in 60 seconds"

- **Source timestamp:** 1:45 - 7:30 (Skill 1, `/yt-search`)
- **Format:** Screen recording (terminal + report + thumbnails folder) with voiceover, brief talking-head bookends
- **Length:** ~45 sec

**Hook (first 3 sec):**
> Researching YouTube used to eat 2 hours of my week. Now it's one command.

**Script:**
> Every Monday I ask one question before I make anything: what's actually working in my space right now? If you skip that, you're guessing, and guessing is how channels die.
>
> So I type `/yt-search claude code`. That's the whole thing. It pulls every video in the last 30 days, sorts by views, downloads the thumbnails, and writes me a report.
>
> Sixty seconds later: 33 videos, ranked. I can see what titles are hitting, who's making them, what lengths work. The thumbnails are already downloaded so I can see what's working visually too.
>
> Two hours of clicking around YouTube, gone. This is the research that feeds everything else I build.

**Text overlays:**
- "2 hrs → 60 seconds"
- "/yt-search claude code"
- "33 videos, ranked by views"

## Short 2 - "I don't watch my competitors. I read them."

- **Source timestamp:** 7:30 - 13:30 (Skill 2, `/transcribe`)
- **Format:** Screen recording (transcribe running + transcript + Claude breakdown), talking-head hook
- **Length:** ~45 sec

**Hook (first 3 sec):**
> Everyone watches their competitors. I read mine. I get through five in the time it takes you to watch one.

**Script:**
> Watching a 30-minute video to take notes is a waste of time when you can just read it.
>
> I paste any YouTube URL into `/transcribe`. It pulls the audio, runs it through Whisper, and saves the full transcript with timestamps. About 90 seconds for a 12-minute video.
>
> Then I tell Claude: read this and give me the hook, the three main beats, and the CTA. Thirty seconds of reading and I have the whole structure. I can riff on it, beat it, or steal the framing.
>
> That's how I plan a video without ever watching the competition.

**Text overlays:**
- "Read, don't watch"
- "/transcribe <url>"
- "5 competitors in the time it takes to watch 1"

## Short 3 - "A full YouTube video package in 5 minutes"

- **Source timestamp:** 13:30 - 21:30 (Skill 3, `/yt`)
- **Format:** Screen recording (package folder filling up + opening files), talking-head bookends
- **Length:** ~50 sec

**Hook (first 3 sec):**
> I never start a video with a blank doc. Five minutes and I have the entire package.

**Script:**
> Most people sit down to make a video and stare at an empty page. I point `/yt` at a transcript and let it work.
>
> First it asks me one thing: what's my angle? My take, the unique slot I'm filling. If I skip that, the script sounds like everyone else's.
>
> Then watch the folder fill. Titles, scored. Four hooks to test. A full script, scene by scene. A description. A filming guide with b-roll notes and energy cues for my editor.
>
> This used to be my entire weekend. Now it's five minutes. And it isn't writing the video for me, it's writing the 80 percent draft I edit and make mine. I just never start from zero.

**Text overlays:**
- "Blank doc → full package"
- "/yt <transcript>"
- "A weekend of work → 5 minutes"

## Short 4 - "I built a Claude skill from scratch in 5 minutes"

- **Source timestamp:** 21:30 - 29:30 (The AHA + live build of `/meeting-notes`)
- **Format:** Screen recording (SKILL.md typing + restart + the command firing), talking-head hook
- **Length:** ~55 sec

**Hook (first 3 sec):**
> Every Claude Code skill I have is just a markdown file. Let me prove it. I'll build one right now.

**Script:**
> No SDK. No framework. Nothing to install. A skill is about 50 lines of plain English in a file called SKILL.md.
>
> Here's an empty folder. I want a `/meeting-notes` command that builds a meeting template and saves it. Top of the file is the frontmatter: the name, a description that tells Claude when to fire it, and the tools it's allowed to use.
>
> Below that, plain English. Ask me what the meeting's about. Get today's date. Build the agenda, decisions, action items. Save it to a predictable folder. And a rules section so it never overwrites a file.
>
> Save, restart, type `/meeting-notes`. It asks me the question, I pick "Team sync," and there's the file. Exactly what I described, in plain English.
>
> Once you see that it's just a markdown file, the floodgates open.

**Text overlays:**
- "It's just a markdown file"
- "~50 lines. No code."
- "Built live in 5 min"
