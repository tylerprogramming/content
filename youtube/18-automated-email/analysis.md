# Analysis - I Automated My Email With Claude Code

## The bet
This video rides the single best-performing lane on Tyler's channel: the honest "I automated my ___ with Claude Code" showcase. The proof is the reference video, "I Automated My Entire YouTube Workflow with Claude Code" (2,854 views, +42 subs, the #1 cohort video). That video worked because it showed a real automation running on Tyler's own work, explained exactly how it was built, and then pointed at the opportunity for the viewer. This video copies that formula move for move, just swapped to a new, higher-frequency pain: email.

Email is a stronger topic than the YouTube workflow in one important way: everyone has an inbox. The YouTube video only spoke to creators. This one speaks to creators, freelancers, agency owners, consultants, and any solopreneur drowning in email. The pain is universal, the demo is instantly legible (an inbox everyone recognizes going from chaos to triaged), and the business angle is obvious because inbox triage and outbound email are things companies already pay real money for.

## Why this lane works
- Proof over promises. The channel's edge is that Tyler shows the real thing running, not a theory. The reference video's retention came from watching an actual automation execute on his actual channel. We do the same: real Gmail inbox, real Claude Code session, real send.
- Legible transformation. "Chaotic inbox to triaged inbox" is a visual anyone understands in two seconds. That is a strong cold open and a strong thumbnail.
- Copyable system. The middle of the video is a build people can replicate. That is what earns the subscribe and the click to the free pack. Viewers subscribe to channels that hand them a system, not a highlight reel.
- Clear opportunity ramp. Inbox triage plus outbound email is a service businesses buy. The closing layer ("you could build this for a client") turns a personal demo into a reason to keep watching Tyler.

1. Showcase - "I don't really do my email anymore. Claude Code triages it." Show it running live on a real inbox.
2. System - "Here is exactly how, and you can copy it." The Gmail skill (read/search/summarize via the Gmail API + OAuth), the Resend email skill (send one-offs, blasts, drip/welcome campaigns, contact sync, daily cron), and a Claude Routine that runs triage on a schedule.
3. Opportunity - "You could build this for a business or a client. Inbox triage and outbound is something companies pay for." Soft, honest, no income claims.

## The differentiator
Most "AI email" videos on YouTube are one of two things: a thin wrapper demo of a paid SaaS tool (Superhuman, Shortwave, Fyxer, and the rest), or a hand-wavy "AI will read your email" concept video with no real build. Tyler's differentiator is that this is a build you own, running in Claude Code, wired to the actual Gmail and Resend APIs with your own OAuth and keys. Nothing is a black box, nothing is a subscription you rent, and every piece is shown on screen. That is the honest-builder position the channel already owns.

Second differentiator: the routine. Most AI-email content stops at "ask the AI to read your inbox." Tyler shows the next step, a Claude Routine that runs the triage on a schedule so it happens without him even opening the laptop. That is the payoff most competitors never reach, and Tyler already made a Short about it ("The Email Triage Routine That Saves Me Hours Every Week"), so it is proven and real.

## Honest-framing guardrails (non-negotiable)
- Draw the line clearly. Claude Code is the operator and the brain. The Gmail API is what actually reads and searches the inbox. Resend is what actually sends the mail. Say this out loud in the video so nobody thinks Claude Code magically has email powers. This honesty is the brand.
- No fake money. No "this made me $X," no invented client revenue, no fabricated results. The opportunity layer is framed as "this is a service companies pay for," not "here is the money I made." If a number is not real and verifiable, it does not go in.
- No overclaiming automation. Be honest that Tyler still reviews drafts before they send and still makes the final call on important replies. The automation triages and drafts. A human approves. Say so.
- Privacy on camera (critical). The demo is a real inbox, which means real private content. Before filming:
  - Use a demo or secondary inbox if possible, or pre-clean the account of sensitive threads.
  - Blur or crop sender names, email addresses, subject lines, and any private body content. Plan the blur in the edit, and frame shots so private text is off-screen where possible.
  - Never show API tokens, OAuth client secrets, .env files, credentials.json, or token.json on screen. Keep terminal scrollback clean. Close any tab or file that could leak a secret.
  - Do not read a real person's private email aloud. Use clearly non-sensitive threads (newsletters, your own test sends, sponsor logistics you are cleared to show) for the visible demo.
  - Add an on-screen note and a spoken line: "I blurred anything private. Never show real inboxes, tokens, or secrets on camera."
- Do not teach spam. The outbound section is framed around a real, permission-based list (people who opted into the newsletter), not cold-blasting strangers. Keep it clean so the video and the brand stay clean.
