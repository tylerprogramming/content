Most tutorials rush setup and that is exactly why people get a worse experience than they should. Cowork's whole power comes from what it can see and remember, and you decide that in the first five minutes. Get this right once and everything else in the course just works.

### What you need

You need two things. A paid Claude plan, and the Claude desktop app. Cowork lives in the desktop app, not the website. That is the number one point of confusion, so let me say it clearly. You cannot do this from claude.ai in your browser. You need the app installed on your Mac or Windows machine.

### Steps - follow along

[SHOW: Each step on screen as you say it]

Step 2. Open the app. Along the top you will see tabs. Chat, Cowork, and Code. Click Cowork. This is the workspace we are living in for the rest of the video.

Step 4. Point Cowork at that folder. When you start, it will ask which folder to work in, or you will see an option to add a working directory. Choose the Cowork folder you just made. This is the safety boundary. Cowork can only touch the folders you explicitly give it. It cannot wander into the rest of your computer.

Step 5. Take the quick tour. On the main screen you have the chat box in the middle where you type what you want. ## MODULE 3: The Memory System - Train Your AI Employee (19:00 - 28:00) ~1,150 words

### Why this is the most important module in the course

Everything so far, you had to explain yourself each time. This module fixes that forever. This is the difference between a tool you use and an employee that knows you. If you only take one thing from this whole video, take this.

### The two files that change everything

[SHOW: The Cowork folder with two files highlighted - a CLAUDE.md and a memory file]

There are two pieces to memory.

The first is your instructions file, usually called CLAUDE.md. This is where you write the things that are true every single time. Who you are, what your business is, how you like things formatted. Think of it as the employee handbook you hand over on day one.

The second is the memory the assistant builds over time. As you correct it and work with it, it saves what it learns about you. Your writing voice, your preferences, your recurring context. You do not write this one by hand, it grows on its own from your feedback.

### Steps - set up your instructions file right now

Step 1. Ask Cowork to create it for you. You do not have to write it from a blank page.

[SHOW: Typing the prompt]

Prompt: "Create a CLAUDE.md file in this folder. Interview me with five questions about my business, my audience, and how I like documents formatted, then write the file based on my answers."

[SHOW: Cowork asking the questions, Tyler answering briefly, the finished file appearing]

Step 2. Read it and tighten it. Keep it short and specific. Good lines look like: "I run a YouTube channel about AI tools. My audience is business owners, not developers. I always want reports in markdown with the recommendation at the top. I never use dollar amounts in titles."

Step 3. Test that it is working. Ask for a report and see if it follows your rules without being reminded.

[SHOW: A report coming back already formatted the way the instructions specified]

### Demo 3: Watch memory do the work

[SHOW: Asking for an email draft]

Here is the proof. I am going to ask it to draft a reply to a customer, and I have never told it my email style in this session.

Prompt: "Draft a reply to this email thanking them and offering a call next week."

[SHOW: The draft coming back short, in Tyler's voice, signing off the way he always does]

See how it is short, it sounds like me, and it signed off the way I always do? I never said any of that just now. It knew, because over the last month I corrected it enough times that it saved my style. That is the compounding effect. The employee you train in week one is far better in week four.

### The one rule for memory

Correct, do not redo. Every time you fix Cowork's output instead of fixing it yourself, you are paying into an account that pays you back forever. People who redo the work manually never get the compounding. People who give feedback get an assistant that eventually needs almost no instruction.

### Module 3 recap

[SHOW: Recap card]

CLAUDE.md is the handbook you write. Memory is what it learns from your feedback. Set up the handbook, then correct instead of redo, and Cowork gets measurably better every week. Now let's point that trained employee at real analysis.

## MODULE 7: Skills and Plugins - Make Your Best Work Reusable (52:00 - 58:00) ~850 words

### What you will learn

By now you have a few prompts you keep reusing. Skills are how you save a workflow once and trigger it any time, so you are not retyping a paragraph-long prompt every week. This is how you go from doing tasks to having a system.

### The simplest way to think about it

A skill is a saved process. You do a task well a few times, then you tell Cowork to remember how you did it so you can call it with one short command later. The best part is you do not build a skill from scratch. You build it from work you already did.

### Steps - turn a workflow you already do into a skill

Step 1. Do the task manually a few times until the output is consistently good. Do not automate something that is not working yet.

Step 2. Ask Cowork to capture it.

[SHOW: Typing the prompt]

Prompt: "We have done this weekly report the same way three times now. Save this as a reusable skill called weekly-report so I can run it with one command. Write down the steps, the format, and my preferences."

[SHOW: The skill being created]

Step 3. Run it by name next time. Instead of a long prompt, you just say "run my weekly-report skill," and it follows the saved process.

[SHOW: Triggering the saved skill, the report coming out the same as before]

### Plugins and the bigger picture

[SHOW: The plugins or extensions area]

Beyond skills you build yourself, there are plugins that add ready-made capabilities to Cowork. Browse what is available, add what fits your business, and skip the rest. The principle is the same as everything in this course: start with what you actually do every week, and only add the machinery that removes real, repeated work.
