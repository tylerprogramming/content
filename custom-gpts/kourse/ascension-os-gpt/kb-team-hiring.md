## When to Start Building a Team

Max scaled his online course to **$55,000/month completely alone**. By the time he hit that number, he was wearing every hat — building products, managing communities, handling all tech and funnels, doing all traffic, taking all sales calls, running all operations. Everything.

At $55K/month, it started to hurt. That's when he learned to hire.

**Looking back: he should have started earlier.**

The lesson: **you should really learn how to hire before it becomes a crisis.** When the pain hits, you want to already know exactly what to do.

### The Signal: Hire Where It Hurts

From Sam Ovens at the first School Games event: *"You should start to hire when it starts to hurt, and you should hire where it hurts."*

**Realistic solo ceiling:** You can get to roughly $30–55K/month by yourself. Beyond that, your time, attention, and energy become the limiting factor. You become the bottleneck.

**Signs it's time:**
- You're doing tasks that shouldn't require you (admin, editing, support)
- New customers are coming in faster than you can handle them
- Product quality is suffering because you're stretched thin
- You're burning out trying to keep up
- You can't find time to think, strategize, or work on the next thing

> "If you want to go fast, go alone. If you want to go far, go together."

## What Positions to Hire First

A small, focused team removes low-leverage work so you can stay focused on **traffic, product, and strategy** — the three things that actually move the needle as the founder.

### 1. Community Manager (First Hire)

**What they do:**
- Manage community admin, member support, and moderation
- Keep the group running smoothly without you
- Handle day-to-day operations inside Skool
- Can expand into tech support, promotions, and even some sales

**What to look for:** A T-shaped person — good at many things, exceptional at one. Someone who understands your product, fits your culture, and can represent you in the community.

### 2. Sales Rep / Sales Manager (Hire Early)

**What they do:**
- Take sales calls, manage follow-ups
- As the team grows: oversee setters and closers, manage the full sales function

**Why this is critical:** This is arguably the most important department in the business. You focus on traffic and promotions to generate booked calls — and then a conversion machine runs the sales for you. With the right commission structure, you make more money, your reps make good money, and the business scales.

Miles (Max's sales manager) had been on thousands of calls and was closing 70%+ some months. That level of expertise cannot be faked. Find someone with real skill, structure the incentives correctly, and you've built a machine.

**The goal:** A sales department that runs without you. You shouldn't be on sales calls when you're also running traffic, improving the product, and strategizing.

### 3. Video Editor / Creative Director

**What they do:**
- Editor: handles all video editing for YouTube and other content
- Creative director: handles ideation, titles, thumbnails, packaging, scripting, analytics, testing

**Why this matters:** When you have a creative director who handles everything around YouTube, all you need to do is show up, have the idea, work on the hook and script, and record. Everything else is handled.

**The outcome:** You can focus on being the face and brain of the content without being buried in production.

### Future Hires (as traffic grows)
- More setters and closers to handle increased call volume
- Copywriter for email newsletters and list engagement
- Media buyer for paid ads management

## Why Build a Team at All

### The Bottleneck Problem

At some point, you ARE the bottleneck. You can't scale a business by wearing every hat. The math doesn't work.

Things you shouldn't be spending your time on after a certain stage:
- Editing videos
- Managing community support tickets
- Taking every single sales call
- Handling all tech issues
- Running every piece of admin

These are important — they just shouldn't be done by you.

### The Leverage Question

> "Would you rather own 100% of a $500K business or 70% of a $10M business?"

The more talented people you bring in with aligned incentives, the bigger the business gets — and the higher your profits grow in absolute terms, even if your percentage shrinks slightly.

### Sustainable Growth

Hiring done right allows you to:
- Scale revenue without burnout
- Take a couple of days off without the business dying
- Focus on what only you can do — traffic, product, vision, strategy
- Build something that doesn't require your constant presence to function

> "You might be afraid that profits will go down. They won't — when you set up incentives and contracts correctly, the more the business grows, the more everyone gets paid, including you."

## Who to Hire and How

### Finding the Right People

Look for people who:
- Believe in you and your company and your vision
- Are genuinely talented — true killers who work as hard as you do
- Fit your culture (don't underestimate this — culture is hard to repair)
- Are T-shaped (broad skills + deep expertise in one area)

**Signs of the right hire:**
- They're self-motivated — they find a way to get things done
- They're organized in their personal life (how you do one thing is how you do everything)
- They care about the outcome, not just the task

### Getting Their Buy-In

How do you get people so invested in your company that they show up on Saturdays because they want to?

It comes down to:
1. **Culture** — a shared belief in what you're building and why
2. **Incentives** — commission structures that reward performance (everyone wins when the business grows)
3. **Leadership** — lead by example; people follow energy and results, not titles
4. **Opportunity** — talented people stay when they see they can grow and thrive

At Acquisition HQ, Alex Hormozi said: "Pretty much everyone upstairs is working right now — on a Saturday — and they don't have to be." That's what the right culture creates.

### Contracts & Compensation

This was a major question mark for Max when he started. Key principles:

- **Commission structures** — protect the business from fixed overhead during growth phases; tie pay to performance
- **Clear agreements** — set expectations upfront on roles, responsibilities, deliverables
- **Protect the business** — don't create a huge overhead that burns profits; especially critical in early stages
- **Make it worth their while** — the people working with you should see real earning potential

> "It took me a lot of time to figure this out. I invested $28,000 into a mastermind specifically to learn this from someone who'd built a big, profitable digital course business."

This is exactly what's covered inside the Kourse Mastermind — contracts, payment structures, incentives, all of it.

### From Solopreneur to Leader

Going from doing everything yourself to managing a team is a real transition. Things to develop:

- **Lead by example** — your team watches how hard you work; why would they outwork you if you're not outworking yourself?
- **Clarity of vision** — communicate where the business is going and why
- **Delegation without losing quality** — trust your people, but build systems so quality stays high
- **Give feedback** — a great team thrives on clear, direct, respectful feedback
- **Protect the culture** — don't let the wrong person in; one bad hire can damage an entire team environment

### Performance Tracking for Teams

As the team grows, the performance tracker grows with it. You'll need:
- Metrics for every individual on the sales team (calls taken, show rate, close rate, contracted value, cash collected)
- An overview dashboard that shows the whole team's performance at a glance
- Regular reviews to identify what's working and what needs adjustment

Max and his team built a custom software backend to track all their reps' metrics in one place (graduated from Google Sheets to purpose-built software).

## Summary: When, Why, Who, and How

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **When to hire** | When it starts to hurt, around $30–55K/month solo. Learn before then. |
| **Why to hire** | Remove bottlenecks, focus on high-leverage work, protect your energy |
| **First hire** | Community manager |
| **Second hire** | Sales rep / sales manager |
| **Third hire** | Video editor / creative director |
| **How to hire** | Find T-shaped people, align incentives, protect culture, lead by example |
| **What to learn** | Contracts, compensation, onboarding, management, performance tracking |

> "The sooner you can learn about hiring, the better. When you get there, you want to know exactly what to do — not be figuring it out while you're drowning."
