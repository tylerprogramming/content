## Video 5: Payments + Tracking

### Taking Payments

**99% of you should just take payment through Skool.**

Skool's fees are as low or lower than Stripe. It's the simplest method and has additional benefits (you show up on the Skool revenue leaderboard as you grow, which generates more leads organically).

#### Method 1: About Page (Pay in Full — Annual)

For most full-price sales:

1. Settings → Pricing → Set an annual subscription price (e.g., $3,000/year)
2. Click Save
3. Copy the community URL
4. Send the link in the Zoom/Google Meet chat while you're on the call with them
5. They pay, they're in

This is the go-to method. Works for 99% of sales.

#### Method 2: Classroom (One-Time or Edge Cases)

If the about page method has a bug or you need to take a one-time payment:

1. Create a new Classroom
2. Name it (e.g., "Derek — Course Access")
3. Set a one-time price
4. Invite them by email for free
5. Send them the classroom URL — they purchase from there

**More useful for upsells:** Existing paid members who can't pay through the about page can pay through a classroom. They need to already be in the community first.

#### Method 3: Payment Plans

When someone can't pay in full, use payment plans:

1. Settings → Pricing → Switch from Annual to Monthly
2. Set the monthly price (e.g., $1,000/month for a 3-month plan = $3,000 total)
3. Skool auto-bills them each month — you don't have to chase
4. After the final payment is collected:
   - Go to Members → find them → Membership → Cancel Subscription
   - Copy their email
   - Reinvite them for free via the Invite button
   - They stay in the community indefinitely

**Track payment plan members** in your tracking sheet so you don't forget to cancel and reinvite.

#### BNPL (Buy Now, Pay Later)

Options: Klarna, Afterpay, Affirm, Sezzle

**The only way this works:** Klarna through Stripe.

- Create a separate Stripe account
- Enable Klarna in your Stripe payment links
- Customer can pay monthly over 12, 24, or 36 months — Klarna pays you in full (minus ~8–9% fee)
- **Limitation: only works within the same country** (e.g., US company selling to US buyers)

BNPL is complex to set up and has limited use cases. For most people, the Skool payment plan method is easier and sufficient.

### When to Take Payment

**Always on the call.** Never send a payment link after the call and wait for them to complete it. It rarely happens.

When they say yes: *"Do you need to run and grab your card?"*

This signals naturally that payment happens right now. If they push back on paying immediately, that's an objection to handle (covered in the Closing video).

### Recording Calls

**Always record your calls.** It's critical for:
- Follow-up reference
- Reviewing where objections come up
- Improving your script over time
- Training future sales reps

**Recommended tool: Fathom AI**
- Free to use
- Joins your Zoom/Meet call as an AI note-taker
- Provides a recording + summary + transcript after each call
- You can dump transcripts into Course AI to analyze patterns (where are objections appearing? what questions work best?)
- Drop the Fathom link into your tracking sheet after each call

### Contracts

**Use contracts for every sale.** They:
- Help you win disputes and chargeback cases
- Make your product feel more legitimate and premium
- Set clear expectations for what's included and for how long
- Differentiate you from the dozens of other communities that don't have them

#### Setting Up

1. Download the template: **course.com/contract-template**
2. File → Make a Copy
3. Fill in the bolded variables:
   - Company name
   - Program name
   - Program deliverables (training modules, Q&A calls, community access, AI, etc.)
   - Annual renewal amount
4. Unbold the filled text
5. File → Download → PDF

#### Sending via GoHighLevel

1. GHL → Payments → Documents & Contracts → Templates
2. New → Upload Existing PDF
3. Upload your PDF
4. Add three fields for the prospect to complete: Name (text), Signature, Date
5. Add recipient → select the contact → send

**When to send:** Right after they make payment on the call.

If they don't sign immediately, follow up. It's important.

## Video 6: Sales Script

### Why Use a Sales Script

1. **Consistency** — every call follows the same structure. You know what to say next. You're not freestyling.
2. **Iteration** — if you're not closing, you can identify which variable to tweak. If you're freestyling, you can't isolate what's working.
3. **Scalability** — when you bring in sales reps, you hand them a proven system. They don't need to invent it. Kourse's team close rate went from **30% → 55%** purely by enforcing the script.

> "We took the team from 30% to 55% team-wide across the board in a few weeks by using this script. It's very teachable."

---

### Sales Framework (The Stages)

Every stage has an objective — it's a checklist you're completing, not a conversation you're having.

| Stage | Objective |
|-------|-----------|
| **Introduction** | Build rapport, set a professional and relaxed tone |
| **Setting the Frame** | Drop their shoulders, prevent them from being defensive |
| **Pre-Discovery** | Check how familiar they are with you, confirm VSL alignment |
| **Discovery** | Uncover current state, desired state, obstacles, and commitment |
| **Statement of Confidence** | Transition from discovery to presentation with permission |
| **Presentation** | Bridge their gap with your solution, keep them engaged |
| **Pre-Close** | Let them sell themselves before you ask for money |
| **Closing** | Ask for the sale with stacked conditions |

### Question Wrapping

**What it is:** Masking a standard sales question with a reason or joke so it doesn't sound scripted.

**Why it matters:** Everyone you speak to has been on sales calls before. If you ask questions like everyone else, they feel sales resistance immediately and don't tell you the truth. Question wrapping makes calls feel like real conversations.

**The principle:** Wrap a question with:
- A reason why you're asking it
- Context that makes it feel natural
- A joke or self-aware comment that breaks the scripted feeling
- An act of "thinking" (pretend you're working it out in real time)

#### Examples

**Standard:** *"What's your goal?"*

**Wrapped:** *"Look, I know this is a bit of a generic question — I speak to some people who want to make $3,000 a month and some people who want to make $100,000 a month. So I'm just curious, for you, what are you actually aiming at with this project?"*

Someone told Miles "what a great question" when he asked this — because it didn't sound like a question they'd heard a thousand times before.

**Standard:** *"How long has this been a problem?"*

**Wrapped:** *"Look, I speak to a lot of people and they normally say the same sort of thing — they've been stuck on this for anywhere from like 2 to 5 years. Would you say that's kind of the same for yourself?"*

**Standard:** *"Do you have the time to commit to this?"*

**Wrapped (from the script):** *"Look, a lot of our students get great results, but there's obviously been people who've joined and didn't quite get where they wanted to go. The key difference between the two is that the people who get results have the time and they make it a priority. So I like to be pretty upfront on these calls — is this currently a priority for you, and do you have a couple of hours a day to work on this?"*

**Acting as if you're thinking:**
In the pre-close, say: *"What was the goal again? You said… 10K a month, right?"* — even though you wrote it down. It sounds more human than reciting it back mechanically.

> "There's no script for question wrapping. You're just fluffing up questions and giving a reason why you're asking. Do the reps and it becomes natural."

### The Sales Script (Full Walk-Through)

Download: **course.com/sales-framework**

#### Introduction

*"Hey, how's it going? Can you hear me? Can you see me?"*

Get permission to record: *"Hey, quick question — is it cool if my AI joins the call? I can shoot you a copy after."*

If no: remove Fathom, move on.

*"Awesome. Where are you calling in from?"* → brief chit-chat, call out something in the background, mention you saw their form answers, make a joke.

**Booked and busy opener:** When they ask "how are you doing?": *"Honestly, it's super busy — we're back-to-back all day on these calls. Can't complain though, it's what we signed up for. Really excited to chat with you."*

---

#### Setting the Frame

*"Okay, [Name], let's jump into it. By the way, these calls are super chill — the goal is just to see if what we've got can help you out. If it's a good fit, I have a quick presentation for you. If not, I've got a bunch of free stuff I can send you. Sound good?"*

**Why this works:** You're telling them there's no pressure. They drop their shoulders. They won't feel trapped into saying yes or no — which means they'll actually tell you the truth when you dig into their situation.

**Do NOT say:** *"By the end of this call, I need you to tell me yes or no — don't say maybe."* This instantly raises their defenses.

---

#### Pre-Discovery

*"Cool. Hey, before we get into it — I'm curious, how familiar are you with us?"*

If they watched the VSL: *"And did you align with [unique mechanism]? Like the annual high-ticket model?"*

If not: watch the VSL together live. Ask: *"Does that all make sense? Is that something you're interested in, or not really your thing?"*

This pre-handles a huge potential objection (wanting to do monthly instead of annual) before you ever pitch.

---

#### Discovery (6 Core Questions)

**1. Current situation:**
*"Tell me about what you're looking to do."* Then: *"And where are you currently at with this? Is this more of an idea, or do you have something built out?"*

**2. Goal (wrapped):**
*"Look, I know it's a bit of a generic question — I speak to a lot of people and some want to make $3K/month, some want $100K/month. So I'm just curious — what are you actually aiming at with this?"*

If the goal is vague: *"Got it — and after you've done that, what would you like to be making? Like, long-term, what's the dream?"*

Non-financial niche: Tie the goal to something tangible — promotion, relationships, health outcome, etc.

**3. What they feel they need:**
*"In terms of getting to [goal] — what do you feel like you need to actually do that? Like, what kind of support would make the difference for you?"*

If they say "I just need to take action": *"Totally. But what do you feel like you need help with specifically?"*

This tells you what to emphasize in your presentation.

**4. Prior investment:**
*"Have you ever invested in a program like this before? Is this the first time you're looking for outside help?"*

If yes: *"Okay — considering you've been around the block a bit, just to save us some time: what specifically would you be looking for in this to make it worthwhile for you?"* (They tell you their buying criteria.)

**5. Why invest vs. figuring it out alone (CRITICAL — objection pre-handle):**
*"Cool. Last couple of quick questions. I mean, there's plenty of free stuff out there — people giving away free information on YouTube. So I'm curious, why are you looking into more advanced training and mentorships like ourselves rather than just figuring it out on your own?"*

**What you're doing:** Letting them sell themselves on why they want to pay. The answer you're waiting for: "I want to fast-track, I don't want to waste another year figuring it out alone." Once they say this, the investment objection at the end becomes very hard to maintain.

You can frame it explicitly: *"The way I look at it — are you paying time, or are you paying money? You could figure this out in 12–18 months on your own, or you could jump into a proven system and get there in 90 days."*

**6. Time and priority (CRITICAL — pre-handle):**
*"Look, a lot of our students get great results, but there's been people who've joined and didn't quite get there. The key difference? The people who get results make it a priority and have a couple hours a day to work on it. So I like to be pretty upfront: is this currently a priority for you, and do you have a couple of hours a day to work on this?"*

Why this works: It's almost impossible to say no without disqualifying yourself. People rarely say they don't have two hours or it's not a priority. And by answering yes, they've committed to a version of "I'm ready to move forward." The time objection at the end becomes nearly impossible.

---

#### Confirmation + Recap

Before moving to the statement of confidence, play back what you heard:

*"Okay, just so I've understood correctly — you're looking to build a community in the [niche], you're currently in a 9-to-5 that you want to leave. Your goal is $5K/month so you can ditch the job. You consider it a priority and you're open to investing if it's the right fit. Did I miss anything?"*

They say no → you've proven you were listening. They feel seen. Trust goes up.

---

#### Statement of Confidence

*"From what you've told me, you seem very serious. I'm confident we can help you out. Do you have any questions, or should I just show you the program?"*

This gives them permission to ask anything they're hesitant about before the presentation begins. It transitions the call to presentation mode with their consent.

---

#### Presentation Notes (See Presenting video for full detail)

During the presentation:
- **Link solutions to their discovery answers.** If they said they need support → really emphasize your Q&A calls and community.
- **Check in after most slides:** *"Does this make sense? Do you see yourself using this?"*
- **Tab out for pre-prepared stories.** Have 2–3 case studies ready that match their avatar. Present them naturally mid-presentation.
- **Don't read from the slides.** Use bullet points as prompts. Your voice and tonality do most of the selling.

---

#### Pre-Close (The Most Important Part)

After the presentation, ask:

*"Cool — any more questions on what I just went through? Anything you want me to go deeper on?"*

Handle any objections or questions that come up. Then:

**Question 1:**
*"Look, I speak to a lot of people — some get value out of this, some get value out of that. Where do you see yourself getting the most out of this going forward?"*

Let them tell you what's most valuable to them. Future pacing ("going forward") puts them inside the program mentally.

**Question 2:**
*"What was the goal again? You said… [goal], right? Does it seem realistic — I'm not saying guaranteed — but from everything I've shown you here, does it seem realistic you'd be able to get there?"*

If they say yes: *"Why would you say that? What is it about what I'm showing you that makes you feel like it can get you there?"*

Let them sell themselves completely before you go anywhere near price.

If you get surface-level answers (*"yeah, looks good"*), don't move forward. Dig: *"What specifically looks good? Is this aligning with what you were expecting? Do you actually see yourself using this?"*

---

#### Closing

*"Happy for me to break down next steps and pricing?"*

Go to your pricing slide. State the price. **Stay silent.**

Then: *"So obviously it's a solid investment — but can you justify the price? Like, can you see why it's this much?"*

If they hesitate: *"If you got to [goal] — would you pay [price] for that?"*

Yes → *"What's causing you to think you won't get to [goal]?"*

Then move into the Why Now offer + Unique Justification (see Closing video).

Final closing line: *"Do you need to run and grab your card?"*
