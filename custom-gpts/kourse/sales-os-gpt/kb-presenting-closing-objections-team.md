## Video 7: Presenting

### Why Use a Pitch Deck

Three reasons you need a presentation:

1. **Consistency** — Same structure every call. You don't have to invent the pitch on the fly.
2. **Perceived value** — A professional slide deck makes your offer feel premium. A cheap offer looks better with slides than without them.
3. **Scalability** — When you bring in sales reps, you hand them the deck. They present the same offer, the same way, to every prospect.

Download the template: Keynote + Google Slides version below the video.

---

### How to Actually Present

**Do not read from your slides.** Use bullet points as memory cues. Your voice, energy, and tonality do most of the selling — not the text on screen.

**Tab out for pre-prepared stories.** Before the call, have 2–3 case studies ready that match your prospect's avatar. When you hit the testimonials section, tab out to those stories and present them naturally.

**Check in after most slides:**
- *"Does this make sense?"*
- *"Would you jump on these calls?"*
- *"Do you see yourself using this?"*

These micro-commitment questions keep them engaged and prevent you from getting to the end of a presentation where you have no idea if they're even interested. If they're nodding along after every slide, closing is easy. If they're quiet, dig in.

---

### Presentation Structure

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **Who you are** | Your story, background, credentials — social proof for yourself |
| **Who you've helped** | Results, testimonials, case studies — proof your system works |
| **What's included** | Full offer breakdown — every module, every deliverable, every bonus |
| **How to get started** | Next steps — NOT price. Stop here. Go into pre-close before price. |

**Critical:** Do not go to price after the presentation. Move into the pre-close first. Let them sell themselves before you ever mention a number.

---

### Linking Presentation to Discovery

The most powerful thing you can do in a presentation: link what you're showing to what they told you in discovery.

If they said they need support → lean into your Q&A calls and community section.
If they said they've failed on their own before → emphasize the structure and accountability elements.
If they said they want to fast-track → highlight the shortcuts, templates, and done-for-you elements.

They told you what matters to them. Mirror it back in your presentation.

## Video 8: Closing

### What Closing Actually Is

Closing is not a trick, a technique, or a personality trait. It's the natural result of a well-run call. If you do discovery correctly, present well, and run the pre-close properly — closing is easy. Most of the work is already done.

**One-call close is the goal every single time.** Not because you're being pushy — because it's what's best for the prospect. Every hour they spend thinking about it is another hour they stay stuck where they are. You're doing them a disservice by letting them procrastinate.

---

### Prime the Close Before You Go to Price

**Don't jump straight to price.** Go back through pre-close before you ask for the sale:

1. Re-ask: *"So where did you see yourself getting the most value from this?"* (Let them tell you again.)
2. Re-confirm the goal: *"And the goal was [goal], right? Does everything I've shown you make it seem realistic that you'd get there?"*
3. If they give surface-level answers (*"yeah, looks good"*) — **don't move forward.** Dig:
   - *"What specifically looks good?"*
   - *"Is this aligning with what you were expecting?"*
   - *"Do you actually see yourself using this?"*

Surface-level answers mean they're not bought in yet. Get specific before you go to price, or you'll hit a wall.

---

### The Close

*"Happy for me to break down next steps and pricing?"*

Go to pricing slide. State the price. **Stay silent.** The next person to speak loses. This is a real principle. Say the price, then wait.

*"So obviously it's a solid investment — but can you justify the price? Like, can you see why it's this much?"*

If they hesitate: *"If you got to [goal] — would you pay [price] for that?"*

Yes → *"What's causing you to think you won't get to [goal]?"*

---

### Stacking Conditions (The Why Now Offer)

Closing isn't just naming the price — it's creating a stack of conditions that make buying right now the obvious decision.

| Element | Example |
|---------|---------|
| **Scarcity** | "We only take 10 new people per cohort — and I have 3 spots left" |
| **Urgency** | "The price goes up to $X at the end of this month" |
| **Bonuses** | "I'll also include X, Y, and Z — but only for people who join today" |
| **Guarantee** | "And if you do the work and it doesn't work, I'll refund you" (use sparingly) |
| **Silence** | State the price and stop talking |

Stack 2–4 of these together. The more conditions, the stronger the no-brainer.

---

### Unique Justification

**What it is:** Telling people WHY you're making them such a compelling offer. This is from Hormozi — the photocopier study.

The study: asking to skip a queue with no reason = 60% compliance. Asking with a reason ("because I have 5 copies to make") = 94% compliance. The reason doesn't even have to be great. You just have to give one.

**Applied to sales:**

When you stack scarcity + urgency + bonuses, it can sound salesy without context. Unique justification gives the reason why.

**Example script:**
> *"I do about 10 of these calls a day — honestly, I'd be fine if no one bought anything because my calendar's already full. But the reason I'm offering you all of this today is because I want to build some success stories for [upcoming launch / cohort / new module]. So I've stacked all of these bonuses together and I'm offering this at a lower entry price — but only for people I speak to this week."*

Now it's not salesy. It's honest, logical, and generous. They understand why.

**Real example:** During the Skool Games Battle Royale, this script was used in every call — $230,000 in a single month.

---

### Final Closing Line

*"Do you need to run and grab your card?"*

This signals clearly that payment happens right now. If they push back, that's an objection — handle it. If they get up to grab their card, you've closed.

## Video 9: Objection Handling

### The Most Important Thing to Understand

**Most people spend too much time learning objection handles and not enough time running better calls.**

If you run discovery perfectly, build the gap, pre-handle the time and money objections inside your discovery questions, run the pre-close, and get clear buying signals — most objections won't come up.

- The time objection was pre-handled in question 6 of discovery
- The money objection was pre-handled in question 5 of discovery
- The "I need to think about it" objection is usually just uncertainty — which is a presentation problem, not an objection problem

**The 80/20 rule:** One framework handles 80% of all objections. Learn that framework first. Everything else is secondary.

---

### The Looping Framework (Hormozi)

Use this on every non-money objection. It's designed to be run multiple times (hence "looping") while staying in full rapport.

**Loop 0 — The Opener (before you even loop):**

*"What specifically is making you hesitate?"* or *"What are you afraid of?"*

Get them to name the real concern. People often say "I need to think about it" when the actual concern is something specific. You need to know what you're actually dealing with.

---

**Loop Structure:**

**Step 1 — The Reason:**
Acknowledge their concern. Don't argue or dismiss it.
*"That's totally understandable — most people I speak to have that same thought."*

**Step 2 — The Hypothetical:**
Assume the objection is resolved and ask: *"Hypothetically, if [objection] wasn't an issue — is this something you'd want to move forward with?"*

This tests whether the stated objection is the real one. If they say yes, you've isolated it. If they bring up a new objection, you know there's another layer — and you loop again.

**Step 3 — Zoom Out:**
*"Let me ask you — what happens if you don't take action on this? Where will you be in 6 months? A year?"*

Connect them back to the pain of staying stuck. They already told you this in discovery — use it.

**Step 4 — 1–10 Pulse Check:**
*"On a scale of 1 to 10 — 10 being 'I'm in, let's go right now' and 1 being 'absolutely not' — where are you right now?"*

If they say anything above a 5, follow up: *"What would get you to a 10?"*

Their answer tells you exactly what to address.

**Step 5 — Best Case / Worst Case:**
*"What's the worst case scenario here? What's the absolute worst that could happen if you went ahead?"*

Then: *"And what's the best case? What does your life look like 90 days from now if this works like we're saying it will?"*

The worst case is almost never as bad as they imagine. The best case is everything they came to the call for. This reframe often breaks the hesitation.

**Loop the Sequence:** If the objection isn't resolved, loop again from Step 1 with a fresh angle. You can loop up to 3 times while keeping it natural and conversational.

---

**Final step — Book the Follow-Up (if still unresolved):**

*"Okay look — I don't want to pressure you into anything on this call. What I'd rather do is give you a bit of time to think, and then let's jump on another call in 48 hours. That way you're not rushed, and I can answer any questions that come up. Does that work?"*

This is not a failure. A 48-hour follow-up is a live lead. Send them a booking link on the spot.

---

### Isolating the Price Objection

**You cannot use loops on a real money objection.**

If the only thing stopping someone is that they genuinely don't have the money — no amount of hypotheticals or zoom-outs will fix that. You need to isolate the objection first.

**The Money Aside Close:**
*"Just so I understand — is it that this isn't the right fit for you, or is it purely a money thing?"*

You're trying to confirm: is price the ONLY thing stopping them? Everything else checks out?

If yes — now you're dealing with a money problem, not a commitment problem.

---

### Handling the Price Objection

Once price is isolated as the only objection:

1. **Ask permission:** *"Can I share a few thoughts on the investment side?"*

2. **Offer pay-in-two:** *"Most people in your position do a split payment. You put half down today to lock in the bonuses, and we take the second half in 30 days. Does that work?"*

3. **Reconfirm commitment:** *"So outside of the money piece, you're confident this is the right program for you?"*

4. **Ask the open question:** *"How much do you have to put down today?"*

Let them tell you. Some people will say $500. Some will say $1,500. You don't know until you ask.

---

### Taking a Deposit

If they need time to gather funds, take a deposit to lock in their bonuses:

*"What I can do is take a small deposit today — $X — to hold your spot and keep all the bonuses in place. Then we schedule the remaining payment for [date]. That way you don't lose anything while you sort out the rest of the funds. Does that work?"*

A deposit creates commitment and keeps the deal alive. It also removes the "I'll think about it" loop — they've already paid something, so they're invested.

---

### Common Objection Scripts

**"I need to think about it":**
Use the push-pull.
*"Of course — and I'd never want to pressure you into something. Can I ask though — what specifically is making you want to think it over? Is there something I didn't cover clearly?"*

Get the real objection. "I need to think about it" is almost never the actual concern.

**"I need to speak to my spouse":**
Use the BAM from meeting framework.
*"Totally understand — and I respect that. Just curious, has your partner been supportive of you wanting to build this? Because the last thing I'd want is for this to cause friction at home."*

Let them respond. Then: *"Would it make sense for me to jump on a quick 15-minute call with both of you together? I can answer any questions you both have. That usually makes the conversation a lot easier."*

Never send them away to have the conversation alone. Get on a three-way call or book one.

## Video 10: Upsells

### The Buying Window

Right after someone makes a purchase is the best time to upsell them. This is called the **buying window** — and it's the most overlooked revenue lever in sales.

**The marathon analogy:**
When someone decides to run a marathon, they don't sign up and then slowly acquire gear over months. They go online immediately and buy: running shoes, training plan, nutrition guide, compression socks, foam roller, everything. They're in a buying state.

It's the same with courses and coaching. The moment someone buys, their identity has shifted. They're now "someone who is doing this." Their buying resistance is at its lowest. Their motivation is at its highest.

If you have more to offer that can help them — you are doing them a disservice by NOT presenting it.

---

### Hormozi's Value Equation Applied to Upsells

When you upsell, you're not just selling more stuff — you're increasing:

- **Perceived likelihood of success** — "With this add-on, you're much more likely to hit your goal"
- **Speed** — "This gets you there faster"
- **Ease** — "This removes the hardest part so you don't have to figure it out yourself"

Frame every upsell in these terms. Not "do you want more?" but "do you want to get to [goal] twice as fast with half the effort?"

---

### What to Offer as an Upsell

| Upsell Type | What It Is | When to Offer |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| **One-on-one help** | Private coaching calls with you | Immediately after purchase |
| **Group mastermind** | Upgrade to your paid mastermind or higher tier | Immediately after purchase |
| **In-person event** | Live event, retreat, or workshop | Immediately after purchase |
| **More proximity** | More access to you — coaching, feedback, review | Immediately after purchase |

The upsell should always offer more proximity to you. That's what people are buying — faster results, more help, more access.

---

### Rollover Payments

**What it is:** Crediting what they already paid toward a higher-tier offer.

**How to frame it:**
> *"So you just joined at $2,000. Normally the mastermind is $3,000 — but since you've already paid $2,000, I'll credit that toward the mastermind. So it's just $1,000 more and you get the full mastermind plus everything you already bought. You keep the community. You just level up."*

This lowers the psychological barrier dramatically. They're not paying $3,000 — they're paying $1,000 more. That feels very different.

---

### The Monthly-to-Annual Upsell

For anyone who joined on a monthly subscription, there's a simple upsell sequence:

1. **Send a Loom video** the day they join: personalized welcome, quick walk-through of what's inside, congratulate them on joining
2. **At the end of the Loom:** mention that most serious members go annual, it's a better deal, and you'd love to jump on a 15-minute onboarding call to set them up properly
3. **On the onboarding call:** show them the value of going annual, offer 50% off the annual price, credit the monthly payment they just made
4. **Ask:** *"Does it make more sense to stay monthly or just lock in the year and save the money?"*

Most people will go annual. They already committed when they paid monthly. The onboarding call is also just the right thing to do — it improves their experience regardless of whether they upgrade.

---

### Moral Obligation to Upsell

This is the same moral obligation framework from conviction — just applied post-purchase.

If you have a mastermind, a higher tier, or a one-on-one offer that can get them to their goal faster and with more support — not offering it is selfish. You're withholding help because it feels uncomfortable to ask for more money.

They already trust you. They already paid. The hardest sale is already done. If you genuinely believe the upsell will help them — offer it. Every time.

## Video 11: Building a Sales Team

### When to Hire

**Hire when it hurts.** Specifically:

1. **Too many leads, not enough setting** — You have 50+ new leads per day and you can't DM or call them all. You're leaving booked calls on the table.
2. **Too many calls, not enough capacity** — You have 5+ calls per day on your calendar and it's eating into your ability to run the business, record content, or do anything else.

**Do NOT hire because you hate sales.** That's the wrong reason. If you haven't dialed in your own script and close rate first, a rep inherits a broken system. Fix the system first, then hand it off.

> "If you bring someone in before you've proven it works, you're just paying someone to fail."

---

### Threshold for Hiring

| Role | Hire When |
|------|-----------|
| **Appointment Setter** | 50–100+ new leads per day that you can't manually reach |
| **Closer** | 5+ calls per day consistently on the calendar |
| **Hybrid Setter-Closer** | 25–50 leads per day — can set and close, good for smaller operations |
| **Community Manager + Sales Hybrid** | High community activity that needs someone to manage conversations AND convert |

If you're not at these thresholds yet, keep doing it yourself. You'll learn more, save the payroll, and have a proven system ready when you do hire.

---

### Who to Hire

**Look inside your community first.** Before posting a job ad anywhere:

- Check your paid community for members who are crushing it and love sales
- Check your free community for engaged people who have been around a while

**Miles and Dylan are the example:**
Miles was a member of the Kourse community before becoming Head of Sales. Dylan (setter) came the same way. They already understood the product, believed in it, and had seen the results firsthand. That's what you want in your first hire.

Someone who bought your product and got results from it will sell it better than anyone you hire from a job board. They're not pretending to believe in it. They actually do.

---

### What to Look for in a Closer

- Can they talk through their sales experience confidently?
- Have they done commission-based work before?
- Do they ask good questions when you talk to them? (Meta signal: a good closer will close YOU on the interview)
- Do they actually use your product or at least understand what it does?

**On commission structure:** Closers typically work on performance-based pay — a percentage of cash collected or contracted value. Structure this conversation clearly before they start. Define: What counts as a won deal? What's the commission rate? Is there a draw?

---

### Handing Off the System

When you hire a rep, you don't just hand them the call calendar. You hand them:

1. The sales script (course.com/sales-framework)
2. Recording access (Fathom AI) — so you can review their calls
3. Access to the GHL pipeline — so they can manage leads and outcomes
4. A role-play session or two before they go live
5. Clear KPIs: call show rate, close rate, cash collected per week

The reason Kourse's team went from 30% to 55% close rate team-wide wasn't talent — it was enforcing the script. Reps who freestyled underperformed. Reps who followed the framework hit numbers.

> "It's very teachable. Once someone learns to follow the framework, they close at the same rate I do. The script removes the dependency on being a natural."

---

### The Long-Term Vision

The sales team is a system, not a person. Your goal is:

1. **You close** — until your close rate is 50%+
2. **You set + close** — until you have 5+ calls/day
3. **Hire a setter** — you focus on closing only
4. **Hire a closer** — you step back from calls entirely
5. **You manage the system** — review recordings, improve the script, coach reps

At that point, sales runs without you. That's the business.
