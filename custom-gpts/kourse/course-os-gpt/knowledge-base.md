## 2. Free vs. Paid Community Strategy

### Why You Need Both (Mandatory)
- A free course is the **ultimate lead magnet** — giving away something for free that people usually pay for
- Works extremely well for paid ads and YouTube CTAs ("join my free course")
- Don't mix leads with customers in one community — it creates a messy experience
- Paid customers get pitched content they already bought; free members see pitches not meant for them
- Having a free community is NOT optional — "If you complain about managing a free course, you're basically saying you don't have time to promote"

### What to Put in the Free Course
- The **fun, actionable, and wanted stuff** — NOT the boring theory-heavy material
- Start with things that create an "identity shift" (e.g., setting up tech, designing their community)
- Use the **sunken cost fallacy** strategically — get them invested in time and energy before introducing harder content
- Give away something so good that people think "if the free is this good, imagine the paid"
- The free course is the START of the transformation, not the complete transformation
- Consider moving your best content from paid to free if it's what people really want

### What to Put in the Paid Course
- The **full course** — completion of the entire transformation
- **Exclusive community** — separate, smaller community with only paying customers
- **More support** — answer questions, host 1-5 Q&A calls per week
- Higher quality community because everyone has invested money to be there
- People are always willing to pay to reach goals faster with more help

### The Conversion Flow
1. People join the free course (lead magnet)
2. They go through fun, actionable content and experience an identity shift
3. They realize they need the full transformation
4. They book a call and buy the paid course
5. "If you like the free, you're going to love the paid" — easiest pitch ever

### Course Structure Strategy
- Put fun/actionable stuff FIRST in the free course, not theory
- Example: In Course OS free, tech setup comes before niche selection because theory-heavy niche work would lose people early
- Once students have invested time building tech, they're more committed when the harder theory arrives
- Challenge yourself: maybe take something you think is "too valuable" and put it in the free

## 3. Course Creation Process (SOP)

### Before You Begin Checklist
- Everything in Trello must be done: course name, module names, video names, bullet points, potential resources
- Niche and Course Design video watched (2-3 times recommended)
- Clear distinction between free and paid content
- Understand that the plan and structure is literally everything

### The Creation Process (Order of Operations)
1. **Build ALL slides first** — Stay in "slide creation mode" for the entire course
2. **Record ALL videos** — Then switch to "recording mode" and do them all in one go
3. **Edit ALL videos** — Then switch to "editing mode"
4. **Upload ALL videos** — Then post everything to Skool in one go

This batch approach keeps you in one mode at a time instead of switching between tasks.

### Workflow Per Video
1. Look at Trello card for the video
2. Take the 4-5 bullet points planned in Trello
3. Build slides from those bullet points
4. Record the presentation (read bullet point → speak openly about it)
5. Edit in Tella (trim beginning/end, cut mistakes)
6. Upload to Skool with description, transcript, and resources

### Pacing Goals
- Aim to complete one week's content per week
- 1-2 videos per day
- Example: 10 videos in free course = done in 5 days at 2/day

### Setting Up the Skeleton in Skool
- Create your classroom, give it a name and description, upload cover image
- Use the Skool Design Pack for pre-designed templates
- For the **free community**: videos listed directly (no folders needed for just one module)
- For the **paid community**: set up folders for each week/module
- Include a VSL in the paid community classroom to promote the paid course
- Set up Q&A recordings / workshop recordings section

### File Organization
- Create a main folder on Desktop named after your course
- Create sub-folders for each week (Week 1, Week 2, etc.)
- Inside each week, create folders for each video
- Each video folder contains: keynote/slides, recorded video, resources
- Back up to Google Drive as well
- You'll end up with 4 copies: computer, Google Drive, Skool, Tella

### Gamification
- Color-code Trello cards as you progress:
  - White/blank = not started
  - Yellow = slides built
  - Orange = recorded
  - Green = posted/published
- Hover over a card and press 1 for green, 3 for orange

## 4. Presentation Design (5 Principles)

### Source
These principles come from **David JP Phillips**, the "presentation master" (Swedish). Alex Hormozi also uses these principles in his book launches and presentations.

### The 5 Keynote Design Principles

1. **One Message** — Only have one message per slide that your audience focuses on. Don't present all bullet points at once. Reveal one at a time.

2. **Contrast** — Use contrast to steer audience attention. Eyes are drawn to things that move, have color, are bigger, or have higher contrast. Use this to highlight the current point.

3. **Dark Backgrounds** — Use dark backgrounds. Makes it easier on the eyes, helps the presenter stand out on screen, and avoids the "blasted by white screen" effect.

4. **Six Items Max** — Don't use more than 6 items per slide. One headline, one image, and up to 4 bullet points maximum.

5. **Short Sentences** — Avoid long sentences because people will read while you talk. Combat this by reading the sentence for them first, then explaining openly.

### Presentation Teaching Method
- Read each bullet point out loud to the audience
- Then speak openly about it — add stories, context, examples
- This keeps the audience focused (they read along with you, then look at you when you explain)
- Write bullet points the way you speak — you'll read them during recording

### Why Presentations Matter
- Presentations literally turn into money in an online course business
- They ARE your product
- The better you get at building and presenting keynotes, the more money you make
- Learning this skill applies to courses, webinars, workshops, sales material

## 5. Gear Recommendations

### Philosophy
- Good audio is FAR more superior than having good video
- The most important piece of gear is your **microphone**
- Value matters more than production quality
- Example: A $24,000 mastermind was recorded on a MacBook with wired Apple headphones — and it was amazing
- Meanwhile, courses with fancy gear and crazy editing can be "mid or kind of bad"

### What You Need
- A good microphone (most important)
- Your computer's built-in camera is fine to start
- A quiet recording environment
- Tella software installed on your computer (Mac or PC)

### What You DON'T Need
- Expensive camera
- Expensive lenses
- Big studio lights
- Massive production setup
- Background music in course videos

## 6. Recording

### Recommended Tool: Tella
- Sign up at the course discount link (20% off via course.com)
- Download the Tella desktop app (Mac or PC)
- Records, edits, exports, and hosts videos in one place
- "By far the best, fastest, most affordable and easy to use option"

### Recording Settings
- Select the correct microphone
- Select the correct camera
- Enable screen recording
- Record in **4K** (highest quality possible)
- Record in **30fps**

### Recording Tips
- **Show your face** — mandatory. Creates trust, makes communication easier, makes the course more alive
- **Stay casual** — talk naturally like chatting with a friend
- **Stick to the plan** — introduce the module, give overview, cover each point, summarize
- **Read bullet points then speak openly** — this is the core method
- **No teleprompter or scripts needed** — just read your slides and explain
- **Record in one go** — try to do everything in one take
- If you make a mistake, just pause silently, then restart from that bullet point (edit it out later)
- Don't re-record entire videos for small mistakes — just leave them in or cut that section
- "If you can speak with a friend, you can record a course"

### Video Length
- Each video should be 15-20 minutes to 2 hours max
- Don't make massive amounts of short videos — it's messy
- Don't make videos short for short's sake or long for long's sake
- Give each concept the time it needs
- Each video should flow into the next one

### Camera Presence
- Be yourself — don't try to be someone else
- Put a smile on your face — great energy performs better
- You WILL get better as you record more
- It's okay to be nervous — accept it and do it anyway
- You can always re-record later — nothing is set in stone

## 7. Editing with Tella

### Philosophy
- Editing is NOT the secret behind great course videos
- The value you bring IS the star
- Hollywood analogy: amazing editing + bad storyline = bad movie. Simple filmmaking + great storyline = great movie
- Don't overcomplicate editing — keep it simple
- "Done is better than perfect"

### Editing Process in Tella
1. **Trim** — cut the beginning and end of the video
2. **Cut** — remove any mistakes or unwanted sections in the middle (use the "split" feature)
3. **Layout** — set up the screen layout:
   - Full screen for intro/outro (just you talking to camera)
   - Screen share + you on the side for the main content
4. **Background** — choose a background (Apple-style vibe recommended)
5. **Transitions** — drag and drop layouts to create smooth animations between full screen and screen share

### What NOT to Do
- Don't use background music (everyone has different taste, sounds weird at 2x speed)
- Don't add fancy effects or complex animations
- Don't try to make it look like a Netflix production
- Don't hire expensive editors for course videos (save that for YouTube)

### Organizing in Tella
- Create playlists/folders for your course
- Save videos in the appropriate playlist
- Tella auto-generates transcripts for each video

## 8. Publishing to Skool

### Export Process
1. In Tella, click Download
2. Export in **4K**
3. Export at **30fps**
4. Speed at **1x**
5. Save to your computer (in the organized folder structure)
6. Also upload to Google Drive for backup

### Publishing to Skool
1. Go to your Skool classroom
2. Edit the video page
3. Drag and drop your video file — Skool has **built-in free video hosting** (unlimited bandwidth, unlimited viewers, included in $99/month)
4. Add a description (model how Course OS does their descriptions)
5. Add the transcript (copy from Tella and paste into Skool)
6. Add resource links or files (Google Docs, PDFs, etc.)
7. Mark the video as green in Trello once published

### Why Skool Hosting is Great
- Free, unlimited bandwidth, unlimited viewers
- Included in the $99/month Skool subscription
- Previously they used Vimeo which wanted to charge $2,000/month or $12,000/year
- Having transcripts makes everything searchable in the community

### After Publishing
- Announce new videos to your community
- Let free members know when new content is live
- Let pre-sold paid members know when new course content launches
- Gamify the process — mark completed videos in Trello

## 9. Mindset & Overcoming Fear

### On Perfectionism
- "Done is better than perfect"
- You can always re-record videos later
- Courses need updates anyway — you'll evolve, learn new things, get customer feedback
- Most courses get "patched" with extra videos over time, then eventually get a full redo
- Small mistakes make it more authentic — don't edit them out
- The 95% is in the planning; recording and editing are the easy/fun part

### On Being on Camera
- "If you can speak with a friend, you can record a course"
- You don't need a teleprompter or script
- Accept that it's a bit scary and do it anyway
- You will get better at recording as you do it — the only way to improve is to start
- Be yourself — don't try to be someone you're not
- A smile and positive energy do more than any editing trick

### On Course Updates
- Your course is your product — it should evolve
- Expect to update at least periodically
- Common pattern: launch → get questions → add patch videos → eventually redo the whole course
- Rebuilding is easier the second time because the structure already exists

### On Value vs. Production
- The value you bring is the star, not fancy editing
- "I paid editors and videographers tens of thousands per month — but the viewer doesn't care"
- A well-structured course with simple production beats a poorly structured course with amazing production every time
- Focus on the transformation, not the polish
