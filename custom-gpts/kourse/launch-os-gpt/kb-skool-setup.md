## The Skool Platform Overview

Skool is the community and classroom platform. It has five main sections:
1. **Community** — like a Facebook group but better. Members post, comment, engage
2. **Classroom** — where your course modules and videos live
3. **Calendar** — for scheduling live calls, events, Q&As
4. **Leaderboards** — gamification. Members earn points for posting, commenting, completing modules
5. **About** — your community's landing page (this is your free community's homepage)

You run TWO Skool communities:
- **Free community** — lead magnet, grows with organic content and ads. Lower barrier to entry
- **Paid community** — where Course OS (or your main course) lives. Members pay monthly or annually

## Setting Up Your Skool School (Video 1 Walkthrough)

### Basic Setup Steps
1. Go to Skool.com → Create a new community
2. Choose your community name (matches your brand/course name)
3. Set your access: **Public** (anyone can find it) vs **Private** (invite only)
   - Free community: set to Public so it's discoverable
   - Paid community: set to Private / paid access only

### Community Description
Write a clear, benefit-driven description:
- Who this community is for
- What they'll learn/get
- What makes it different

### Profile & Branding
- Upload a professional profile photo and cover image
- Match your brand colors if possible
- Keep it clean and simple — doesn't need to be fancy

### Leaderboard Setup
Turn on leaderboards to drive engagement:
- Members earn points for posting, commenting, liking
- Top members appear on the leaderboard
- Creates friendly competition and keeps people active

## The 3 C's Framework (Video 3)

Every successful Skool community is built on three pillars:

### 1. Community
The social layer. This is where members interact, ask questions, share wins, and support each other. A strong community creates retention — people stay because of the relationships, not just the content.

**How to build community:**
- Post daily (or encourage members to)
- Welcome every new member publicly
- Celebrate wins (even small ones)
- Ask engagement questions to spark conversations
- Respond to every comment, especially in the early days

### 2. Classroom
The educational layer. Your course content lives here organized into modules.

**Classroom best practices:**
- Organize content into clear modules (Module 1, Module 2, etc.)
- Keep lesson titles simple and benefit-driven ("How to Set Up Your Skool School" not "Lesson 4")
- Start with a welcome/orientation module
- Use a mix of video + short text notes for each lesson

### 3. Calendar
The live events layer. Scheduled calls, workshops, Q&As.

**Why the calendar matters:**
- Creates accountability and routine
- Gives members a reason to stay active
- Live calls are your highest-value touchpoint
- Even one weekly group call dramatically improves retention

## About Page Setup (Video 3)

The About page is the landing page for your free Skool community. This is what people see before they join — treat it like a sales page.

### About Page Structure
1. **Headline** — clear statement of who this is for and the main benefit
   - Example: "For coaches and consultants building their first online course"
2. **Subheadline** — expand on the promise or add urgency
3. **What you'll get** — bullet list of benefits (3-5 max)
4. **Social proof** — member count, testimonials, results
5. **Call to action** — "Join Free" button (Skool handles this automatically)

### Tips for the About Page
- Keep it short — this isn't a 5,000-word sales letter
- Use plain language, not jargon
- Focus on outcomes, not features
- Add a short video if possible (a talking-head or VSL works great)

## VSL (Video Sales Letter) on Skool About Page

Adding a VSL to your About page dramatically increases join rates.

**What is a VSL?**
A short (2-5 minute) video where you speak directly to your ideal member:
- Acknowledge their pain/problem
- Introduce yourself and your credibility
- Explain what the community is and what they'll get
- Give a clear call to action to join

**Where to host it:**
- Record with Tella (screen + webcam, professional look)
- Upload to YouTube (unlisted) or Vimeo
- Embed the link on your Skool About page

## Skool Classroom Setup

### Creating Modules
1. Go to Classroom → Add Module
2. Name it clearly (Module 1: The Foundation, Module 2: Setting Up Your Tech Stack, etc.)
3. Add lessons within each module
4. Lessons can be: video, text, or a combination

### Lesson Structure Best Practice
- **Title:** Benefit-driven, clear
- **Video:** Upload directly to Skool or embed from external source
- **Description/Notes:** Key takeaways, links to tools, next steps
- **Completion tracking:** Skool tracks which lessons members have completed

### Locking Content
You can lock certain modules until members complete earlier ones. This is great for:
- Ensuring people go through content in order
- Creating a structured learning journey
- Gamification (unlock advanced content by completing basics)

## Skool Plugins

Skool has built-in plugins to extend functionality:

- **Zapier plugin** — connects Skool to GoHighLevel and other tools. This is how you sync new members to your CRM
- **Stripe integration** — for collecting payments directly through Skool
- **Affiliate program** — lets members refer others and earn commissions
- **Drip content** — release lessons on a schedule (e.g., new module every week)

**The most important plugin for Launch OS: Zapier**
This is how new free community members get automatically added to GoHighLevel so your email sequences fire automatically. See kb-tech-stack.md for full setup.

## Community Design Tips

- **Keep it focused** — one clear topic/niche per community. Don't try to cover everything
- **Set community guidelines** — what's allowed, what's not. Keeps quality high
- **Pin important posts** — pin your welcome post, course access post, and key resources
- **Weekly rhythm** — create a predictable schedule (Monday motivation, Wednesday Q&A, Friday wins)
- **Engagement triggers** — ask questions in your posts to invite comments. "What's your biggest challenge with X?"
