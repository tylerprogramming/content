## The Core Business Model

The Launch OS model is a simple, proven funnel:

```
Free Community (Skool) → Lead Capture → VSL → Booked Call → Sale → Paid Community
```

1. **Free Community** — your audience lives here. You grow it with content, ads, and referrals
2. **Lead Capture** — someone opts in to watch your VSL (Video Sales Letter)
3. **VSL** — a short sales video that warms them up and sells the next step (booking a call)
4. **Booked Call** — a 1-on-1 or group sales call where you close
5. **Sale** — they join your paid community or buy your course
6. **Paid Community** — ongoing recurring revenue, high retention, upsell opportunities

This is the exact model Max uses. It's not complicated — it's a free community that feeds a paid product.

## Why Two Communities?

**Free community = your lead magnet**
- Lower barrier to entry (free = anyone can join)
- Gives massive value upfront ("if you like the free, you're going to love the paid")
- Builds trust and relationship before asking for money
- Grows passively as members invite others and see it on Skool's leaderboard

**Paid community = your revenue engine**
- Members pay to access your course content + live calls
- Ongoing monthly or annual subscription
- High retention because of community + accountability
- Easier to sell to someone who's already in your free community

**The objection Max addresses:**
"I don't have time to run two communities."

Max's response: "If you say you don't have time for two communities, you're basically saying you don't have time to promote your business. The free community IS your marketing."

## Annual vs Monthly Pricing

This is one of the most important decisions you'll make. Max strongly recommends **annual pricing** as your primary offer.

### Annual Pricing Model
**Example price point:** $997/year or $1,497/year

**Why annual is better:**
1. **Higher upfront cash** — you collect the full year upfront. Better for cash flow, better for business
2. **Lower churn** — people don't cancel after month 2 because they've already paid. Annual members stay longer
3. **Commitment signal** — annual buyers are more serious. They take action, get results, become case studies
4. **Simpler math** — one sale = one year of MRR. No monthly churn tracking
5. **Psychology** — annual feels like a bigger decision (it is), which filters out tire-kickers

### Monthly Pricing (as a fallback)
**Example price point:** $97–$197/month

Keep a monthly option available for people who say they can't afford annual. But always lead with annual and only offer monthly if they push back.

**How to present it:**
- Lead: "It's $997 for the full year"
- If objection: "We also have a monthly option at $197/month — but most people do annual because it's a much better deal"

### Pricing Philosophy
- Don't underprice. Cheap prices attract cheap clients who don't take action
- Your price signals the value. $97/month feels like a hobby. $997/year feels like an investment
- Alex Hormozi principle: price for the outcome you deliver, not the time it takes you

## The Free Lead Magnet Strategy

Alex Hormozi's principle: **"The ultimate lead magnet is giving away something for free that people are used to paying money for."**

Your free Skool community should contain genuinely valuable content — not a watered-down teaser. Give away so much value that people think "if the free is this good, the paid must be incredible."

**What to put in your free community:**
- Starter content related to your niche (short videos, guides, resources)
- Community where your target audience hangs out
- Regular live calls or Q&As (even monthly is enough to start)
- Quick wins — things people can implement immediately and see results

**Gary Vee's principle applies:** Jab, jab, jab, right hook. Give, give, give — then ask.

## Revenue Projections (Realistic Expectations)

Max is transparent about what's realistic:

**Starting out (0-6 months):**
- Focus on getting your first 10 paid members
- $997 x 10 = ~$10K. That's a real business milestone
- Don't worry about scale yet — nail the process first

**Growing (6-18 months):**
- 50-100 paid members = $50K–$100K/year
- At this point you have a business, not just a side hustle
- Reinvest in ads to accelerate growth

**Scaling (18+ months):**
- 100+ paid members at $997/year = $100K+/year
- Add higher-ticket coaching/mastermind upsells
- Hire help for community management

## The Recurring Revenue Advantage

Why recurring > one-time sales:

- **Predictability** — you know what's coming in each month/quarter
- **Compounding** — each new member adds to your base. It grows over time
- **Retention incentive** — you're motivated to keep creating value (members leave if you stop)
- **Valuation** — subscription businesses are worth more (10x+ ARR vs 2-3x for one-time sale businesses)

## Power Activities

Max talks about "power activities" — the things that actually move the needle in your business:

1. **Building your product** (Course OS, Launch OS content creation)
2. **Promoting** (posting content, running ads, engaging in your free community)
3. **Sales calls** (closing conversations with leads)

Everything else is a distraction or a support activity. Time yourself — if you're spending most of your day on admin, you have a prioritization problem.

---

## The Business Stages

**Stage 1: Build** — Create your course/community, set up your tech stack (this is what Launch OS teaches)
**Stage 2: Launch** — Get your first paying members. Focus on personal outreach and content
**Stage 3: Automate** — Set up systems so leads flow in and get nurtured automatically
**Stage 4: Scale** — Invest in paid traffic to accelerate what's already working

You need to be honest about what stage you're in. Don't run ads until you've validated your offer manually first.
