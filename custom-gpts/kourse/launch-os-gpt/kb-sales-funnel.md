## The Sales Page (Video 7)

The sales page is where you make the ask. Someone lands here and decides: "Is this worth my money?"

### Sales Page Purpose
Convert warm leads (people who've watched your VSL, been in your free community, or been nurtured by email) into paying customers.

### Sales Page Structure
A high-converting sales page follows a proven structure:

**1. Headline**
- The single most important element on the page
- Clear transformation: "Go From [Current Pain] To [Desired Outcome] In [Timeframe]"
- Examples:
  - "Build Your First Online Course in 30 Days — Even If You Have No Audience"
  - "Launch Your Coaching Business on Skool Without Spending a Fortune on Ads"
- Gary Halbert principle: The headline is 80% of the work. Spend 80% of your copywriting time here

**2. Subheadline**
- Supports and expands the headline
- Often handles an objection or adds specificity

**3. The Problem**
- Describe exactly what your ideal customer is struggling with
- The more specific, the better ("You've bought 5 courses but still haven't launched..." vs "You're struggling")
- When someone reads this and thinks "How did you read my mind?" — that's a good problem section

**4. The Solution**
- Introduce your product as the answer
- What it is, what's inside, how it solves the problem
- Keep it benefit-focused, not feature-focused

**5. What You Get (The Offer Stack)**
- List everything included
- Skool community access
- Course modules (name them)
- Live calls (how often, how long)
- Bonuses (extra resources, templates, tools)
- Assign a dollar value to each component
- Show the "real value" vs the actual price

**6. Social Proof**
- Testimonials from past students (text + photo for credibility)
- Results-focused: "I made $X" or "I launched my course in 3 weeks" not just "Great course!"
- Before/after transformations are powerful

**7. Who It's For (and Who It's NOT For)**
- Qualify your buyer: "This is for you if..."
- Also: "This is NOT for you if..." — counter-intuitive but builds trust and filters bad leads

**8. Your Story & Credibility**
- Brief bio: who you are, your relevant experience, why you built this
- Don't overcomplicate — 2-3 paragraphs max
- Make it relatable: share a struggle you had that led to building this solution

**9. FAQ**
- Address the top 5-8 objections you hear on sales calls
- "How long do I have access?" → "Lifetime access to the course, membership is annual"
- "What if I'm not technical?" → "Super simple. If you can use Google, you can do this"
- "Is there a refund?" → State your policy clearly

**10. Pricing + CTA (Call to Action)**
- Show the price clearly — no hiding it
- Annual vs monthly option (lead with annual)
- Big button: "Join [Community Name] Now" or "Start Today"
- Repeat the CTA 2-3 times down the page (not everyone scrolls to the bottom)

## Calendar & Booking Setup (Video 8)

A frictionless booking experience is essential. Every extra click = fewer bookings.

### GoHighLevel Calendar Setup
GHL has a built-in calendar feature that handles:
- Your availability settings
- Booking confirmation emails
- Reminder emails and SMS
- Calendar sync with Google Calendar

**Setting up your calendar:**
1. GHL → Calendars → Create New Calendar
2. **Calendar Type:** Choose "Simple" (one person) or "Round Robin" (team)
3. **Name it:** "Free Strategy Call" or "Launch OS Discovery Call"
4. **Meeting Duration:** 30 or 45 minutes (keep it tight — you don't need an hour for a sales call)
5. **Availability:** Set your working days and hours
6. **Buffer time:** Add 15 min between calls so you have breathing room
7. **Time zone:** Confirm your correct time zone

### Calendar Link
Once set up, GHL gives you a booking link:
- `yourbrand.gohighlevel.com/calendars/yourname`
- Or use your custom domain: `calendly.yourdomain.com` (you can redirect this)

Share this link:
- In your email sequences ("Book your free call here: [link]")
- On your VSL thank you page
- In your Skool free community profile
- In your email signature

### Connecting Calendar to Google Calendar
1. GHL → Calendars → Settings → Integrations
2. Connect Google Calendar
3. GHL will check your Google Calendar for existing events and won't double-book you

### The Confirmation & Reminder Sequence
When someone books a call, they should automatically receive:
- **Immediate:** Booking confirmation email with call details
- **24 hours before:** Reminder email (reduces no-shows dramatically)
- **1 hour before:** SMS reminder (if you've collected phone number)

This is set up via automations in GHL (covered in Video 9).

## Automations (Video 9)

Automations are the secret weapon. They handle nurturing, follow-up, and booking confirmation without you lifting a finger.

### The Three Core Automations in Launch OS

---

#### Automation 1: Email Welcome Sequence

**Trigger:** Tag added → `free-community-joined` (fired by Zapier when someone joins free Skool community)

**Goal:** Welcome new members, deliver value, build trust, invite them to opt in to the VSL

**Sequence:**
- **Email 1 (Immediate):** Welcome email
  - Subject: "Welcome to [Community Name]! Here's what to do first"
  - Content: Warm welcome, introduce yourself, tell them what to expect
  - CTA: "Check out the free resources in the community"

- **Email 2 (Day 1):** Value email
  - Subject: A quick win related to your topic
  - Content: One useful tip, insight, or resource they can use today
  - CTA: Soft invite to book a call or check out the VSL — "By the way, I put together a free training on [topic]..."

- **Email 3 (Day 3):** Story email
  - Subject: "How I went from [problem] to [result]"
  - Content: Your origin story — relatable, honest, not a brag
  - CTA: Link to your VSL opt-in page

- **Email 4 (Day 5):** Social proof email
  - Subject: "[Student Name] did X in Y days — here's how"
  - Content: A student case study or result
  - CTA: "Want to do the same? Watch this free training: [VSL link]"

- **Email 5 (Day 7):** Direct ask
  - Subject: "Are you ready to [outcome]?"
  - Content: Straightforward. You've given value, now make the ask
  - CTA: Strong CTA to watch VSL or book a call directly

**Tips for the welcome sequence:**
- Write in your voice — conversational, not corporate
- Each email should be able to stand alone (someone might skip Email 2 and open Email 3)
- Always include plain text versions (better deliverability)
- Subject lines: curiosity > clickbait. Be intriguing but honest

---

#### Automation 2: VSL Opt-in Sequence

**Trigger:** Contact opts in on VSL landing page (submits form)

**Goal:** Follow up with people who opted in but haven't booked a call yet. Drive them to book.

**Sequence:**
- **Email 1 (Immediate):** "Here's your access"
  - Subject: "Your free training is ready"
  - Content: Link to watch the VSL, brief description of what they'll learn
  - CTA: "Watch Now" button

- **Email 2 (Day 1):** "Did you watch?"
  - Subject: "Quick question about your [goal]"
  - Content: Check in. Remind them of the value inside the training. Add one compelling reason to watch
  - CTA: Link back to the VSL

- **Email 3 (Day 2):** Value + CTA
  - Subject: One useful insight related to your topic
  - Content: Give a tip that previews what's inside the paid program
  - CTA: "Ready to go deeper? Book a free call: [calendar link]"

- **Email 4 (Day 4):** Urgency/FOMO
  - Subject: "I only take [X] calls per week"
  - Content: Create mild scarcity around your time. This isn't fake — you genuinely can't do unlimited calls
  - CTA: Book a call link

- **Email 5 (Day 7):** Last chance
  - Subject: "Last email about this"
  - Content: Honest. "I've sent a few emails — if this isn't the right time, no worries. But if you're serious about [goal], here's how I can help"
  - CTA: Book a call

**Best practices:**
- Stop sending this sequence once they book a call (add a condition: if call is booked → stop)
- Use conditional logic: if they opened Email 1 → continue sequence. If they didn't open first 2 emails → they may be inactive, consider removing from sequence

---

#### Automation 3: Appointment Confirmation & Reminder

**Trigger:** Appointment/call booked in GHL calendar

**Goal:** Reduce no-shows. Confirm the booking and keep them excited about the call.

**Sequence:**

- **Immediately after booking:** Confirmation email
  - Subject: "Your call is confirmed — [Date] at [Time]"
  - Content: Confirm all details (date, time, timezone, call link)
  - Include: What to prepare / what to expect on the call
  - CTA: "Add to Google Calendar" link (GHL generates this automatically)

- **Day before the call:** Reminder email
  - Subject: "Reminder: Our call is tomorrow at [Time]"
  - Content: Friendly reminder, reinforce excitement about the call
  - Include: Call link again, any prep they should do
  - CTA: "I'll see you tomorrow!" — keep energy high

- **1 hour before the call:** SMS reminder (if phone number collected)
  - Keep it short: "Hey [First Name]! Our call is in 1 hour. Looking forward to chatting! Here's the link: [link]"

- **If they NO-SHOW:** Separate automation fires
  - **Tag added:** `no-show` (GHL can trigger this if the call wasn't marked as completed)
  - **Email 1 (30 min after missed call):** "Are you okay?"
    - Subject: "Missed you on our call"
    - Content: Empathetic, not passive-aggressive. "Life happens — want to reschedule?"
    - CTA: Booking link to reschedule
  - **Email 2 (Day 1):** One more attempt
    - Subject: "Still want to connect?"
    - Content: Brief, direct
    - CTA: Reschedule link
  - After 2 no-show follow-ups with no response → mark as lost and stop sequences

## Automation Best Practices

**Conditional logic:** Use "if/then" branches in your automations
- If they booked a call → remove from VSL sequence
- If they bought → remove from all marketing sequences, add to paid member onboarding sequence
- If they didn't open 3 emails in a row → move to a re-engagement sequence or pause

**Wait times:** Be thoughtful about spacing
- Don't email 3x in the first day — you'll get marked as spam
- 1-day waits in the beginning, longer waits as the sequence progresses

**Personalization:** Always use `{{contact.first_name}}` in GHL
- "Hey Tyler," beats "Hey there," every time

**Test before going live:**
- Add yourself as a test contact and go through every automation
- Check: Do emails look right? Do links work? Does timing make sense?
- Fix issues before real leads flow through

**Monitor regularly:**
- Open rates: above 25% is decent, above 40% is great
- Click rates: above 3-5% is good
- If something's performing poorly, rewrite the subject line or CTA first
