## Video 12: YouTube Scripting

### Storytelling Structures

**A/B Plot Storytelling**
Most videos have one main plot (A plot). Adding a secondary storyline (B plot) running underneath keeps retention higher.

- Mr. Beast's "$1 vs. $500,000 Dates" = A plot (the dates) + B plot (his relationship with his fiancée, which viewers rarely see)
- For education: your B plot might be a recurring analogy you keep returning to throughout the video

**Jenga / Tension-Release Storytelling**
Tension builds → payoff → immediately start building tension again. Like Hot Ones: ask question → hot wing → reaction (release) → next question → hotter wing → bigger reaction. Never let tension stay at zero for long.

**Therefore Storytelling (South Park framework)**
Between every beat of your video, the connective tissue should be "but" or "therefore" — never "and then."

- "And then" = boring, no causation
- "But [complication], therefore [consequence]" = story with momentum

Test your outline: if you can only connect your beats with "and then," you have a list, not a story.

---

### Scripted vs. Off the Cuff

**L1 scripting:** One-word bullet points for each section
**L2 scripting:** Full sentence bullet points per section
**L3 scripting:** Word-for-word teleprompter script

**Teleprompter tip:** Practice the script once before reading. Look away naturally — at hands, off-camera — just like a real conversation. Put deliberate spaces in the script where you want to "appear to think."

**Recommendation:** Test both. Whichever you enjoy more and that performs better is right for you.

---

### Setup / Tension / Payoff (Every Section)

Every point in your video should follow this structure:

1. **Setup:** Evoke curiosity. Ask a question or tease a problem without revealing the answer.
2. **Tension:** Explain the main point through story, analogy, or illustration — without ever saying what the payoff is.
3. **Payoff:** Reveal the main point.

**Why:** If you give the payoff immediately, viewers who think they already know the answer will click off. The tension earns the payoff.

Before writing each section, identify: What is my payoff (main point)? What setup creates curiosity about it? What story/analogy builds tension without revealing it?

---

### Hook Framework

Every hook = **Target + Transformation + Stakes**

- **Target:** Who is this video for?
- **Transformation:** What will they be able to do after watching?
- **Stakes:** What happens if they don't watch?

Combine these into a 3–5 sentence hook. Dylan provides a **hook cheat sheet** (linked below the video) with 200+ formulas from top-performing videos across niches — plug-and-play.

---

### Script Clarity Test (Do Before Writing)

Before scripting, write down:
1. Who am I targeting?
2. What transformation will they achieve?
3. What are the stakes of not watching?

This prevents tangents and keeps every line serving the viewer.

## Video 13: YouTube Editing

### Software

- **Final Cut Pro** (recommended) — $300 one-time, Apple-native, fast and intuitive
- **DaVinci Resolve** — free, professional grade
- **Premiere Pro** — monthly subscription, skip unless already on Adobe Cloud
- Avoid CapCut for professional-quality output

### File Organization (Do This Before Editing)

Create a folder for each video with subfolders:
- `A Roll` — your camera footage
- `B Roll` — supplementary footage
- `Music`
- `Projects` — your editing project file
- `Exports` — finished renders

---

### Key Editing Techniques

**J and L Cuts**
Instead of hard cuts, offset audio slightly from video:
- **J cut:** Audio from next clip starts playing before the video switches
- **L cut:** Audio from current clip continues briefly after the video switches

This cues the brain subconsciously and makes edits feel seamless.

**Zoom ins / Punch ins**
At cuts, zoom in on one clip to create visual variety. Keep eye tracing consistent — if your eyes are in the upper third of frame, make sure the zoomed clip keeps eyes in the same position. Mismatched eye position is jarring.

**The rule:** Best editing is invisible. Nothing should distract the viewer from the content.

**B Roll: Show, Don't Tell**
Add supplementary footage whenever you're describing something. If you're talking about working hard, cut to footage of you at your computer. Build a B roll library over time — shoot constantly, not just for specific videos. ArtList has high-quality stock footage as a backup.

---

### Segment Color Coding + Music

Color code every natural section of your video on the timeline (intro, point 1, point 2, etc.). Assign a different piece of music to each segment.

Why: A new song re-engages viewer attention at the start of each section. Matches how our brains respond to fresh auditory stimulation.

**Cathedral echo trick:** When ending a music segment, use the Cathedral effect in Final Cut Pro + keyframe technique to make the song feel like it was composed to end exactly there — not just cut off.

---

### Color Grading

Keep it subtle. Adjust highlights, shadows, saturation, and warmth minimally. The goal is a clean, polished look — not a stylized grade.

If shooting in log format (like shooting RAW for photos), apply a conversion LUT first to restore base color, then grade from there.

## Video 14: YouTube Studio Setup

### Cameras

- **Primary:** Sony A7IV with Tamron 17–28mm f/2.8 (professional)
- **Budget:** Sony ZV-E10 4K (~$500) — nearly indistinguishable quality, great starting camera
- **iPhone:** Totally viable with good lighting — modern iPhones have excellent dynamic range

### Camera Settings (any camera)

- **Resolution:** 4K
- **Frame rate:** 24fps (25fps if in Europe)
- **Shutter speed:** 1/50
- **Aperture:** As low as your lens allows (f/2.8 ideal for background blur)
- **ISO:** Keep as low as possible (typically 800 with a softbox)

### Audio

**Shure SM7B** (XLR, ~$400) → **Rode Caster Duo** (audio interface/mixer) → computer via USB-C. The Rode Caster Duo lets you EQ and process your voice before it hits any recording software — no audio editing needed in post.

In-ear monitoring headphones (same as singers use) let you hear yourself in real time and catch audio issues immediately.

### Recording Setup: OBS + Cam Link

**Cam Link 4K** (Elgato): Takes HDMI from camera → feeds camera as a webcam into your computer.

**OBS** (free): Records everything as a single file. Switch between camera angles, screen share, and picture-in-picture with one button click. Eliminates the need to sync separate camera/audio/screen recordings in editing.

**Stream Deck** (Elgato): Physical buttons to switch OBS scenes instantly. Makes multi-camera/screen-share recording feel effortless.

### Lighting

- Place your key light at **45 degrees** from your face, slightly above eye level
- Goal: one side of face lit, other side in shadow, upside-down triangle of light on the cheek
- Use a **softbox** (diffused light) — direct LED without diffusion is unflattering
- Close blinds when filming to avoid inconsistent natural light between cuts

### Set Design

Everything in your background should be intentional and on-brand. Decide:
- Your brand colors → use in props, lights, book spines, backdrop paper
- Your brand font → print and frame it
- Your "vibe" → journalistic? tech? minimal?

As Dylan quotes from Diary of a CEO: *"The easiest way to do big things is by focusing on the small things."*

## Video 15: YouTube Analytics & Distribution

### The 3 Metrics That Actually Matter

| Metric | What It Measures | Target |
|--------|-----------------|--------|
| **Impressions** | How many people YouTube showed your video to | No universal target — benchmark against your own channel average |
| **CTR%** (Click-Through Rate) | % of impressions that clicked | **5%+** = good. Below 5% → change thumbnail first |
| **AVD** (Average Viewer Duration) | % of video watched on average | **30%+** = good. Below 30% → YouTube stops pushing |

**The plateau pattern:** If your analytics graph plateaus, your AVD dropped below 30% and YouTube pulled back distribution. You can't fix AVD on a video after it's posted — take the L and apply the lesson to the next video.

**Low impressions** = title or idea problem
**Low CTR** = thumbnail problem (90% of the time)
**Low AVD** = scripting/retention problem

---

### How to Post Analytically

1. Post with one title + one thumbnail
2. Wait 24 hours
3. Check impressions and CTR:
   - **Impressions good + CTR bad** → split test 3 new thumbnail concepts
   - **Impressions low** → split test 3 new title concepts; if impressions still low after good titles, the idea has run its course
   - **Everything good** → don't touch it

**Alternative (for channels <5K subs):** Launch with a 3-thumbnail split test from day one so you get data immediately.

---

### Split Testing

**Never split test titles and thumbnails simultaneously.** You won't know which variable caused the result.

Always fix thumbnails first (9/10 times it's the thumbnail). Then test titles.

**Thumbnail Tester** (third-party tool) — preferred over YouTube's native A/B test because:
- You control how long the test runs (recommended: 24 hours)
- You choose what to optimize for (views, CTR, watch time)
- Rotates thumbnails every hour
- Auto-picks a winner based on your chosen metric

**YTRetention.com** — Dylan's free tool. Paste in your metrics and it tells you exactly what needs to be fixed and what to do.

---

### Distribution: 10x Every Video

After posting, run this workflow to distribute to multiple platforms in under 10 minutes:

1. **Get transcript:** youtube2transcript.com (free, paste video link)
2. **Paste into Poppy AI** (or ChatGPT) with the appropriate prompt:
   - Write an optimized YouTube community post
   - Write an email newsletter
   - Write a short-form script
   - Write a LinkedIn post / Facebook post
3. **Post to each platform**

**YouTube Community Post:** Post immediately after publishing. Link back to the video. Gets views from subscribers who don't see it in their feed.

**Email list:** Every new video should go to your list. Even a 5,000-person list getting 20% open rate is 1,000 warm people seeing your video.

**Social media:** Only promote YouTube videos to your Instagram/social if your audience there aligns with your YouTube audience. If it's friends and family, don't — YouTube will initially show your video to subscribers, and low engagement from uninterested subscribers hurts distribution.

## Video 16: AI for YouTube

### Fix AI's Biggest Problem First

Default AI = yes man. Every idea is great. Every script sounds the same.

**Fix:** Go to ChatGPT → Settings → Personalization → Custom Instructions. Paste:

*"Be my ruthless mentor. If my ideas are trash, tell me why. Stress test everything until it's bulletproof. I don't want to feel good — I want to level up and get results."*

---

### Prompting Framework

**Vagueness in = garbage out. Clarity in = precision out.**

Always structure prompts as: **Role → Context → Instructions**

```
## ROLE
Please take the role of the world's best YouTube scriptwriter.

## CONTEXT
I am working on a video called [title]. I want every section to follow
setup/tension/payoff. Here is my outline: [outline]

## INSTRUCTIONS
Please write me an optimized script in my speaking style based on the
transcripts uploaded. Let me know if you have questions before writing
word-for-word. Please bullet-point the outline first for my approval.
```

Always ask AI to outline first before writing word-for-word — saves revision time.

---

### ChatGPT Projects (Train on Your Voice)

Create a **ChatGPT Project** for YouTube scripting. Train it on:
1. **Your speaking style** — paste 3 past video links into youtube2transcript.com, convert to PDF, upload
2. **Scripting framework** — upload the setup/tension/payoff framework as a PDF
3. **Story bank** — a 40-page Google Doc answering questions about your life, experiences, and analogies (takes 1–2 hours to build; this is what gives AI real examples to use instead of making things up)

---

### Poppy AI (Paid — Best for YouTube)

Dylan's most-used AI tool. Whiteboard-style workspace where you create "groups" (brains) trained on specific sources. Uses Claude, GPT, Gemini, and Grok simultaneously for better outputs.

**Best use cases:**
1. **Script writing** — trained on your voice + scripting framework + story bank + your CTA
2. **Competitor analysis** — train on your channel analytics, audience data (channels your audience watches), and competitor channels
3. **Content repurposing** — take one video transcript → output optimized LinkedIn post, email newsletter, short-form scripts, community post, all at once

Templates available below the video. Your assistant (or you) runs this workflow after each video upload.

---

### Other AI Tools

**Perplexity AI** (free tier sufficient): Deep research. Finds interviews, articles, and quotes from specific people. Use when making videos that reference external sources. Paste YouTube links from Perplexity into youtube2transcript.com to get exact timestamps without watching the whole video.

**Atlas** (ChatGPT's browser): ChatGPT integrated into Chrome. Analyze competitor channels, read YouTube analytics, get video ideas — all from within YouTube itself.

---

### AI Thumbnails

**Pexels** (~$40/month): Create a "Persona" trained on 15–20 photos of yourself. Recreate competitor thumbnails with your face, or generate from scratch with a ChatGPT-written prompt. Use "Recreate" feature to swap your face onto proven thumbnail concepts.

**Nano Banana Pro** (via ArtList, ~$25/month): ArtList also includes copyright-free music, stock footage, sound effects, and AI video generation. Nano Banana Pro produces more photorealistic thumbnails than Pexels in most cases. Workflow: give ChatGPT your reference thumbnail + photo of yourself → get a detailed prompt → paste into Nano Banana Pro.

**Higgs Field** (newest, best quality): Upload one good headshot → incredibly realistic AI thumbnails. Also generates cinematic B roll video from a single photo. Dylan's current favorite for both thumbnails and AI video B roll.

**Workflow for all thumbnail AI tools:**
1. Find a reference thumbnail from a top outlier in your niche
2. Screenshot and upload to ChatGPT with a photo of yourself
3. Ask ChatGPT to write an optimized prompt for the AI tool
4. Upload reference + your photo to the AI tool with the prompt
5. Iterate — face swap, edit, refine until it looks right

## Video 17: YouTube in 2026 — What's Changing

### Hard Mode Has Arrived

Views are down across YouTube. The easy-mode era is over. But — like the stock market — the people who understand strategy get rich in bear markets. This is your unfair advantage.

### Subscribers Don't Matter Anymore

Brands now measure engagement (average views per video), not subscriber count. A channel with 10K subscribers getting 30K views per video gets paid more than a channel with 500K subscribers getting 2K views.

Stop chasing subscribers. Optimize for views (don't focus on them — optimize for them).

### Stand Out by Being Human

The sea of sameness is AI-generated scripts that all sound identical. The fastest way to stand out:

1. **Write scripts manually** — at least the outline and core ideas
2. **Use AI for grunt work only** — the last 20%, not the first 80%
3. **Lean into your personality** — what's unique about how you talk, think, tell stories

Your unfair advantage is you. AI can't replicate that.

### Lazy Videos Are Dying

Low-effort webcam-quality videos with screen-grab thumbnails are declining fast. The data is clear.

**Higher standards don't mean expensive production.** They mean:
- Decent camera (iPhone is fine with good lighting)
- Good audio (most important)
- Clean, intentional lighting
- **Storytelling** (the real differentiator)
- **Value** (not just information — insight)

### Packaging + Ideas Are King (More Than Ever)

Most people film first, design thumbnail last. This is backwards.

**The right order:**
1. Validate the idea (outlier research)
2. Create thumbnail concepts + title options
3. Script and film the video that delivers on that packaging promise

If people don't click, it doesn't matter how good the video is. Packaging is everything.
