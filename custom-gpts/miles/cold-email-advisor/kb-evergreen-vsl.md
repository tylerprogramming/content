# Knowledge Base: Evergreen VSL Creation Guide

## Outbound Systems — Evergreen VSL Module

The **Evergreen VSL** is the Loom video you send to interested prospects after they reply positively to your cold email. Its job is to build trust and prove you can help them — *before* they get on a call with you.

It is the single biggest conversion step between "I'm interested" and "booked call."

---

## The 3 Rules of the Evergreen VSL

### Rule 1 — Keep it short

Your Evergreen VSL should be **no longer than 3 minutes.** Aim for the **2-3 minute mark.** Shorter is better.

These are cold leads. They will not spend more than a couple of minutes watching your video. Every second has to count.

### Rule 2 — Build trust, don't explain your process

The only goal of this VSL is to **prove that you can help them.** You are not trying to explain *how* you do it. You are not walking through your process. Save all of that for the sales call.

Build **enough trust and credibility to get them to book the call.** That's it.

### Rule 3 — Don't pretend it's personalized

Do not try to pass this off as a personalized Loom. People will figure it out and it will kill trust.

Instead, **call it out.** Say something like:

> *"Hey, this is a pre-recorded Loom showing you more information about XYZ."*

People respect the honesty. It will not hurt your conversions.

---

## What to Focus On

The content of your VSL should focus on **three things**:

1. **Your story** — who you are and how you got here
2. **Who you help** — your ideal client and the outcome you promise
3. **Proof** — that you've helped others achieve that outcome

This can be filmed on Loom with a simple slideshow presentation showing case studies and results from people you've worked with.

### If you don't have client proof yet

Use proof that you've done it **for yourself.** In many cases, showing that you've personally achieved the result is enough to get someone on a call.

---

## The Structure (5 Steps)

### Step 1 — Introduction (face on camera, no screen share)

Start the Loom **full screen with your face showing.** No screen share yet. Briefly introduce yourself and explain what you're going to cover in this video.

### Step 2 — Agenda set

Tell them what you're about to walk through. Keep it simple. This sets expectations and keeps them watching.

### Step 3 — Your story

Briefly talk about your background and how you got to where you are. Keep it **high level.** This builds relatability and credibility.

### Step 4 — Share your screen and show proof

Switch to **screen share** and walk through a presentation with your case studies, results, and examples of people you've helped achieve the outcome.

### Step 5 — The CTA

End with a **low-friction ask.** Present the call as a quick chat to see if what you do would even be relevant for them.

- Do **not** make it feel like a high-pressure sales call.
- Make it feel like a **casual conversation.**

---

## Quick Reference (Full Flow)

1. Introduction (face on camera)
2. Agenda set
3. Proof / case studies (screen share)
4. Your story
5. More proof
6. Low-friction CTA

---

## The 3 Things to Remember

1. **Keep it under 3 minutes.**
2. **Focus on proof, not process.**
3. **Be upfront that it's a pre-recorded video.**

That's it.

## Reviewing a Student's VSL

When a student shares their VSL, ask:

1. **How long is it?** (Over 3 minutes = too long)
2. **Do you open with your face on camera?** (Yes required)
3. **Do you explicitly say it's pre-recorded?** (Yes required — honesty builds trust)
4. **Do you show proof?** (Case studies / numbers / named clients required)
5. **What's your CTA wording?** (Must be low-friction — "quick chat to see if this is even relevant")
6. **Are you explaining your process?** (If yes — cut it. Save for the call.)

If any of those are off, be direct and point to the specific rule they're violating.

---

## The VSL Delivery Message

When you send the Evergreen VSL to a prospect after a positive reply, the message is:

> Awesome. Here's a quick video breaking down some more info.
>
> {Video link}
>
> Let me know if it's worth a quick chat.

Short. Low-friction. Matches the VSL's tone.
