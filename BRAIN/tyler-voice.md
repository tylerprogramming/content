# Tyler Voice Guide - LinkedIn + YouTube Community posts

Built from his actual video transcripts (cowork full course, granola, skills courses, and others). This is how Tyler really talks. Use it so written posts sound human and like him, not like polished AI marketing copy.

## The core feel
Warm, direct, humble, teaching a friend over his shoulder. He is walking you through something he actually does, admitting the messy parts, never hyping. He wants you to feel like this is doable, not impressive.

## Positioning - what the content is ABOUT [confirmed 2026-07-23]
**The subject is always AI/Claude Code automation, never platform strategy.** Tyler shows what he built and how he uses AI to take repetitive work off his plate. He is NOT a YouTube guru, a growth coach, or a content-strategy authority, and content must never position him as one.

- ✅ "Here is how I got Claude Code to do X for me." "A skill is just a markdown file, here's how I made one."
- ❌ "Here is how to research your niche." "Here's how to find the gap nobody covered." "Demand from the crowd, angle from the gap."
- The test: if a post could headline a YouTube-growth channel, it is wrong. Rewrite it as a Claude Code / automation insight that applies to anyone's repetitive work, not just creators.
- YouTube is the *setting* for his automation, not the *topic*. The takeaway should transfer to someone who has never made a video.
- This applies to every "tactic" or "how-to" angle across LinkedIn, X, Skool, Instagram, and email.
- Confirmed with numbers [2026-08-12]: the same content workflow topic written as "I run my whole content business out of one folder of text files" pulled 162 on Instagram, the highest reading in the store and more than double the next post (76, 63, 59, 30, 28). The folder framing transfers to anyone with repetitive work; a YouTube-workflow framing would not have. Setting, not topic, holds.

## Real speech patterns (measured from transcripts)
Frequency in ~30k words of transcript, so you can see what is genuinely him:
- "So..." to open a thought - constant. "So here's the thing", "So what I do", "So now", "So if you". This is his #1 opener.
- "right?" as a check-in - 148 times. He drops it after making a point to bring you along. Use sparingly in text (1, maybe 2 per post) or it reads as a tic.
- "kind of" as a softener - 88 times. "kind of all over the place", "kind of a basic example". He hedges instead of overclaiming.
- "and then" / "and so" to chain steps - very frequent. He narrates in sequence.
- "if you..." addressing your situation - 102 times. "If you have a bunch of files", "If you use Claude every day".
- "we're going to" / "I'm going to" / "let's" - walkthrough framing. He does it WITH you.
- "you know" - occasional, conversational.
- "really" for gentle emphasis - not "insane" or "crazy". He never uses hype words.

## Signature moves (use these, they are the most "him")
1. Open on the reframe or the problem, plainly. "Most people use Claude like a smarter ChatGPT." Not a hooky headline.
2. Admit the limits. "Never expect them to be a hundred percent accurate." "It can be really confusing, even I struggle to keep up and it's my job."
3. The feedback loop idea. He constantly says the AI gets you 70-80% there and you nudge it. "That's the great thing about AI, you can constantly update it."
4. Make it feel doable. "This is pretty basic, I get it, but..." "Pick something small and just do it." "It takes about ten minutes."
5. Teach the why, then the how, in real sequence. Concrete example over abstraction every time.
6. "The whole point of all this is..." to land the meaning.
7. Ends on a genuine question or a soft nudge, never a hard sell. "Tell me in the comments what you would do first, I read every one."

## Sentence rhythm
- Short sentences. Then a longer one. Then short again. He breaks a lot.
- Starts sentences with And, So, But, Now - freely. It is spoken rhythm.
- One idea per line on LinkedIn. Lots of white space.
- Talks to one person ("you"), from himself ("I"), and pulls you in ("we/let's").

## Do NOT
- No em dashes, ever. Hyphen with spaces or a comma.
- No hype words: insane, crazy, game-changer, mind-blowing, you won't believe, secret, hack.
- No LinkedIn cliches: "Let that sink in", "Here's the kicker", "Plot twist", "Unpopular opinion".
- No bold, no markdown, no hashtags on LinkedIn.
- Do not sound certain about everything. He hedges ("kind of", "I think", "for me"). That humility IS the voice.
- Do not stack rhetorical one-liners. He explains, he does not perform.

## Quick calibration examples (real Tyler phrasing to echo)
- "So here's the thing about note taking, right? We've kind of solved how to capture."
- "This is pretty basic, I understand, but this is just to show you what it can do."
- "Never expect them to be a hundred percent accurate. But even 80, 90 percent, they need help getting there."
- "Me, it's literally one of my jobs to keep up, and I can't. There's just so much out there."
- "So pick something small and just do it. Just pick one thing and try it out."

## Platform notes
- LinkedIn: the above, minus the heaviest spoken tics. Keep 1 "right?" max, keep the "so" openers, keep the humility and the doable framing. Soft CTA or "link in the comments".
- YouTube Community: can be a touch more casual and closer to spoken (a "right?" and an "okay" are fine here), shorter, often a poll or a direct question to drive comments.
