## Rules that are working right now

1. **The build post is the engine. Every video gets one.** [confirmed 2026-08-19]
   The 31,875-impression post (CrewAI Flows + Composio workout planner) is a complete buildable system announcement. Second tier: 1,390 (bolt.new habit tracker), plus the FastAPI/Docling/ngrok agent-frontend post (67 reactions, 6 comments, 3 reposts) and the HuggingFace smolagents course post. Every all-time winner is this shape. Nothing else has ever come close on this account.

2. **Write for the repost, not the like.** [confirmed 2026-08-19]
   31,875 impressions came with only 2 comments but **12 reposts**. Reposts are what broke that post out of the follower graph. The whole-year total is 3 reposts. A post earns a repost when it works as a standalone resource someone forwards to a teammate: they can build the thing from the post alone, even without clicking. Optimize for "would a staff engineer send this to their team channel."

3. **Name at least 5 real tools, and name them as products.** [confirmed 2026-08-19]
   The 31k post names CrewAI, Composio, Google Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slack. The runner-up names bolt.new, FastAPI, Docling, CrewAI, ngrok, Netlify. Every named tool is a discovery surface (followers of that tool, its employees, its own team resharing). The recent 40-to-200-impression posts name almost nothing. This is the single most mechanical difference between the two eras.

4. **Always ship an image.** [confirmed 2026-08-19]
   Every all-time winner carries one: the 31k post has a branded "Build AI Agents" banner over a pixel-art architecture diagram showing the actual pipeline (Google Drive to Research/Summarizer to Excel/Docx to Slack). The habit-tracker post has a Napkin-style numbered stack diagram. Recent flat posts are text-only. An architecture diagram is worth more than a talking-head thumbnail: it is the part people screenshot.

5. **Emoji step markers work on this account. Keep them.** [confirmed 2026-08-19]
   ✅ for outcome bullets, 👉 for build steps, 🎥 for the video preview header, 👇 for the closing ask. These are in the 31,875 winner. They are not dated on LinkedIn and they are not a voice violation here; they are structural scaffolding that makes a long post skimmable in the feed. Do not "clean them up."

6. **Outcome bullets, never feature bullets.** [confirmed 2026-08-19]
   The 31k post's five ✅ lines are capability statements with a real service in each: "Sends the finished workout plan directly to a Slack channel," not "Slack integration." Verb first, concrete object, real destination.

7. **Link in the body is fine. Never make clicking it the ask.** [confirmed 2026-08-19, refined from the Capitalyst decks]
   The 31,875 post has an inline `lnkd.in` video link in the body, and the smolagents post links YouTube directly, so the blanket "links kill reach" advice is false for this account. But look at what those posts actually *ask* for: the 31k post closes on "Let me know what you've created," a comment CTA. The Capitalyst decks reach the same place from the other side ("a CTA to click a link takes people off the platform and hurts your post performance"), and their own worked example closes on "comment 'Skool' below." Both keep the ask on-platform. So: put the link in the body as reference material, and always close on a **comment or DM** ask. Never "click here" as the CTA. Full reasoning in `research/linkedin/2026-08-19-capitalyst-deck-teardown.md`.

8. **Lead with continuity and standing, not with a hook line.** [confirmed 2026-08-19]
   The 31k post opens "As I've said before, CrewAI Flows are wonderful for AI Automation..." That is a practitioner picking up an ongoing thread, not a copywriter swinging for a hook. It signals this person has been in this tool for a while. Tyler's actual credentials (AI engineer at Pfizer, 8 years at IBM and JPMorgan Chase, 30k+ people taught) appear in **zero** of the last 15 posts. That standing is an unused asset.

9. **State the step count up front.** [confirmed 2026-08-19]
   "In just 7 steps, you'll go from zero to a working AI system that:" gives the reader a bounded promise before the scroll. The winner does it. The flat posts promise nothing measurable.

10. **Long is fine when it is a build.** [confirmed 2026-08-19]
    The 31k post is ~250 words with two separate lists and still won. Length is not the constraint. Density of buildable specifics is.

11. **Feed the engagement flywheel. It is the account's dead metric and the platform's main growth lever.** [deck-sourced 2026-08-19, unconfirmed on this account]
    47 comments in 365 days, and 0 comments on 13 of the last 15 posts. On LinkedIn a comment gets shared with the commenter's network, a reply from you pushes the post out further, and a like lets someone reshare in two clicks. The 31,875 post is this same flywheel running through the share path: 12 reposts, only 2 comments. The rest of the year produced 3 reposts total. Nothing is being fed in. Two plays, both free: **respond to every comment yourself, first** ("people want to speak to YOU"), and **engage daily with ~10 accounts that share your avatar but are not competitors**.

12. **Ask a question answerable in one line from the reader's own job.** [deck-sourced 2026-08-19]
    Not "what do you think?" The deck's model is ending a protein post with "What's your favorite high protein meal?" Concrete, four-word answer, from their life. The account's current closers get zero replies, so the current approach is disproven even if the replacement is not yet proven.

13. **The accomplishment hook lane is unused and the platform boosts it.** [deck-sourced 2026-08-19, unconfirmed]
    "LinkedIn actually promotes your accomplishments for you. People are literally looking for ways to measure your status." The deck allots up to 10% of a post to what you have done. Tyler's material (AI engineer at Pfizer, 8 years at IBM and JPMorgan Chase, 30k+ taught, a content business that runs out of one folder of text files) appears in zero of the last 15 posts. See rule #8.

## The insight post: the B tier

Short text posts with no artifact are the secondary format, and they cap far lower. Ranked from the last 15:

| Impressions | Opening line | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 551 | "Most people use Claude Code like a fancier chatbot." | Correction. Tells the reader they are doing it wrong. |
| 366 | "I stopped editing my own videos. Not because I got better at it. Because I stopped doing the parts I hated." | Confession plus a subverted expectation. Earned 2 of the year's 47 comments. |
| 213 | "I never learned the video editor." | Bald admission that sounds like a weakness. |
| 197 | "Most people think the hard part is connecting them. It really isn't." | Correction plus a checkable failure mode (Google auth dies every 7 days). |
| 55 | "Most people pick the wrong task to automate first." | Generic advice. No stakes, nothing checkable. |
| 53 | "I used to start every project from a blank page and a guess." | Process narration. Nothing at risk. |
| 40 | "I used to be the person moving files around for Claude." | Process narration, vaguest of the set. Worst post on the account. |

**The pattern:** insight posts win when the first line is a *confession* ("I never learned," "I stopped") or a *correction* ("Most people think X. It really isn't"). They lose when the first line is *process narration* ("I used to X, now I do Y"). "Most people..." is not itself the driver: it opens both the 551 and the 55.

**Cross-check against the Capitalyst hook framework** [2026-08-19]: the decks name three lanes, and two of them
map exactly onto the measured winners. Independent confirmation from a different source.

| Deck lane | Tyler's version | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Big Mistake or Threat | "I never learned the video editor." / "I stopped editing my own videos." | 213, 366 |
| Contrast or Controversy | "Most people use Claude Code like a fancier chatbot." | 551, 197 |
| Big Accomplishment | never used | untested, see rule #13 |

Use contrast, skip controversy. The deck recommends controversy and then warns in the next slide that it draws
"upset internet addicts." Contrast already produced the 551 without that cost.

**PAS is the fallback when an insight post will not come.** Problem, Agitation, Solution. It describes the
197-impression arcade post almost exactly: problem (connecting is not the hard part), agitation (your agent
quietly dies after seven days and never tells you), solution (arcade.dev holds the auth).

Even the best insight post (551) is 1.7% of the best build post. Use insight posts to fill days between videos, never as the main play.

## Post anatomy: the build post (the 31,875 formula)

Reproduce this order. It is the shape of the account's best post.

```
1. Continuity + authority line          "As I've said before, <TOOL> is <plain assessment>, which means <what it unlocks>."
2. The system + bounded promise         "In my latest video, I'll walk you through how to build <SPECIFIC SYSTEM> using
                                         <TOOL A> and <TOOL B>. In just N steps, you'll go from zero to a working AI system that:"
3. 4-6 ✅ outcome bullets                verb-first capability, each naming a real service
4. Video link on its own line           "Video: <url>"
5. 🎥 preview header                     "Video Preview - Here's what we'll build together:"
6. N numbered 👉 build steps             "Step 1: <action> with <named tool>"
7. Value close                          who this is for, what they walk away able to do
8. 👇 soft ask                           "Let me know what you've created!"
9. IMAGE                                branded banner + architecture diagram of the actual pipeline
```

Hard limits: no em dashes, no bold, no markdown, no hashtags (per `BRAIN/tyler-voice.md`). Note the 31k post does contain "game-changer"; that is a voice violation and it is **not** what earned the reach. Keep the format, drop the hype word.

## CTA architecture (authority first, Skool second)

- Default close: the soft build ask ("Let me know what you've created" / "Tell me what you'd point this at"). This is what the winners used.
- Skool CTA on roughly **1 in 3** posts, never every post.
- Comments are the account's weakest metric: 47 across the whole year, 0 on 13 of the last 15 posts. A vague "what do you think?" is not working. Ask a question the reader can answer from their own job in one line, with a concrete unit ("what's the one 5-step thing in your week you'd hand this?").

## Cadence [deck-sourced 2026-08-19, unconfirmed on this account]

No posting-time data exists for this account yet. Adopt these as defaults because they cost nothing, and log results.

- **3 to 5 posts per week.** 80% long-form (300+ words), 20% short.
- **Tuesday to Thursday** for maximum visibility, Monday to Friday generally.
- **Before 9am**, or **4-5pm onwards** when people log back on after work.
- "Only 1% of LinkedIn users post weekly," so consistent posting fills a real void.
