POST 1 - The confession hook

I haven't opened Canva in months, and I make more than 30 pieces of visual content a week.

For a long time I thought I needed to get good at design. I didn't. I needed to get good at describing what I wanted, and then let AI do the part I was slow at.

Here is the stack that replaced Canva for me.

Claude Code writes the actual copy for my carousels. Not headlines I have to rewrite, the whole thing, in my voice, structured slide by slide.

Nano Banana through Kie.ai makes the images and backgrounds. I describe the vibe, it hands me something clean I would never have made by hand.

Then Kie.ai resizes that one image into every format I need. Instagram, LinkedIn, Pinterest, a Reel cover. Same graphic, every platform, no manual cropping.

The thing that surprised me most was how much faster the writing got, not the design. The design was never really the bottleneck. Deciding what to say was.

If you have been telling yourself you can't make good graphics because you can't design, watch this. It is not a design problem.

POST 2 - The math angle

30 plus graphics a week. Zero design tools. Here is how that is even possible.

People assume volume means either a big team or low quality. It usually means neither. It means you removed the steps that were slowing you down.

For me there were three of them.

Writing the copy. I used to stare at a blank slide. Now Claude Code drafts the full carousel from a rough idea, and I edit instead of invent. That alone saved hours.

Making the image. I am not a designer, so this used to be where I quit. Nano Banana on Kie.ai gives me a background or a scene from a plain description. No layers, no templates.

Resizing for every platform. This is the boring one nobody talks about. One graphic has to become five sizes. Kie.ai does the reformatting so I am not nudging things around in a canvas.

None of these tools are the point on their own. The point is what happens when you chain them. A rough idea becomes finished, sized, ready-to-post visuals in the time it used to take me to pick a template.

POST 3 - The reframe

Canva was never my problem. Being slow was.

I used to blame the tool. New template, new plan, new fonts, and I was still producing a couple of decent graphics a week. The tool was fine. My process had too many manual steps.

So I stopped trying to design and started trying to describe.

Now the copy comes from Claude Code. I give it the idea, it gives me a full carousel written the way I would write it. The images come from Nano Banana on Kie.ai, from a plain sentence about what I want to see. And every resize, every platform format, gets handled by Kie.ai instead of by me dragging corners.

What changed is not that my graphics got fancier. They got faster, and I got consistent. Consistency is the thing that actually moves a channel, not one perfect post.

If you have been stuck at a few graphics a week and blaming the software, it might not be the software.
