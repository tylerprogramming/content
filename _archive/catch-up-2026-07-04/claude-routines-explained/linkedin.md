## Post 1 - The stack angle (correcting the n8n hot take)

So everyone is saying Claude Routines killed n8n. And I get why that is the take, but I think it kind of misses the actual story.

A Routine is really just Claude Code running in a cloud container on Anthropic's servers. Not on your laptop. So you can be asleep with the lid closed and it still runs. You give it a schedule, you connect a few things like Gmail, Slack, or Apify, and it does the work while you are gone.

But that is only half of it.

The other half is Claude Code on your own machine, hands-on, for the work that needs you in the loop. Building, testing, iterating in real time.

Here is the mental model that made it click for me. A skill tells Claude what steps to take. A Routine tells Claude what steps to take and when to do it, without you there. And Claude Code is when you want to sit next to it and drive.

I have built hundreds of these automations, for Fortune 500 companies and for creators with 150K plus subscribers. And the people who get the most out of it are not picking one side. They use the cloud side to run the recurring stuff and the local side to build the next thing.

So which recurring task in your week would you hand to a Routine first?

## Post 2 - The VA math (business case)

So a virtual assistant runs you 15 to 30 dollars an hour. Call it 20. Five hours a day, five days a week is 500 dollars a week, and about 26 grand a year.

A Claude Routine run costs you pennies. You are really just paying for tokens.

Now, I am not telling you to fire anyone. What I am saying is that every recurring task you do, or pay someone else to do, is kind of a candidate for a Routine.

Morning inbox triage. That is 45 minutes a day for me. Over a year that is 195 hours.

Competitor research. Two hours a week. A hundred hours a year.

Content pipeline busywork. Gone.

The move is not to do more work. It is to stop doing the work that does not really need you. You direct it, you do not do it. And every hour a Routine runs for you is an hour you get back for the stuff only you can do. Recording, talking to customers, thinking.

One thing to know though. Routines run hands-off, so you cannot course correct in the middle of a run. So you write the prompt like an SOP. Step one, step two, step three, done. You do not hope the model figures it out, you just tell it exactly.

## Post 3 - The move nobody covers (Claude Code CLI)

So most Claude Routines videos stop at the dashboard. They show you the scheduler on the website and kind of call it a day.

But there is a move almost nobody is covering, and it is the one I use most.

You can create Routines directly from the Claude Code CLI on your own machine. You do not have to click through the web interface. You describe the automation in your terminal, wire up the connectors, and schedule it, all from the same place you already build everything else.

And that matters because your local setup is where your skills, your context, and your real work already live. Being able to spin up a scheduled cloud automation from that same terminal means there is no context switch. You build it where you think, then it goes and runs in the cloud on Anthropic's servers while you move on.

This is really the part that turns Routines from a neat feature into an actual system. The cloud side runs the recurring work. The local side is where you author and refine it. Same tool, two surfaces, one workflow.

So that is the whole stack, and it is why I think pairing Routines with Claude Code is a bigger deal than any n8n comparison.
