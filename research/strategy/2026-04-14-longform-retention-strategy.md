## The First 30 Seconds (Make or Break)

### What the top performers do:

**Eliot Prince (892K)** - Opens with vulnerability
> "I'll admit something straight off the bat, even I feel overwhelmed right now and it's literally my job to stay on top of all the newest AI tools"

**Why it works:** The viewer thinks "if this expert feels overwhelmed, I'm not alone." Instant trust.

**Jeff Su (317K)** - Opens with direct qualification
> "You're in the right place if you use Cloud Chat every day, are intimidated by Cloud Code, and know Co-Work exists but not why you need it."

**Why it works:** The viewer self-selects in 5 seconds. "That's literally me." They're locked in.

**Dan Martell (498K)** - Opens with shocking data
> "ChatGPT uninstalled spiked 295% in a single day. Experts say OpenAI could run out of cash by mid-2027."

**Why it works:** Creates urgency. "Wait, what? I need to hear this."

**Matt Pocock (249K)** - Opens with a counterintuitive insight
> "At your fingertips now, you have access to a fleet of middling to good engineers. But the weird thing is they have no memory."

**Why it works:** Reframes something familiar in a new way. "I never thought of it like that."

## Your Intro Formula (Use This for Every Video)

### Template:
```

[0:05-0:15] THE PROMISE - exactly what they'll learn/gain
```

## Mid-Video Retention (The 3 Minute Rule)

### The problem:
YouTube analytics show the biggest drop-off happens at the **3 minute mark** and again at the **7 minute mark**. If you don't re-engage viewers at these points, they leave.

### What top performers do every 2-3 minutes:

**1. Open Loops (Jeff Su does this constantly)**
Before finishing a section, tease the next one:
> "Persistent memory is the most important capability we're covering today... let me explain with a silly example."

**2. Pattern Breaks (Dan Martell)**
Change the energy or format:
- Switch from talking head to screen recording
- Switch from screen to a visual diagram
- Drop a one-liner: "Now let me show you how crazy this can be."

**3. Progress Markers (Eliot Prince)**
Tell viewers where they are:
> "So that's amazing. We can organize and create files. But one of the REAL secret powers of cowork is..."

**4. Mini-Hooks (Matt Pocock)**
Treat each new section like a new video with its own hook:
> "Wouldn't it be great if there was a skill that made your code base look more like this? Well, isn't it nice..."

### Your Re-engagement Lines (use 1 every 2-3 minutes):
- "Now here's where it gets interesting."
- "But watch what happens next."
- "This is the part most people miss."
- "And here's the thing nobody talks about."
- "Now let me show you why this matters."
- "Okay, this next one is going to blow your mind."
- "But that's not even the best part."
- "Stay with me here, because this changes everything."

## Section Transitions (Never Go Flat)

### Bad transition:
> "Okay so that was CLAUDE.md. Now let's talk about context windows."

### Good transition (tease + bridge):
> "CLAUDE.md handles the instructions. But there's a problem. Claude forgets. And if you don't manage this next thing, your sessions will fall apart after 20 minutes."

### The formula:
```
[Acknowledge what we just covered] + [Create tension] + [Tease the solution]
```

## The Last 2 Minutes (CTA That Doesn't Kill Retention)

### What kills retention:
- "If you liked this video, please subscribe" (everyone leaves)
- "Check out my course at..." (sounds like an ad)
- Long outros with nothing new

### What works (from Dan Martell, 498K):
> "So here's my challenge to you. Use Claude as your main AI for one week, not as a backup, not as a test. Your primary tool. Give it your hardest problems. Then come back and tell me what changed."

### Your CTA template:
```
[Challenge the viewer to try ONE thing from the video]
[Ask a specific question for comments]
[Quick Skool mention - "the full system with all 25 skills is in my community"]
[Next video tease - "if you want to see how I built [specific skill], that's this video"]
```

### Example:
> "Here's what I want you to do right now. Pick ONE task you do manually every week. Try building a Claude Code skill for it. Just a markdown file with your process written down.
>
> Drop a comment telling me what you'd automate first.
>
> And if you want the full system with all 25 skills, the Skool community link is in the description.
>
> If you want to see how I built the skill that plans my entire YouTube videos, watch this next."

## Confidence Tips for Filming

You mentioned sometimes feeling like you won't do well enough. Here's what the data says:

1. **Your content outperforms your self-doubt.** "Built a Skill in 5 Minutes" got 3,494 views and 107 likes. "Create Carousels with AI" got 114 likes (your highest). People love what you make.

2. **Nobody watches for perfection.** They watch for real results. Dan Martell stumbles through his demos. Eliot Prince says "I'm overwhelmed too." Authenticity > polish.

3. **You have something nobody else has.** 25 real skills running a real business. Not a demo. Not a tutorial about a fake project. Your actual workflow.

4. **Script your first 30 seconds word-for-word.** After that, you'll be in the flow. The intro is where nerves hit hardest. Nail it on paper, then the rest flows.

5. **Film the screen recording parts first.** They're easier. Once you see the output looking great, your talking head confidence goes up because you know the content delivers.
