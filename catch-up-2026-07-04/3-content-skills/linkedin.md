# LinkedIn - 3 Content Skills

Three distinct posts. No hashtags, no markdown, no bold. Soft CTA to the video.

---

## Post 1 - The "hundreds but only 7" angle

So I have hundreds of Claude Code skills sitting on my machine. And I use about 7 of them every week.

That number kind of surprises people.

They think the goal is to build this huge library. It really isn't. Most of my skills were experiments I built once and never touched again. The value was never in the count. It was in finding the handful that actually run my week.

And three of those seven kick off every Monday.

The first one researches what is working in my space in about 2 minutes, instead of me clicking around YouTube for an hour.

The second pulls a clean transcript from any video, so I can read a competitor in 5 minutes instead of watching for 30.

The third takes that transcript and builds a full video package for me. Titles, hooks, script, description, filming guide. About 5 minutes.

Research, input, output. A whole pipeline before I have finished my coffee.

Here is the thing that took me a while to learn. You do not need more skills. You need the right three, used every single week, until they are boring. And boring is kind of the goal. Boring means it is a system.

So I broke down all three in my latest video, then built a brand new one live on camera. Link is in the comments if you want to watch how it works.

## Post 2 - The "it is just a markdown file" angle

So most people think a Claude Code skill is some complicated piece of engineering.

It is a text file.

That is the part nobody really tells you. No SDK. No framework. No plugin to install. A skill is a markdown file with a few lines of instructions on top and plain English below telling Claude what to do. That is the whole thing.

I run my entire content business on three of them. Research, transcripts, and full video packages. Each one is maybe 50 to 100 lines of English.

So in my latest video I did something I had been meaning to do for a while. Instead of just showing you the polished ones, I built a brand new skill from scratch, live, no edits. It is a personal people tracker called peeps. It remembers details about the people in your life, names, birthdays, how you know them, notes.

Start to finish it took a few minutes. You type a description of what you want it to do. Save the file. Test it. Done.

And the moment that clicks for you, everything kind of changes. You stop asking how do I automate this, and you start asking what do I call the skill and what should it say.

So if you have been curious what a skill actually is, this one shows you and teaches you to build your own. Link in the comments.

## Post 3 - The "leverage when you have no time" angle

So I am a full-time software engineer with a wife and kids. My content gets made between 4 and 5:30 PM on weekdays.

That is the time budget. That is all of it.

And when you have that little time, you cannot out-work anyone. You have to out-leverage them. The way I get leverage out of Claude Code is skills. Small reusable prompts that do a real job every time I run them, without me rewriting the instructions.

Three skills carry the whole operation.

One does my research. One turns any video into a readable transcript. One builds a full video package from that transcript. Together they take my Monday planning from most of a weekend down to about 90 minutes.

The trap I see people fall into is thinking they need to master some big automation platform first. You really don't. You need one small skill that saves you 20 minutes, and the confidence that came from building it. Then another. Then another.

So pick something small and just do it.

I walk through all three in my new video, then build a fourth one from scratch so you can see how simple it actually is. If time is your constraint too, this is the one to watch. Link in the comments.
